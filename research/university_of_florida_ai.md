## Comprehensive Reference Document

## 1. Executive Summary

The University of Florida (UF) has become the first major U.S. public university to integrate artificial intelligence education across its entire curriculum. Launched in 2020, the initiative combines a $70 million NVIDIA partnership, one of the fastest academic supercomputers in the world (HiPerGator), 300+ AI-focused faculty, 230+ AI-integrated courses across all 16 colleges, and a $150 million physical hub (Malachowsky Hall). The effort predates ChatGPT by two full years and is now linked to UF's formal accreditation through its Quality Enhancement Plan (QEP).

## 3. The $70 Million NVIDIA Partnership

### Funding Structure
- **$25 million** personal donation from Chris Malachowsky (UF EE '80, NVIDIA co-founder)
- **$25 million** in-kind from NVIDIA (hardware, software, training, and services)
- **$20 million** from the University of Florida
- **Total: $70 million**

### What Was Funded
1. **HiPerGator AI supercomputer** -- NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD architecture
2. **NVIDIA AI Technology Center (NVAITC)** at UF -- embedded NVIDIA engineers supporting research
3. **NVIDIA Deep Learning Institute (DLI)** training for faculty and staff
4. **Curriculum development** across all colleges
5. **Data center infrastructure** upgrades

### HiPerGator AI Supercomputer

**3rd Generation (2021):**
- 140 NVIDIA DGX A100 systems
- 1,120 NVIDIA A100 Tensor Core GPUs
- NVIDIA Mellanox HDR 200Gb/s InfiniBand networking
- 700 petaflops of AI performance
- Ranked as fastest AI supercomputer in U.S. higher education

**4th Generation (2025):**
- NVIDIA DGX B200 systems (Blackwell architecture)
- 504 Blackwell GPUs
- NVIDIA Quantum-2 InfiniBand networking
- 30x faster than previous generation
- 20,000 additional AMD CPU cores

**Usage:**
- Up to 8,000 UF students use HiPerGator in their research each semester
- Made available to Florida's 11 other public universities and industry partners
- GatorTron medical AI model trained in just 7 days on HiPerGator

## 4. AI-Integrated Courses: 230+ Across All 16 Colleges

### Scale
- **230+ courses** with AI content across all 16 colleges
- **12,000 students** annually enrolled in AI courses
- **7,500 students** took an AI course in a single semester (Spring 2023)
- Enrollment projected to double year over year

### AI Course Designation System

UF created a five-level taxonomy to classify every AI course:

| Designation | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Use-AI** | Students apply AI tools in their coursework |
| **Know-AI** | Students develop foundational understanding of AI concepts |
| **Build-AI** | Students create or develop AI systems |
| **Ethical-AI** | Students examine responsible AI implementation, bias, and policy |
| **Enable-AI** | Students develop supporting technical infrastructure for AI |

These designations align with AI literacy skills and help students identify the level of AI engagement expected in a course, ranging from novice to expert.

### AI Certificate Program

The **AI Fundamentals and Applications Certificate** is available to every UF undergraduate regardless of major. It requires three courses:
1. **AI Fundamentals** (common to all students)
2. **AI Ethics** (common to all students)
3. **College-specific AI application course** (varies by discipline)

No background in computer programming, engineering, or data science is required.

### Degree Programs
- AI-focused **majors and minors** available
- **Master of Science in AI Systems (MSAIS)** in the College of Engineering
- **Micro-credentials** and **stackable professional certificates** in business, medicine, and life sciences
- **Graduate certificates** across multiple colleges

## 5. Faculty Training Model

### NVIDIA Deep Learning Institute (DLI) Ambassador Program
- UF is a hub for NVIDIA's DLI Ambassador Program, a free training opportunity for university faculty on GPU-accelerated computing
- Faculty receive hands-on training in deep learning, computer vision, natural language processing, and other AI techniques
- Trained faculty then integrate these skills into their own discipline-specific courses

### Institutional Support
- UF Information Technology (UFIT) hires dedicated training and support staff
- Extensive AI training materials, sessions, and workshops developed in-house
- Leverages NVIDIA DLI materials adapted for non-CS disciplines
- AI Days and AI Scholars programs provide ongoing faculty development
- The AI2 Center coordinates workshops, seminars, and cross-disciplinary collaboration

### Faculty Hiring
- **106 new AI faculty** hired during 2021-2022 initiative across all colleges
- **300+ total AI-focused faculty** now at UF (new hires supplementing 200+ existing AI experts)
- **30+ AI faculty** hired specifically in UF Health colleges (Dentistry, Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy, Public Health, Veterinary Medicine)

## 6. Student Requirements and AI Competency

### Philosophy
UF defines **AI literacy** as "the knowledge and understanding of AI necessary for individuals to participate in broader discourse around AI and make informed decisions about its use and implications." The initiative ranges from novice-level awareness to expert-level system building.

### Structure
- The AI Fundamentals and Applications Certificate is **available to all undergraduates** regardless of major
- Each of UF's 16 colleges offers at least three AI and data science courses
- AI competency is embedded into the **Quality Enhancement Plan (QEP)**, which is part of the university's SACSCOC accreditation -- making AI integration a formal institutional commitment
- **I-CURE courses** (Interdisciplinary Course-Based Undergraduate Research Experiences) feature AI components with teams from multiple disciplines working on industry-presented challenges

### NaviGator AI
Launched in 2024, NaviGator AI is a university service providing students and faculty access to 30+ large language models, making AI tools available across all disciplines without requiring individual subscriptions.

### Career Connections
- **268+ AI-related internships** facilitated through the Career Connections Center
- Faculty emphasize that students gaining AI competencies will possess "a distinct advantage" in the workforce

## 8. The AI2 Summit

### Overview
The AI2 (AI Academic Initiative) Summit is UF's flagship conference for sharing its AI-in-education model with other institutions.

### 2025 Summit (February, Orlando)
- **450 participants** from 40+ states and 6 countries
- Over half were university leaders (provosts, deans, department chairs)
- Expert-led, hands-on workshops on AI classroom applications
- Keynote addresses and panel discussions with leaders from **IBM**, **Mark III Systems**, and **NVIDIA**
- Focus areas: AI fundamentals, ethics, applications in academia, utilization in university operations, technical innovations

### Key Outcome
Many attendees reported they "didn't know where to start" with institutional AI strategies. The summit provided a replicable framework including governance structures, course designation systems, and infrastructure planning. UF positioned itself as a consultancy for other universities attempting similar transformations.

### 2026 Summit (March-April, Orlando)
- Expanded programming continuing the same mission
- Additional focus on AI in university operations and administration

## 11. Lessons Learned and Replicable Framework

### What Other Universities Can Copy

**1. Start with Infrastructure, Not Curriculum**
UF's approach began with securing computing power (HiPerGator) before redesigning courses. Universities that may lack experience acquiring HPC systems can start with smaller, mid-range, or cloud-based systems and partner with industry and government to overcome this barrier.

**2. Use a Course Designation System**
The five-level taxonomy (Use-AI, Know-AI, Build-AI, Ethical-AI, Enable-AI) is discipline-agnostic and can be adopted by any institution. It helps students and faculty understand the level of AI engagement in each course.

**3. Create a Centralized Coordinating Body**
The AI2 Center serves as the organizational hub, avoiding duplication and ensuring coherence across 16 colleges. A single center with a dedicated associate provost provides the authority and coordination needed.

**4. Embed in Accreditation**
By making AI Across the Curriculum their QEP for SACSCOC accreditation, UF ensured the initiative would survive leadership changes and budget cycles. This is a powerful strategy for institutional permanence.

**5. Require Ethics from Day One**
Every AI certificate pathway includes an ethics course. This is not an afterthought but a structural requirement.

**6. Hire Discipline-Specific AI Faculty**
Rather than concentrating AI expertise in computer science, UF hired AI faculty across all colleges -- agriculture, medicine, arts, business -- so that AI integration is authentic to each discipline.

**7. Share Resources Externally**
UF made HiPerGator available to Florida's 11 other public universities and K-12 partners, building a network effect and regional AI ecosystem.

**8. Partner with Industry Early**
The NVIDIA partnership provided not just money but embedded engineers (NVAITC), training programs (DLI), and software -- a model of public-private collaboration that goes beyond a donation.

**9. Faculty Development Is Non-Negotiable**
Without the DLI Ambassador Program and internal UFIT training, faculty could not have created 230+ courses. Training must precede course development.

**10. Design the Certificate to Be Accessible**
Requiring no programming prerequisites for the AI certificate ensures that humanities, education, and social science students can participate.

## 12. Criticism and Challenges

### Florida's "Countries of Concern" Law (SB 846 / HB 1)
The most significant external challenge to UF's AI ambitions came from a 2023 Florida law signed by Governor Ron DeSantis that restricts interactions with seven "countries of concern" (China, Iran, Russia, Cuba, Venezuela, Syria, North Korea):

- **Impact on recruitment:** UF professors previously recruited 1,000+ graduate students per fall from these countries. After the law, recruitment dropped to near zero from affected countries.
- **Faculty pushback:** 280+ UF faculty members signed a petition urging clarification of the law's scope.
- **Research consequences:** New AI researchers reported the law could "cripple" their ability to staff labs and generate preliminary results for federal grants.
- **Legal challenge:** Two Chinese graduate students at FIU and a UF faculty member sued; a federal magistrate judge recommended a preliminary injunction to suspend enforcement.
- **Media coverage:** Fortune headline: "Nvidia billionaire co-founder created a $70 million supercomputer at University of Florida. Then Ron DeSantis banned top AI experts from China."

### Curriculum and Pedagogical Challenges
- **Faculty readiness:** Not all faculty in non-technical disciplines felt prepared to teach AI, even with DLI training. Ongoing support remains necessary.
- **Curriculum lag:** AI evolves faster than four-year degree programs. Officials expressed concern that students' job outlooks may change significantly during their time at UF.
- **AI hallucination and accuracy:** Provost Glover publicly acknowledged the problem of AI "hallucinating" -- providing incorrect information in an authoritative tone -- as a pedagogical and research concern.

### Ethical Concerns
- **Bias amplification:** UF researchers documented cases where "large pools of biased data can amplify bias on a giant scale," including an example of AI producing biased recidivism predictions used in parole hearings.
- **Privacy and transparency:** The university formed a Working Group in AI Ethics and Policy to address privacy, transparency, bias, and academic integrity.
- **Academic integrity:** Generative AI raises ongoing questions about plagiarism, authentic assessment, and student learning.

### Institutional Challenges
- **Leadership turnover:** President Ben Sasse's departure for health reasons, and Provost Joe Glover's move to the University of Arizona, removed two key champions from the initiative. The QEP accreditation embedding helps insulate against this.
- **Sustainability of funding:** The initial $70M was transformative, but ongoing infrastructure costs (HiPerGator 4 upgrade alone is $24M) require continued state and private support.
- **Equity concerns:** Smaller Florida universities sharing HiPerGator access may not have the faculty or curricular support to fully benefit from the resource.

## 15. Summary for Book Authors

The University of Florida's AI initiative is significant for several reasons:

1. **It predates ChatGPT.** The initiative launched in 2020, two years before generative AI became mainstream. This demonstrates institutional foresight rather than reactive policy.

2. **It is truly university-wide.** Unlike most institutions where AI lives in CS departments, UF integrated AI into agriculture, medicine, humanities, journalism, design, and every other college.

3. **It combines all four pillars.** Infrastructure (HiPerGator), faculty (300+ AI experts), curriculum (230+ courses), and physical space (Malachowsky Hall) -- all were addressed simultaneously.

4. **It is accreditation-linked.** By embedding AI in the QEP, UF made the initiative survive leadership changes and budget cycles.

5. **It produced world-class research.** GatorTron/GatorTronGPT are among the most downloaded clinical AI models globally.

6. **It is replicable.** UF deliberately published its framework and hosts the AI2 Summit so other universities can follow.

7. **It faces real tensions.** The DeSantis-era immigration restrictions created a direct conflict between Florida's AI ambitions and its talent pipeline, illustrating how political context shapes institutional AI strategy.

For a book about AI in scientific research, UF serves as the most complete case study of a major research university reorganizing itself around AI -- with both the successes and the contradictions that entails.
