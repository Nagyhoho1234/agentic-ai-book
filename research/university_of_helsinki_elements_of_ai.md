# Elements of AI: The University of Helsinki's Groundbreaking AI Literacy Program

> **Research compiled**: March 2026
> **Purpose**: Reference material for a book on AI in scientific research

---

## 1. History and Origins

### The Problem
In 2017, Finland published its national AI strategy, *Finland's Age of Artificial Intelligence* (Tekoalyaika), recognizing that AI would transform information-intensive industries such as healthcare, education, and government. A key challenge was identified: the general public lacked basic understanding of AI, creating a gap between technological advancement and societal readiness.

### The Partnership
In spring 2018, the **University of Helsinki** (Finland's oldest and largest university, established 1640) partnered with **Reaktor**, a Finnish technology consultancy, to create a free online course that would demystify AI for non-experts. The goal was ambitious: to educate 1% of Finland's population (approximately 54,000 people) in the fundamentals of artificial intelligence.

### Key People
- **Teemu Roos**, Professor of Computer Science (AI and Data Science) at the University of Helsinki, served as lead instructor and a driving force behind the course's design and delivery.
- **Ville Valtonen**, CEO of MinnaLearn, was instrumental in the course concept and platform development.
- The course emerged from the broader ecosystem of the **Finnish Center for Artificial Intelligence (FCAI)**, a national competence center initiated by Aalto University, the University of Helsinki, and VTT Technical Research Centre of Finland.

### Launch
Elements of AI launched on **May 14, 2018**. Within four months, nearly 90,000 people from 80 countries had enrolled, making it Finland's most popular online course ever. The 1% target for Finland's population was exceeded rapidly.

### Organizational Evolution
The education arm of Reaktor was later rebranded as **MinnaLearn**, which continues to co-manage the course alongside the University of Helsinki. MinnaLearn now focuses on AI literacy training for organizations and workplaces.

## 2. Course Content and Structure

### Part 1: Introduction to AI

A free, beginner-friendly course requiring **no programming or advanced mathematics**. It takes approximately **30 hours** to complete and covers six chapters:

1. **What is AI?** -- Defining AI, related fields, and the philosophy of AI
2. **AI Problem Solving** -- Search algorithms, solving problems with AI, search and games
3. **Real World AI** -- Odds and probability, the Bayes rule, naive Bayes classification
4. **Machine Learning** -- Types of machine learning, nearest neighbor classifier, regression
5. **Neural Networks** -- Neural network basics, how they are built, advanced techniques
6. **Impact** -- Predicting the future, societal implications of AI

### Part 2: Building AI

Released in **late 2020**, this sequel course introduces the actual algorithms behind AI methods. Basic Python programming skills are recommended. It covers five chapters:

1. **Getting Started with AI** -- Why AI matters, optimization, hill climbing
2. **Dealing with Uncertainty** -- Probability fundamentals, Bayes rule, naive Bayes classifier
3. **Machine Learning** -- Linear regression, nearest neighbor, working with text, overfitting
4. **Neural Networks** -- Logistic regression, from logistic regression to neural networks, deep learning
5. **Conclusion**

### Exercise Design
Each exercise offers **three difficulty levels** (beginner, intermediate, advanced). Completing any one level counts as finishing the exercise, accommodating learners of different backgrounds and ambitions.

## 3. Scale and Demographics

### Enrollment Milestones
- **May 2018**: Launch
- **September 2018**: ~90,000 enrolled from 80 countries (within 4 months)
- **May 2023**: 1 million students from 110+ countries
- **2025-2026**: Over **2 million students** enrolled, graduates from **170+ countries**

### Demographics
- **~40% of participants are women** -- more than double the average for computer science courses
- Among **Nordic participants**, the share of women approaches **~60%**
- **Over 25% of participants are aged 45 and over**
- Participants span diverse backgrounds, professions, and levels of technical experience
- The course is the most popular in the 385-year history of the University of Helsinki

### Organizational Adoption
- In Finland alone, over **250 companies** have used the course to train employees
- By autumn 2019, more than **450 Finnish and Swedish organizations** had joined the #AIChallenge initiative, encouraging employees to complete Elements of AI

## 4. The EU Expansion

### Finland's 2019 EU Council Presidency
During its EU Council Presidency in the second half of 2019, Finland chose AI literacy as a flagship initiative. The Finnish government decided to offer the Elements of AI course to all EU Member States **free of charge**.

### The 1% Goal
The stated objective was to train **1% of EU citizens** (approximately 4.5 million people) in the basics of AI, strengthening digital leadership across the European Union.

### Language Expansion
The course was translated into **all official EU languages** (22+ languages) and localized in **30 countries** through partnerships with local universities and businesses:

- Countries that already had the course: **Estonia, Sweden, Germany**
- First EU expansion launch: **Latvia** (May 2020)
- Last EU expansion launches: **Spain and Slovenia** (April 2021)
- Additional countries included: Netherlands, Belgium, Bulgaria, Ireland, Italy, Austria, Greece, Croatia, Cyprus, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Poland, Portugal, France, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Denmark, Czech Republic

### Total Language Availability
The course is now available in **26 languages**, including English, Finnish, Swedish, German, Italian, French, Spanish, and many others.

## 5. Pedagogical Approach

### Design Principles

- **No prerequisites**: No programming, mathematics, or prior technical knowledge required for Introduction to AI
- **Self-paced**: Learners complete the course on their own schedule
- **Interactive exercises**: Theory is combined with practical exercises at multiple difficulty levels
- **Accessible language**: Complex AI concepts are explained in plain, non-technical language
- **Real-world examples**: Concepts are grounded in everyday applications and scenarios
- **Approximately 30 hours**: Manageable time commitment for working professionals

### What Makes It Work
The pedagogical design follows principles of constructive learning, where learners actively engage with material through exercises rather than passively consuming lectures. The multi-level exercise system ensures that both complete beginners and those with some technical background find the course challenging but not overwhelming.

### Format
- Self-study resources and reading materials
- Interactive content and visualizations
- Practical exercises with tiered difficulty
- Community platform for peer engagement
- Certificate of completion available

## 6. Impact on Finnish Society and AI Literacy

### National Impact
- Finland became the **first country in the world** to offer AI education to its general public at national scale
- The 1% population target was exceeded, with over 54,000 Finns completing the course
- The University of Helsinki saw **increased applications to ICT programs** and a **higher proportion of female applicants** after the course launch
- The course became a cornerstone of Finland's broader *Artificial Intelligence Programme* (AI Finland)

### Finland's National AI Strategy Goals
Finland's AI strategy, published in October 2017, aimed to:
- Increase competitiveness of business and industry
- Provide high-quality public services and improve public sector efficiency
- Ensure a well-functioning society and citizen wellbeing
- Guarantee AI literacy across the entire Finnish population, including the elderly

### Broader Cultural Shift
Elements of AI contributed to a broader cultural normalization of AI in Finland. The societal implications of AI -- including algorithmic bias, data privacy, and de-anonymization -- are explicitly addressed in the course, helping create a more informed citizenry capable of participating in public debates about AI regulation and policy.

## 7. Follow-Up Courses and Programs

The success of Elements of AI spawned an expanding ecosystem of AI education at the University of Helsinki:

### Ethics of AI
- Free online MOOC addressing the ethical dimensions of AI
- Covers what AI ethics means, what can and cannot be done to develop AI ethically, and how to think about AI from an ethical standpoint
- Available at: [ethics-of-ai.mooc.fi](https://ethics-of-ai.mooc.fi/)

### Accessible AI
- Introduces AI and its use in empowering people with disabilities
- Focuses on communication, employment, daily life, diversity, and inclusiveness

### Practical AI
- Provides basic AI information in a clear, practical format
- Includes sections on defining AI, generative AI, language models, image models, and ethics
- Worth 2 ECTS credits

### The AI Collection
The University of Helsinki offers a curated set of AI courses that provide various perspectives. Courses relate to each other but can be studied in any order, and most require no previous knowledge.

### MinnaLearn Enterprise Offerings
- **AI Literacy for Organizations**: 60-minute modules for staff training
- **Elements of AI for LMS**: Available in SCORM and xAPI formats for integration into corporate learning management systems
- **Workplace AI adoption programs**: Practical training for turning AI curiosity into safe everyday use

## 9. Success Factors

### Why Elements of AI Became a Global Phenomenon

1. **Free and open access**: No cost barrier, removing the single biggest obstacle to mass education
2. **No prerequisites**: Designed for absolute beginners, unlike most AI courses that assume programming knowledge
3. **High-quality content**: Ranked **#1 among 1,167 online courses** by Class Central in 2019 as the best computer science course in the world
4. **Government backing**: Finnish national AI strategy provided institutional support and legitimacy
5. **Corporate partnership**: Reaktor/MinnaLearn brought technology expertise and platform design
6. **Inclusive design**: Multi-level exercises, plain language, self-pacing -- all reduce barriers to completion
7. **Multilingual availability**: 26 languages made it accessible to non-English speakers across Europe and beyond
8. **Cultural timing**: Launched when public anxiety about AI was growing, offering empowerment over fear
9. **Strong demographics**: 40% women and 25% aged 45+ demonstrated genuine inclusivity, not just rhetoric
10. **EU institutional support**: Finland's EU Presidency gave the initiative continental scale and political visibility

### Awards and Recognition
- **MIT Inclusive Innovation Challenge (2019)**: Won $250,000 grand prize in the "Skills Development and Opportunity Matching" category, alongside JobGet, Agros, and TiendaPago
- **Class Central (2019)**: Named #1 computer science online course globally out of 1,167 courses
- Most popular course in the 385-year history of the University of Helsinki

## 11. Ethics and AI Literacy Components

### Within Elements of AI
The final chapter of Introduction to AI ("Impact") explicitly addresses:
- Societal implications of AI
- Algorithmic bias and fairness
- Data privacy and de-anonymization risks
- The need for policies and regulations
- Predicting AI's future impact on society and work

### Dedicated Ethics of AI Course
The University of Helsinki's standalone Ethics of AI MOOC provides deeper coverage:
- What AI ethics means in practice
- Ethical sustainability in AI development
- Frameworks for thinking about AI from an ethical perspective
- Available free at [ethics-of-ai.mooc.fi](https://ethics-of-ai.mooc.fi/)

### Philosophical Grounding
The Introduction to AI course includes philosophical dimensions from the first chapter, asking fundamental questions about what intelligence is, what constitutes AI, and how we should think about machine cognition -- unusual for a technical course and important for developing genuine AI literacy rather than mere technical skill.

## 12. Lessons for Other Universities

### What Elements of AI Demonstrates

1. **Public universities can lead mass education**: A traditional research university successfully educated millions, proving that MOOCs are not solely the domain of private platforms like Coursera or edX.

2. **Industry-academic partnerships work**: The Reaktor/MinnaLearn partnership brought design thinking, platform expertise, and corporate networks that a university alone could not have achieved.

3. **Government alignment amplifies impact**: Embedding the course in Finland's national AI strategy and EU Presidency gave it political momentum and institutional legitimacy far beyond what a standalone academic initiative could achieve.

4. **Inclusivity must be designed in, not added on**: The 40% female participation rate was not accidental -- it resulted from deliberate choices in language, examples, exercise design, and marketing that avoided the exclusionary patterns common in computer science education.

5. **Lowering barriers beats raising standards**: By removing prerequisites (no programming, no math, no cost), the course reached demographics that traditional AI education never touches -- older adults, non-technical professionals, and people in non-STEM fields.

6. **Multilingual localization is essential for global reach**: Translation into 26 languages with local university partnerships was critical for penetrating non-English-speaking markets.

7. **AI literacy is different from AI training**: The course does not aim to produce AI engineers. It aims to produce informed citizens who can participate in democratic decisions about AI -- a fundamentally different and arguably more important goal.

8. **Scale creates network effects**: As enrollment grew, corporate adoption followed (250+ Finnish companies), which drove further enrollment -- a virtuous cycle.

9. **Ethics must be integrated, not siloed**: Including societal impact and ethical considerations within the main course (not as a separate elective) ensures all learners encounter these topics.

10. **Free does not mean low quality**: The #1 Class Central ranking proved that a free course can compete with and surpass paid alternatives.
