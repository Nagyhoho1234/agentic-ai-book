## 1. Partnership Overview: Anthropic's First "University Design Partner"

On April 2, 2025, Northeastern University and Anthropic announced a strategic collaboration making Northeastern the **first "university design partner"** for Anthropic. The partnership aims to transform teaching, research, and business operations through responsible AI implementation.

Key elements of the partnership:

- **Co-designing responsible use frameworks** for AI in higher education
- **Creating new AI-powered education tools** tailored to academic needs
- **Advancing research** to ensure AI empowers rather than replaces human learning
- **Developing best practices** for AI integration that other institutions can adopt
- Northeastern's Institute for Experiential AI (EAI) serves as a key partner, focusing on research evaluation, curriculum co-development, and responsible AI integration

Northeastern was notably the first U.S. university to develop an academic plan (Northeastern 2025) centered on AI's implications for learning, providing institutional groundwork for this partnership.

## 3. Claude for Education Features

### Learning Mode

The centerpiece feature is **Learning Mode**, available within Claude's Projects functionality. Unlike standard AI chatbots that provide direct answers, Learning Mode uses pedagogical techniques grounded in cognitive science:

- **Socratic questioning:** When a student asks "How do I solve this calculus problem?", Claude responds with "What do you think happens to the function as x approaches this value?"
- **Guided problem-solving:** Asks "How would you approach this?" instead of giving solutions
- **Evidence-based reasoning:** When writing a paper, prompts like "What evidence supports your conclusion?"
- **Conceptual emphasis:** Highlights fundamental principles underlying specific problems
- **Structured templates:** Provides formats for research papers, study guides, and outlines

### Cognitive Science Backing

Research from cognitive science shows that information discovered through guided questioning is **retained 3-4 times longer** than information received passively. Learning Mode users develop genuine competence because they practice the thinking process rather than memorizing solutions.

### Faculty Controls

Faculty can configure Learning Mode through Projects, setting custom system prompts that define how Claude should interact with students in specific course contexts. This gives instructors control over the pedagogical approach while leveraging AI capabilities.

## 4. Faculty Integration into Curricula

### Anthropic-Northeastern Education Report

Anthropic analyzed **~74,000 anonymized conversations** from higher education professionals (May-June 2025) and conducted qualitative research with **22 Northeastern University faculty members** who were early adopters of AI. Key findings:

#### Top Three Uses by Educators

| Use Category | % of Conversations |
|---|---|
| Curriculum development | 57% |
| Academic research | 13% |
| Student performance assessment | 7% |

#### Curriculum Development (57%)
- Designing educational games and escape rooms
- Creating interactive tools and simulations
- Building multiple-choice assessment questions
- Generating outcome-aligned rubrics
- Creating assignment variations at different difficulty levels

#### Academic Research (13%)
- Supporting bibliometric analysis and database operations
- Implementing and interpreting statistical models
- Revising academic papers in response to reviewer feedback

#### Student Performance Assessment (7%)
- Providing detailed feedback on student assignments
- Evaluating work against assessment criteria
- Summarizing student evaluation reports

**Notable finding:** Despite 48.9% of grading-related conversations showing automation patterns, faculty rated AI-grading as the **least effective** application, revealing a significant gap between attempted use and educator comfort levels.

#### Augmentation vs. Automation Patterns

**High augmentation (collaborative) uses:**
- University teaching/classroom instruction: 77.4% augmentation
- Grant proposal writing: 70.0%
- Academic advising: 67.5%
- Supervising student work: 66.9%

**High automation uses:**
- Financial management: 65.0% automation
- Student records/performance evaluation: 48.9%
- Admissions/enrollment: 44.7%

#### Faculty Quotes

> "What was prohibitively expensive (time) to do [before] now becomes possible." -- On custom simulations and interactive experiments

> "Use it as a thought partner, not a thought substitute." -- On brainstorming grant proposals

> "It's my moral obligation to do a good job (with the assistance, perhaps, of LLMs)." -- On student assessment

#### Time Savings

A Gallup survey found teachers using AI tools save **5.9 hours per week** on average.

## 7. Data Privacy and Security Arrangements

### Enterprise Security

- Anthropic's security controls were **comprehensively reviewed** by Northeastern's Office of Information Security and determined to meet university security standards
- Access is through Northeastern's **enterprise agreement** with strong privacy protections
- Authentication required via university SSO credentials

### Data Handling Commitments

- **No model training on user data:** Anthropic will not train its models using any user inputs
- **Data isolation:** Information is not used to train AI models or shared across accounts
- Data stored within university-approved systems only
- The university reserves the right to access submitted data per its Policy on Appropriate Use of Computers and Networks
- The university does **not** actively monitor Claude usage

### FERPA Considerations

An important caveat: the product is **not fully FERPA compliant** in all use cases. If an individual uses Claude for specific education-related tasks involving protected student records (e.g., analyzing application essays with identifiable student data), this could violate FERPA. The university has issued specific guidance on what data categories must never be entered into AI tools.

### Prohibited Data Categories

The following must never be pasted into AI tools, including Claude:
- Social Security numbers, dates of birth, addresses, NUIDs
- Student records: grades, financial aid details, disciplinary actions
- Protected health information (HIPAA)
- Unpublished research findings, proprietary datasets
- Financial information: bank details, payment records
- Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI)
- Passwords and API keys

### AI Review Committee Requirement

Users must complete **AI Review Committee approval** before uploading confidential, personal, or restricted research data to Claude.

## 8. Impact on Teaching and Learning Outcomes

### Early Evidence

- Faculty report that previously time-prohibitive educational tools (custom simulations, interactive experiments) are now feasible to create
- Teachers using AI tools save an average of **5.9 hours per week** (Gallup)
- Learning Mode's Socratic approach produces **3-4x better retention** compared to passive information delivery (based on cognitive science research)
- Students develop genuine problem-solving competence rather than memorization

### Institutional Research

The Institute for Experiential AI is conducting ongoing research evaluating:
- How Learning Mode shapes student outcomes
- Impact on faculty workflows and efficiency
- Effectiveness of responsible AI integration frameworks

### Faculty Adoption Drivers (Three Primary Motivations)

1. **Automating tedious, repetitive tasks** (grading rubrics, email responses)
2. **Collaborative thought partnership** for complex work (grant writing, research design)
3. **Creating personalized learning experiences** for students

### Known Limitations

- Research based on a narrow temporal window (May-June 2025) and a single institution's qualitative survey
- Only 1.5% of higher education email conversations captured in quantitative analysis
- Early adopter bias present in faculty survey data
- Long-term learning outcome data not yet available
- Faculty rate AI-assisted grading as least effective despite frequent use

## 9. Comparison to Other University AI Deployments

### Northeastern + Claude vs. Microsoft Copilot Deployments

| Dimension | Northeastern/Claude | Microsoft Copilot (typical) |
|---|---|---|
| **Pedagogical focus** | Learning Mode with Socratic questioning; designed to develop critical thinking | General-purpose productivity assistant embedded in Office 365 |
| **Integration approach** | Standalone platform with SSO; faculty Projects with custom prompts | Embedded in existing Microsoft 365 tools (Word, Teams, etc.) |
| **Training data commitment** | No training on user data | Enterprise data protection; no training on user data |
| **Education-specific features** | Learning Mode, faculty system prompts, student builder program | General enterprise features; no education-specific mode |
| **Scale** | 50,000+ at Northeastern; early-stage education program | 79% of enterprises report usage; 6.9M US knowledge workers (2024) |
| **Philosophy** | "Responsible AI" with emphasis on augmentation over automation | Productivity-first; AI as efficiency multiplier |

### Other University AI Platforms

- **OpenAI/ChatGPT:** Dominates generative AI education space with robust API; integrates with Microsoft Teams and Office
- **Google Gemini:** Leverages Google Scholar and Workspace ecosystem; strong for institutions already in Google's ecosystem
- **Open-source (Llama, etc.):** Higher upfront technical investment but lower ongoing costs; full data sovereignty

### Key Differentiators of Claude for Education

1. **Pedagogical design:** Only major AI platform with a dedicated education mode that actively teaches rather than answers
2. **University design partner model:** Deep co-development relationship, not just licensing
3. **Research integration:** Joint studies with faculty producing published findings on AI in education
4. **Student builder ecosystem:** API credits and ambassador programs creating student-led AI innovation

## 11. AI Policy and Governance at Northeastern

### Policy 125: Policy on the Use of Artificial Intelligence Systems

Northeastern established a formal AI policy (Policy 125) covering faculty and staff use of AI in university operations and outside professional activities. Key provisions:

**Universal Requirements (All Uses):**
- Provide appropriate attribution when using AI to generate content in scholarly publications
- Regularly verify AI outputs for accuracy and appropriateness
- Test systems processing personal information to ensure no bias or discrimination

**University Operations (Additional Requirements):**
- Submit AI systems involving confidential information, personal data, or restricted research to the **AI Review Committee (AIRC)**
- Obtain Office of Information Security approval for vendor or internal systems

**Governance Structure:**
- **AI Review Committee (AIRC):** Reviews and approves AI systems handling sensitive data; maintains an approved systems list
- **Office of Information Security:** Conducts security reviews for all systems
- **Responsible Offices:** Provost, General Counsel, and Information Security coordinate implementation
- Three operational areas with published detailed guidelines: administrative work, research applications, teaching and learning

### Center for Responsible AI and Governance (CRAIG)

- **First NSF-funded** responsible AI center of its kind
- Led by **John Basl**
- Partners with industry leaders including **Meta**
- Combines academic rigor with real-world industry expertise
- Focus: making responsible AI practices the standard across the field

### Institute for Experiential AI

- **Responsible AI Practice** led by Director Cansu Canca
- Framework includes: Minimal Viable Governance (MVG), policy-to-practice controls, model inventory, lifecycle accountability, and continuous monitoring
- Bridges the gap between awareness and action for organizations

### Strategic Positioning

Using AI to enhance invention, creativity, discovery, effectiveness, and efficiency in teaching, research, and administration is declared a **strategic priority** of the university.

## 12. Lessons for Other Universities Considering Campus-Wide AI Deployment

### From Northeastern's Experience

1. **Start with institutional commitment.** Northeastern had an existing academic plan centered on AI before the Anthropic partnership. This strategic foundation made campus-wide deployment viable rather than ad hoc.

2. **Choose a design partner, not just a vendor.** The "university design partner" model means Northeastern co-creates tools and frameworks rather than simply licensing software. This produces better-adapted solutions and positions the institution as a thought leader.

3. **Invest in governance infrastructure first.** Policy 125, the AI Review Committee, and the Office of Information Security review process were established to provide clear guardrails before deploying AI at scale.

4. **Emphasize augmentation over automation.** The Anthropic report shows the most effective uses are collaborative (77.4% augmentation in teaching), while the most automated use (grading) is rated least effective by faculty.

5. **Provide faculty with control.** Claude's Projects feature lets instructors customize AI behavior for their specific courses, preventing a one-size-fits-all approach.

6. **Address the FERPA gap explicitly.** Northeastern's clear guidance on what data categories cannot be shared with AI tools helps avoid legal exposure while still enabling broad access.

7. **Create student pathways.** API credits, ambassador programs, and hackathons transform students from consumers to builders of AI tools.

8. **Invest in training.** Virtual workshops, livestream tutorials, and student guides are essential for driving responsible adoption across a diverse community.

### Broader Best Practices from the Higher Education Sector

- **Start with targeted pilots, then scale** based on lessons learned and proven frameworks
- **Establish cross-functional governance** with representatives from IT, academic leadership, operations, security, and compliance
- **Evaluate total cost of ownership** including licensing, infrastructure, training, and ongoing support
- **Leverage existing cloud partnerships** (institutions with strong Microsoft or AWS relationships may find advantages with corresponding AI platforms)
- **Develop AI literacy programs** before deploying tools -- stakeholders need to understand both opportunities and risks
- **Design assessments for the AI age** -- faculty need support reimagining assignments that AI cannot simply complete

### Platform Selection Considerations

| Factor | Consideration |
|---|---|
| Pedagogical needs | Does the platform have education-specific features? (Claude: yes; Copilot: limited) |
| Existing ecosystem | Microsoft-heavy institutions may benefit from Copilot integration |
| Data sovereignty | Open-source options offer full control; cloud vendors offer convenience |
| Research needs | API access and compute credits matter for research-intensive institutions |
| Budget | Enterprise licenses vs. per-user costs vs. open-source infrastructure investment |
| Student engagement | Builder programs and hackathons drive innovation and learning |
