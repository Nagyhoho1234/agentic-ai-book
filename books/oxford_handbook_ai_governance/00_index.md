## Core Thesis

AI governance is the process by which society organizes, steers, and manages the integration of AI into the governance ecosystem. AI differs from prior "artificial entities" (governments, corporations, markets) in two critical ways: (1) the pace of AI progress vastly outstrips institutional adaptation, and (2) AI systems increasingly make decisions without humans in the loop. The handbook argues that governing AI requires not just regulating the technology itself, but reshaping how AI interacts with all other actors in the governance ecosystem -- governments, markets, organizations, and individuals.

## Key Relevance for University AI Adoption

Universities occupy a unique position in the AI governance landscape as simultaneously:
- **Producers** of AI research and trained talent
- **Consumers** deploying AI in administration, teaching, and research
- **Educators** shaping the next generation's understanding of AI ethics and governance
- **Public institutions** subject to accountability, transparency, and fairness requirements

The most directly relevant sections for university governance are:
- **Section II** (values: fairness, privacy, accountability, explainability, structural injustice)
- **Section IV** (frameworks for public organizations, ecosystem governance models)
- **Section V** (assessing when to automate, transparency, auditing, bias mitigation)
- **Section VI** (public opinion, stakeholder engagement, worker roles)
- **Section VII** (labor market impacts, shared prosperity)
