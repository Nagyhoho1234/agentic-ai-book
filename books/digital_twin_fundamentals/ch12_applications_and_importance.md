## Summary

This chapter provides a broad survey of digital twin applications across diverse sectors, emphasizing the technology's versatility and growing importance. It serves as a comprehensive review chapter, touching on agriculture, education, healthcare, manufacturing, automotive, security, smart cities, and weather forecasting applications.

The chapter begins by distinguishing between digital model, digital shadow, and digital twin (reinforcing the definitions from Chapter 1), then traces the history of digital twins from NASA through Grieves' 2002 presentation. It presents data-centric perspectives on digital twins, identifying five categories of data involved: product design data, process design data, product manufacturing data, product service data, and product scrap/recycling data.

For each application domain, the chapter discusses current implementations, benefits, challenges, and examples. The manufacturing section is particularly detailed, covering GHOST (Geometric information-Historical samples-Object attribute-Snapshot collection-Topology constraint) digital twin modeling, flexible manufacturing systems (FMS), and electromechanical product frameworks with four-layer architectures.

The chapter concludes with a discussion of challenges facing digital twin adoption: operability, security, sustainability, dependability, and training requirements.

## Key Concepts

- **Five data categories in digital twins:**
  1. Product design data
  2. Process design data
  3. Product manufacturing data (inspection, logistics)
  4. Product service data (service, maintenance, usage)
  5. Product scrap or recycling data

- **Application domains surveyed:**

  1. **Agriculture:** Cost savings, quality improvement, crop management, logistics and supply chain optimization

  2. **Education:** Curriculum integration, virtual factory visits during pandemic, hands-on learning without physical presence

  3. **Healthcare:** Medical recommendation systems, surgical risk planning, medical device design, Mater Hospital radiology optimization

  4. **Manufacturing and industry:** GHOST modeling for FMS, digital twin workshops (virtual + physical), electromechanical product design with four-layer architecture (physical space, virtual space, data processing, system application)

  5. **Automotive:** Vehicle performance tracking, software updates, cyber security (Cybellum), bridging physical and virtual prototypes

  6. **Security:** Blockchain integration, building information modeling with IoT authentication, Cyber Physical Production Systems (CPPS), ANGEL system for microgrid security

  7. **Smart cities:** Urban scenario simulation, observation and administration

  8. **Weather forecasting and meteorology:** Mobile phones as distributed data acquisition stations, duplicating physical weather systems digitally

- **GHOST digital twin modeling:** Geometric information--Historical samples--Object attribute--Snapshot collection--Topology constraint framework for flexible manufacturing systems

- **Four-layer electromechanical product framework:**
  - Physical space layer (sensors, instruments, equipment)
  - Virtual space layer (product twin model, parallel-environment model)
  - Data processing layer (capturing, noise removal, feature extraction, quality management)
  - System application layer (knowledge mining, diagnosis, prediction)

- **Challenges:**
  - Operability (functioning in all conditions)
  - Security (data protection at all times)
  - Sustainability (long-term viability)
  - Dependability (reliability in all situations)
  - Training (skilled personnel needed)

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Agriculture is an underexplored digital twin domain.** The literature shows promise for crop management, supply chain optimization, and quality improvement -- relevant for agricultural scientists looking at precision farming.

2. **Weather forecasting via distributed sensing** is an innovative concept. Using billions of mobile phones as weather data acquisition stations, combined with digital twin modeling, could revolutionize meteorological science.

3. **The four-layer framework** for electromechanical products provides a practical architecture template applicable beyond its original manufacturing context -- any domain with physical sensing, virtual modeling, data processing, and decision support.

4. **Cyber security for digital twins** is a growing concern, especially in automotive (Cybellum example) and critical infrastructure. The ANGEL system for microgrid security demonstrates how digital twins can model both physical and cyber layers.

5. **Education and training applications** should not be overlooked. Digital twins of factories and laboratories enable hands-on learning experiences without physical access -- particularly valuable post-pandemic.

6. **Blockchain + digital twin** combination addresses data integrity and accountability concerns, ensuring that twin data has not been tampered with.
