# Chapter 1: Digital Twin -- Origin to Future

**Authors:** Maulshree Singh, Evert Fuenmayor, Eoin P. Hinchy, Yuansong Qiao, Niall Murray, Declan Devine
**Affiliations:** Athlone Institute of Technology, Ireland; University of Limerick, Ireland
**Pages:** 1--18

## Summary

This foundational chapter traces the complete trajectory of digital twin technology from its conceptual origins to its projected future. The authors provide a rigorous definition-based framework, distinguishing digital twins from related concepts like digital models and digital shadows. They establish a five-dimensional digital twin model and discuss the technology's evolution through the lens of product lifecycle management (PLM).

The concept originated with NASA in the 1960s, where mirrored physical systems were used for space exploration. Michael Grieves formally introduced the digital twin concept in 2002 at a Society of Manufacturing Engineers conference at the University of Michigan, framing it as a "Conceptual Ideal for PLM." The term "digital twin" was later coined by John Vickers of NASA in a 2010 roadmap report.

The chapter defines digital twin as a virtual representation of a physical entity that mirrors its real-world counterpart in real time through continuous data exchange. This distinguishes it from a digital model (no automatic data transfer) or a digital shadow (one-way data transfer only). A true digital twin requires bidirectional data flow.

## Key Concepts

- **Three definitions clarified:**
  - **Digital model:** digital representation with no automatic data exchange with the physical object
  - **Digital shadow:** one-way data transfer from physical to digital
  - **Digital twin:** bidirectional data flow between physical and digital entities

- **Five-dimensional digital twin model:** physical entity, virtual entity, services, data, and connections

- **Digital Twin Prototype (DTP):** describes a prototypical physical artifact; contains informational sets needed to produce a physical version

- **Digital Twin Instance (DTI):** describes a specific corresponding physical product; remains linked throughout the lifecycle

- **Digital Twin Environment (DTE):** integrated, multi-domain physics application space for operating digital twins; serves predictive and interrogative purposes

- **Product lifecycle phases** where digital twins apply: design, production, operations, maintenance, and disposal

- **Key enabling technologies:** IoT, cloud computing, big data analytics, AI/ML, simulation platforms

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Distinguish your digital representation carefully.** If your model only receives data from the physical system (one-way), it is a digital shadow, not a twin. True twins require bidirectional data flow enabling the virtual model to also influence the physical system.

2. **Start with the five-dimensional model** when planning a digital twin implementation: define the physical entity, its virtual counterpart, the services to be provided, the data requirements, and the connection infrastructure.

3. **The DTP/DTI/DTE framework** provides a practical hierarchy: begin with a prototype-level twin for design validation, then create instance-level twins for individual assets, operated within an integrated environment.

4. **Lifecycle thinking is essential.** Digital twins provide the most value when they span the entire lifecycle of a product or system, from design through disposal.

5. **Industry 4.0 context.** Digital twins are positioned as a cornerstone technology of the fourth industrial revolution, alongside IoT, CPS, and advanced analytics.
