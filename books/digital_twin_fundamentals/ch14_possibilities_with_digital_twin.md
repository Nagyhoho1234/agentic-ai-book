# Chapter 14: Possibilities with Digital Twin

**Authors:** Vismay Shah, Anilkumar Suthar
**Affiliations:** L.J. Institute of Engineering and Technology, LJK University, Gujarat, India; Gujarat Technological University, India
**Pages:** 219--232

## Summary

This chapter explores the possibilities and potential of digital twin technology in three specific sectors: aviation, automotive, and supply chain management. For each sector, the authors explain why digital twins are valuable, what possibilities they enable, and provide real-world examples of successful implementation.

The chapter begins with a review of digital twin fundamentals (DTP, DTI, DTE types, predictive and interrogative purposes) and its Industry 4.0 context, then dives into sector-specific possibilities. The aviation section is particularly detailed, covering preventive maintenance, component lifecycle management, fleet-level prediction, and specific examples from Rolls-Royce and GE Aviation. The automotive section features Bridgestone's tire development and Tesla's per-vehicle twins. The supply chain section discusses bottleneck identification, virtual process testing, demand surge response, and Capgemini's D-GEM platform implementation.

The chapter emphasizes that digital twin possibilities extend well beyond these three sectors and that the technology can be applied wherever there is a need for real-time monitoring, prediction, and optimization of physical systems.

## Key Concepts

- **Aviation sector possibilities:**

  1. **Aviation engineering + digital twin:** Aircraft maintenance is critical; digital twins enable preventive (rather than reactive) maintenance, reducing downtime and increasing dependability

  2. **Concept of digital twin for aviation components:** Replicate, analyze, and monitor manufacturing and operations using multi-dimensional, context-sensitive process data

  3. **Importance in aviation industry:** Prediction of difficulties, monitoring, damage prevention, lifecycle extension of aircraft parts and engines, structural life prediction, fleet sustainability needs

  - **Rolls-Royce example:** Digital twin of aero engines enables preventive maintenance, reduces downtime, increases reliability; part of their suite of digital models supporting future vision
  - **GE Aviation example:** Using Azure Digital Twin for digital representation of aircraft and components; real-time state tracking, issue forecasting, repair identification, fleet condition monitoring

- **Automotive sector possibilities:**
  - Vehicle tire development (Bridgestone's Virtual Tire Modelling reduces physical prototypes)
  - Whole vehicle digital twins (Tesla's per-car twin with daily sensor data feeding software updates)
  - Development time reduction, resource savings, improved performance

- **Supply chain sector possibilities:**
  - Bottleneck identification and resolution
  - Virtual work process testing (avoiding real-world losses)
  - Efficiency improvement and operating expense reduction
  - Demand surge and urgent situation prediction
  - Transportation and delivery optimization
  - **Capgemini D-GEM example:** Digital Global Enterprise platform for client order management optimization

- **Cross-sector themes:**
  - Preventive/predictive maintenance reduces downtime and costs
  - Virtual testing before physical implementation saves resources
  - Real-time monitoring enables immediate response
  - Digital twins evolve and improve over time with more data

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Aviation shows digital twin maturity.** The Rolls-Royce and GE Aviation examples demonstrate that digital twins for complex, high-value assets (jet engines, aircraft) are already in production use, not just research prototypes.

2. **Fleet-level digital twins** provide value beyond individual assets. GE Aviation's approach of tracking the state and condition of entire fleets enables fleet-wide maintenance optimization and sustainability planning.

3. **Supply chain digital twins address resilience.** The ability to predict and respond to demand surges, transportation disruptions, and supply shortages through digital twin simulation is a strategic capability.

4. **Bottleneck identification** in supply chains through digital twins is a practical, high-value application. Finding bottlenecks early prevents escalating damage.

5. **Cloud platforms (Azure) are enabling aviation digital twins.** GE Aviation's use of Microsoft Azure demonstrates the trend toward cloud-based digital twin platforms that provide the necessary computational infrastructure.

6. **The three sectors share common patterns:** predictive maintenance, virtual testing, real-time monitoring, and data-driven optimization. These patterns are transferable to other domains.
