# Chapter 11: Digital Twin in Pharmaceutical Industry

**Authors:** Anant Kumar Patel, Ashish Patel, Kanchan Mona Patel
**Affiliations:** Swami Vivekanand College of Pharmacy, Indore, India; M.G.M. Medical College, Indore, India
**Pages:** 173--188

## Summary

This chapter examines the application of digital twin technology in the pharmaceutical industry, focusing primarily on manufacturing processes, supply chain management, and quality control. The pharmaceutical industry faces unique challenges -- precision requirements, regulatory compliance, wastage during manufacturing, and time-to-market pressures -- where digital twins can provide significant value.

The chapter covers the types of digital twins (DTP, DTI, DTE, and application-based types: parts twins, product twins, system twins, process twins), their application to pharmaceutical manufacturing, and real-world examples from major pharmaceutical companies. Continuous manufacturing (CM) is highlighted as a key trend in pharma where digital twins play an enabling role.

The building blocks of pharmaceutical digital twins are discussed: process analytical technology (PAT), data management systems, unit operations models, flowsheet models, system analyses methods, and integration approaches. The chapter presents specific case studies including GlaxoSmithKline/Atos/Siemens vaccine manufacturing, Takeda's Crohn's disease digital twin simulator, Virtonomy's medical device virtual trials, and Kydea's Pharma Digital Twin platform.

## Key Concepts

- **Types of digital twins by application:**
  - Parts twins or components twins (individual component properties)
  - Product twins (interaction between assembled parts)
  - System twins (entire system behavior and interactions)
  - Process twins (how different systems work together in manufacturing)

- **Continuous manufacturing (CM):** Shifting from batch to continuous processing in pharma, where digital twins enable real-time monitoring and adjustment of the continuous process

- **Process Analytical Technology (PAT):** Online/inline monitoring tools integrated with digital twins for real-time quality control during manufacturing

- **Quality by Design (QbD):** Using digital twins to support quality risk management in drug development and manufacturing

- **Building blocks of pharmaceutical digital twins:**
  - PAT methods for real-time measurement
  - Data management systems
  - Unit operations and flowsheet models
  - System analysis methods
  - Integration approaches

- **Pharmaceutical manufacturing applications:**
  - Batch wastage reduction
  - Process optimization
  - Quality control and compliance
  - Real-time process monitoring
  - Scale-up from lab to production

- **Supply chain digital twins:**
  - Modeling supply, manufacturing, inventory, and distribution
  - Predicting disruption impacts
  - Scenario simulation for resilient operations
  - Data inputs: customer insight, business processes, demand data, inventory policies, facility locations, productive capacity

- **Real-world examples:**
  - **GlaxoSmithKline + Atos + Siemens:** Process digital twin for vaccine manufacturing; sensors for data collection, three different models, live in-silica replica reducing batch wastage
  - **Takeda + PwC:** Crohn's disease digital twin simulator for scientific exchange with physicians
  - **Virtonomy:** Reducing time-to-market for medical devices through virtual clinical trials
  - **Kydea:** Pharma Digital Twin platform for planning, managing, and optimizing pharmaceutical processes

- **Advantages of digital twin in pharma:**
  1. Wastage reduction (the GSK example showed clear batch waste reduction)
  2. Cost savings (directly follows from reduced wastage)
  3. Faster time to market (fewer physical prototypes and failed batches)
  4. Smooth management (end-to-end visibility of manufacturing)
  5. Remote monitoring (entire manufacturing process can be monitored remotely)

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Process twins are the most immediately useful** in pharma manufacturing. They model how different unit operations interact in the complete manufacturing process, enabling optimization of the whole rather than individual steps.

2. **PAT integration is essential.** Real-time analytical measurements (inline, online) are what make a pharmaceutical digital twin "live." Without PAT, you have a simulation, not a twin.

3. **Continuous manufacturing is the natural fit** for digital twins in pharma. The continuous nature of CM aligns perfectly with real-time digital twin monitoring, unlike batch processes that have discrete start/stop points.

4. **Supply chain resilience planning** through digital twins is especially relevant post-pandemic. The ability to simulate disruptions (supplier failure, demand spikes, logistics breakdown) and plan responses provides strategic advantage.

5. **Regulatory considerations are paramount.** Pharmaceutical digital twins used for process control or quality decisions may need validation under GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice) requirements. Engage regulatory affairs early.

6. **The GSK/Atos/Siemens vaccine case** is a compelling proof of concept. Three models, sensor-based data collection, live in-silica replication of the physical process, with demonstrated reduction in batch wastage.
