# Chapter 10: Digital Twin in Smart Cities: Application and Benefits

**Author:** Manisha Vohra
**Affiliation:** Independent Researcher, India
**Pages:** 159--172

## Summary

This chapter explores how digital twin technology can be applied to the development and management of smart cities. The author argues that digital twins can transform entire cities by creating virtual replicas that enable planning, simulation, and optimization of urban systems including traffic, construction, healthcare facilities, structural monitoring, drainage, and power grids.

The chapter begins with a review of digital twin fundamentals and their relevance to urban environments, then systematically examines six application areas within smart cities. For each application, the author provides context, discusses how digital twins contribute, gives real-world examples where available, and lists specific benefits.

The concept of creating a digital twin of an entire city is introduced -- enabling what-if scenario simulation for urban planning, disaster preparedness (floods, infrastructure failures), and resource optimization. The linking of sensor data with city models is identified as the foundation of city-scale digital twins.

## Key Concepts

- **City-scale digital twins:** Virtual replicas of entire cities that integrate data from all urban systems for holistic planning and management

- **Six smart city applications:**

  1. **Traffic management:**
     - Finding weak points in traffic networks
     - Testing solutions before implementation
     - Optimizing traffic flow
     - Example: Aimsun company's digital traffic networks for simulating goods and people movement

  2. **Construction:**
     - Design phase optimization and impact testing
     - Construction monitoring (including remote)
     - Modular construction feasibility analysis
     - Examples: Lendlease Group's sustainable timber tower feasibility; CadMakers' hybrid mass-timber building design

  3. **Structural health monitoring:**
     - Real-time monitoring of infrastructure health
     - Predictive maintenance of structures
     - Remote monitoring capabilities
     - Safety hazard prevention

  4. **Healthcare:**
     - Hospital workflow optimization
     - Medical product manufacturing monitoring
     - Testing operational strategy changes virtually
     - Examples: GE's Hospital of Future (HoF) digital twin; Mater Hospital Dublin workflow optimization with Siemens

  5. **Drainage systems:**
     - Real-time monitoring of surface water systems
     - Understanding and controlling system dynamics
     - Flood detection and prevention
     - Example: Pipedream engine (Bartos & Kerkez) for real-time hydraulic modeling using Saint-Venant equations + Kalman filtering

  6. **Power grids:**
     - Monitoring and optimizing power distribution
     - Supporting transition to carbon-neutral energy
     - Example: Hitachi Energy's IdentiQ for high-voltage direct current (HVDC) and power quality solutions

- **What-if scenario planning:** Using city digital twins to simulate floods, traffic changes, infrastructure failures, and policy decisions before implementation

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Start with a single urban system.** Building a complete city digital twin is an enormous undertaking. Start with one system (e.g., traffic or drainage) and expand incrementally.

2. **The Pipedream example is highly relevant for hydrologists.** The combination of Saint-Venant equations with Kalman filtering for real-time drainage system modeling represents a practical, physics-based digital twin approach applicable to water management research.

3. **Traffic digital twins offer quick, visible wins.** Traffic management is one of the most mature smart city applications of digital twins, with commercial solutions (Aimsun) already available.

4. **Hospital workflow optimization** is a proven application. The Mater Hospital/Siemens case study demonstrates tangible improvements in wait times, equipment utilization, and staffing efficiency.

5. **Carbon-neutral energy transition** can be accelerated by digital twins of power grids. Hitachi's IdentiQ shows how HVDC systems can be optimized for renewable energy integration.

6. **Sensor data is the foundation.** All smart city digital twin applications depend on comprehensive sensor networks. Urban IoT infrastructure planning must precede digital twin implementation.
