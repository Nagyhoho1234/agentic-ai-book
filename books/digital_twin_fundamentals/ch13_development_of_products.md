# Chapter 13: Digital Twin in Development of Products

**Author:** Pedro Pablo Chambi Condori
**Affiliation:** Universidad Nacional Jorge Basadre Grohmann, Tacna, Peru
**Pages:** 205--218

## Summary

This chapter focuses specifically on how digital twin technology aids product development in organizations. It takes a business-oriented perspective, discussing how organizations of all sizes can leverage digital twins to reduce risk, improve product quality, accelerate time-to-market, and optimize resource usage during the product development lifecycle.

The author covers the organizational context thoroughly, discussing how product development is fundamental to organizational success and how the traditional development process (concept, design, prototype, test, iterate) is resource-intensive and risky. Digital twin technology is positioned as a solution that enables virtual prototyping, performance prediction, and iterative testing without the cost of physical prototypes.

The chapter includes several real-world case studies: Bridgestone Corporation using virtual tire modeling to reduce physical prototype iterations, Siemens NX CAD for car design with digital twin validation, and Tesla's use of per-vehicle digital twins for continuous software improvement. The advantages of digital twins for product development are enumerated: decision-making support, downtime avoidance, efficiency maximization, cost savings, and optimal resource use.

## Key Concepts

- **Digital twin in the product development lifecycle:**
  - Concept phase: Virtual feasibility studies
  - Design phase: Virtual prototyping and simulation
  - Testing phase: Virtual testing and what-if analysis
  - Production phase: Manufacturing process optimization
  - Operations phase: In-service monitoring and continuous improvement
  - Disposal phase: End-of-life decision support

- **DTP and DTI in product development:**
  - Digital Twin Prototype (DTP): Used during design -- contains information to produce a physical version
  - Digital Twin Instance (DTI): Used during operations -- linked to a specific product throughout its life

- **Digital twin for manufacturing systems:**
  - Decision-making automation through simulation
  - Value creation in operations and service management
  - Real-time analysis for rapid decisions
  - Support for SMEs and large enterprises alike

- **Case studies:**
  - **Bridgestone Corporation:** Virtual Tyre Modelling creates digital twin of tires at development stage, reducing number of physical prototypes, saving materials, time, and cost; improving tire performance and life
  - **Siemens NX CAD:** Digital enterprise solution portfolio for car design, enabling validation before physical build
  - **Tesla, Inc.:** Every Tesla car has its own digital twin; daily sensor data feeds software updates; real-time data drives continuous improvement

- **Advantages for product development:**
  1. Decision making: Real-time analysis of performance, conditions, and error prediction
  2. Avoiding downtime: Predicting and preventing failures before they occur
  3. Maximizing efficiency: Developing products based on real-time performance and condition analysis
  4. Cost savings: Timely error aversion reduces wastage and prototyping costs
  5. Optimum use of resources: Reducing resource wastage through virtual testing

- **Industry 4.0 context:** Automated, interconnected, flexible manufacturing revolution where digital twins enable virtual replicas to interact with physical counterparts bidirectionally in real time

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Virtual prototyping reduces physical iterations.** The Bridgestone case shows how digital twins of products (tires) at the development stage directly reduce the number of physical prototypes needed, saving materials, time, and money.

2. **Per-instance digital twins enable continuous improvement.** Tesla's approach -- giving every sold car its own digital twin fed by daily sensor data -- enables continuous product improvement through real-world usage data, not just development-phase simulation.

3. **SMEs can benefit too.** The chapter discusses guidelines for implementing digital twins in small and medium enterprises, not just large corporations. Start small, focus on the highest-value product or process.

4. **Product lifecycle management (PLM) is the natural host** for digital twin in product development. If your organization already uses PLM tools, extending them with digital twin capabilities is a natural progression.

5. **Decision-making support** is the primary value proposition for product development. Digital twins do not replace human decision-makers but provide them with better data, simulations, and predictions to make informed choices.

6. **Cross-functional benefits.** Digital twins for product development benefit not just R&D but also manufacturing (process optimization), sales (product configuration), and service (maintenance prediction).
