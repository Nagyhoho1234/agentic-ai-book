# Chapter 10: The Big Black Bag

**Author:** Carey Goldberg and Isaac "Zak" Kohane

## Summary

The final chapter is structured in three parts: a literary reference, a speculative fiction scenario, and an interview with Microsoft CTO Kevin Scott.

**The literary frame:** The chapter takes its title from "The Little Black Bag," a 1950s science fiction story in which a high-tech doctor's kit from the future is accidentally transported to the past, falling into the hands of a washed-up, alcoholic doctor. The kit redeems the doctor, allowing him to practice "heroically" -- detecting infections, curing wounds, operating without scars. The story, nearly 75 years old, illustrates enduring hopes and fears about technology transforming medicine. The authors ask: what would the AI equivalent of that little black bag look like?

**Speculative fiction -- Dora and Frida (2033):** The chapter presents a detailed fictional scenario set 10 years in the future. Dora, a 90-year-old woman with multiple chronic conditions (heart failure, diabetes, arthritis), lives independently in rent-controlled elderly housing on Social Security income. Her AI medical aide, "Frida" (powered by GPT-7, a descendant of GPT-4), provides continuous health monitoring and support.

Frida's capabilities include:
- Daily morning check-ins assessing sleep, symptoms, and leg swelling via phone camera
- Automatic weight monitoring via connected scale, with medication adjustments (e.g., adding extra Lasix for fluid retention)
- Heart rate monitoring with alerts to Dora's cardiologist
- Dietary counseling (keeping salt and calories low) interspersed with friendly conversation about telenovelas
- Proactive identification of new treatments (heart muscle-directed gene therapy with FDA approval and Medicare coverage)
- Coordination with Dr. Ramirez for appointment scheduling and contraindication checking
- Visual assessment of skin infections via phone camera with treatment recommendations
- Continuous monitoring through phone sensors for medication adherence, falls, mood changes

The scenario is not utopian -- Frida makes mistakes. Once she recommended an incorrect drug dosage (Dora double-checked with her doctor). Another time her sensors failed to detect Dora's fall in the bathroom for two hours. But overall, Frida enables Dora to remain fiercely independent at 90, providing the kind of continuous, responsive care that the overwhelmed healthcare system cannot deliver. Her doctor's practice is so busy that appointments take weeks and visits last only minutes.

**Interview with Kevin Scott (Microsoft CTO):** Scott provides a technology-industry perspective on AI and medicine. Key points from the interview:

- Scott was not surprised by GPT-4's capabilities because he had been closely tracking progress, but he acknowledges most people were "not expecting it at all"
- His vision: AI models will get more powerful and simultaneously cheaper and more ubiquitous -- available to everyone
- Demographic reality drives urgency: aging populations in industrialized countries (Italy, Japan, Germany, China, US) mean more elderly patients and fewer healthcare workers
- Personal anecdote: Scott's immunocompromised brother got COVID in rural Virginia and his local doctors failed to prescribe Paxlovid. Scott, connected to COVID research, intervened to get a prescription -- illustrating how information access directly affects health outcomes
- AI could be that "knowledgeable friend" for everyone, providing second opinions and current treatment information
- AI cannot replace physical human-to-human healthcare interactions; nursing homes need real humans
- AI can make administrative workers more productive with paperwork and coding, freeing time for patient care
- Self-driving car analogy: AI will save thousands of lives but early deaths will be a "massive hit for the whole idea"
- Current models organize existing knowledge superbly but have not yet made novel scientific discoveries; Scott believes this is coming within 5-10 years
- The choice facing society: constrain AI out of fear, submit to AI replacement, or partner with AI to accomplish things neither can do alone

**Epilogue framing:** Kohane reflects on his 2017 assessment that AI "does not do well at understanding the wide world, at picking up mood or subtle signs of distress, at convincing a resistant human to listen to the doctor." He wrote then: "We don't need AI for that. We need a caring village." But now, with LLMs, AI has entered a new phase where it is or soon could be good at all those skills.

## Key Medical AI Applications

- **Continuous elderly care monitoring:** AI-powered daily health assessments via phone (vision, voice, connected devices)
- **Automated medication management:** Real-time dosage adjustments based on weight, heart rate, and symptom monitoring
- **Proactive treatment identification:** AI scanning new FDA-approved treatments and insurance coverage for individual patients
- **Visual clinical assessment:** Phone camera-based skin infection identification and monitoring
- **Care coordination:** Automated communication between AI aide, patient, and multiple specialists
- **Fall detection and emergency response:** Sensor-based monitoring for elderly patients living alone
- **New drug discovery support:** AI models progressing toward generating novel scientific hypotheses and compound discoveries

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- The 2033 scenario is technically plausible with incremental improvements over current capabilities -- no fundamental breakthroughs are assumed beyond continued scaling and integration
- Continuous monitoring transforms healthcare from episodic (doctor visits) to continuous (daily AI check-ins), fundamentally changing the care model
- Imperfect AI can still be transformative: even with occasional errors, Frida provides care that vastly exceeds what the healthcare system delivers to elderly, low-income patients
- The demographic imperative (aging populations, shrinking healthcare workforce) makes AI-assisted care not optional but necessary in many industrialized countries
- Novel scientific discovery by AI is the next frontier -- current models synthesize existing knowledge but do not yet generate genuinely new hypotheses or compounds
- The human-AI partnership framing (neither constraining AI nor submitting to it, but collaborating) is the most productive stance for scientists
