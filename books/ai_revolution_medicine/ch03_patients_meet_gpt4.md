# Chapter 3: Patients Meet GPT-4

**Author:** Carey Goldberg

## Summary

This chapter shifts the perspective from physicians to patients, exploring how GPT-4 could transform the patient experience. Goldberg examines multiple scenarios where patients interact with or benefit from AI-generated medical information.

A central demonstration involves GPT-4 translating complex medical jargon into plain language that patients can understand. The AI successfully explains diagnoses, treatment plans, and medication instructions at various reading levels -- including 6th grade comprehension. It can also produce these explanations in multiple languages, addressing the needs of the more than 25 million Americans who report limited English proficiency.

The chapter explores GPT-4's role in patient advocacy. Patients often struggle to understand their conditions, navigate insurance systems, or prepare questions for doctor visits. GPT-4 demonstrates the ability to help with all of these tasks, acting as a knowledgeable intermediary that is available 24/7 and never makes patients feel rushed or judged.

However, Goldberg raises serious concerns about patient safety. Unlike a doctor, GPT-4 cannot perform physical examinations, order tests, or intervene in emergencies. If patients use it as a substitute for professional medical care rather than a supplement, the results could be dangerous. The chapter discusses the risk that patients might over-rely on AI advice, delay seeking necessary care, or misinterpret AI-generated information.

The chapter also addresses health equity. GPT-4 could democratize access to medical knowledge for underserved populations who lack access to specialists. But it could also widen disparities if access to AI tools is unevenly distributed or if the AI performs worse for certain demographic groups due to training data biases.

## Key Medical AI Applications

- **Health literacy translation:** Converting medical information to various reading levels (6th grade, 8th grade, etc.)
- **Multilingual communication:** Explaining medical concepts in patients' native languages
- **After-visit summaries:** Generating personalized, accessible summaries of doctor visits
- **Patient education:** Creating tailored educational materials about specific conditions
- **Insurance navigation:** Helping patients understand explanation-of-benefits forms and coverage
- **Visit preparation:** Helping patients formulate questions before appointments

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- Health literacy is a major barrier to effective care; AI-generated plain-language explanations represent a scalable solution to a problem that affects roughly 36% of American adults
- The gap between AI capability and safe deployment is particularly wide in patient-facing applications, where users may lack the expertise to evaluate AI output
- Equity implications cut both ways: AI could narrow or widen health disparities depending on implementation decisions
- User studies with real patients are essential to understand whether AI-generated health information actually improves comprehension and health outcomes, not just produces plausible-looking text
