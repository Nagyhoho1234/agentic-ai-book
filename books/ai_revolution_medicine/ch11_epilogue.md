# Epilogue

**Author:** Peter Lee

## Summary

Written on March 16, 2023 -- just two days after OpenAI officially released GPT-4 -- the Epilogue captures the breathless pace of the AI revolution. Lee notes that on the same day Microsoft revealed GPT-4 powered Bing chat, Google announced their PaLM API, Anthropic announced Claude, and Microsoft announced GPT-4 integrations across Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook.

Lee shares a quote from Kevin Scott, his colleague at Microsoft, by William Hazlitt (1818): "Though [it has] added much to human power, [it has] not much increased human goodness; nay, rather, furnished men with the means of doing more mischief, and made them more vail and arrogant." He asked GPT-4 what Hazlitt would say about LLMs, and it responded: "Behold the marvels of AI, a testament to human ingenuity and boundless ambition. Yet, I must caution that with newfound power comes the temptation to wield it recklessly... For it is in the balance of wisdom and humility that we shall harness AI's potential for the greater good."

The Epilogue is structured around three key ideas:

**Phase Change:** GPT-4 represents a technological phase change, not just an incremental improvement. Previously, general intelligence was "frozen inside human brains" -- now it has "melted into water and can flow everywhere." This means regulations specific to GPT-4 will quickly become obsolete as more powerful models emerge. We must think about a world with increasingly capable machines and decide how we want that world to work.

**Stages of Grief:** Lee describes his personal intellectual journey with GPT-4 as analogous to stages of grief: mild interest, then intense skepticism, frustration, even disgust at colleagues who believed something special was happening, then growing awe and wonderment evolving into euphoria, and finally coming back to earth with a clear view of both positive and negative consequences. He raises the provocative question: is GPT-4 forcing us to confront the possibility that intelligence is based on simpler mechanisms than we assumed? He connects this to Copernicus (Earth is not the center) and Watson/Crick (life defined by four amino acids) -- discoveries that challenged human hubris. Perhaps "the most brilliant AI researchers might be the ones most stuck on this."

**Partnership:** The fundamental choice: constrain AI out of fear, submit to AI and become less needed, or partner with AI today to shape its future. Lee advocates for partnership -- accomplishing things that neither humans nor AI can do alone. He urges readers to get directly familiar with the technology, form their own opinions through direct experience rather than others' reports, and be active and vocal about what they discover.

Lee's closing hope: that the book helps readers join the debate about AI in healthcare, contributing to a future aimed at "human goodness" rather than "doing more mischief."

## Key Medical AI Applications

- **Rapid pace of deployment:** Multiple major AI products launched in the same week as GPT-4
- **Partnership model:** AI augmenting rather than replacing human medical capabilities
- **Continuous improvement:** Each new model generation may resolve limitations of previous versions

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- The pace of AI advancement is so rapid that any assessment of current limitations may be outdated within months; scientists should plan for capabilities that exceed current models
- Direct, hands-on experience with AI tools is essential -- secondhand reports and academic papers cannot substitute for personal experimentation
- Regulations and frameworks must be designed to be model-agnostic and future-proof, not tied to specific AI systems
- The question "can next-word prediction lead to intelligence?" has profound implications not just for computer science but for our understanding of cognition itself
- Scientists have a responsibility to engage in the public debate about AI -- their expertise in evaluating evidence and understanding uncertainty is urgently needed
- The human-AI partnership is not just a philosophical position but a practical research program: identifying the tasks where humans+AI outperform either alone
