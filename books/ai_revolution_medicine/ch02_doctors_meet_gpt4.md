# Chapter 2: Doctors Meet GPT-4

**Author:** Carey Goldberg

## Summary

This chapter documents what happens when practicing physicians encounter GPT-4 for the first time. Goldberg interviews and observes multiple doctors across specialties as they test GPT-4 with real clinical scenarios they find challenging.

The doctors' reactions range from amazement to unease. Many are impressed by GPT-4's ability to generate comprehensive differential diagnoses, suggest relevant tests, and explain clinical reasoning. Some physicians note that GPT-4 catches things they might miss, particularly in areas outside their specialty. The AI demonstrates an ability to integrate information across medical domains in ways that reflect the breadth of a well-trained generalist.

However, the chapter also documents physicians' concerns. GPT-4 sometimes presents incorrect information with the same confident tone as correct information, making errors difficult to detect. It can "hallucinate" -- fabricating plausible-sounding but nonexistent medical studies or clinical guidelines. Doctors note that the AI lacks the physical examination skills, intuition built from years of patient interaction, and the ability to read nonverbal cues that are essential to clinical practice.

The chapter explores the concept of "pajama time" -- the hours doctors spend after work completing documentation. GPT-4's ability to generate clinical notes from conversation transcripts could save physicians significant time, addressing a major contributor to physician burnout.

A key theme is the challenge of integrating GPT-4 into clinical workflows. Even if the AI produces high-quality output, it must fit into existing patterns of care delivery to be useful.

## Key Medical AI Applications

- **Differential diagnosis across specialties:** GPT-4 performing competently in cardiology, endocrinology, psychiatry, and other fields
- **Clinical note generation:** Converting doctor-patient conversations into structured medical documentation
- **Medical education:** GPT-4 as a teaching tool for medical students and residents
- **Second-opinion generation:** Providing an additional perspective on complex cases
- **Reducing "pajama time":** Automating documentation to reduce physician burnout

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- Expert evaluation is essential: only physicians with domain expertise can reliably assess whether GPT-4's medical output is correct
- The "confident but wrong" problem is a fundamental challenge -- GPT-4 does not signal uncertainty the way a human expert might hedge or qualify statements
- Integration into clinical workflows matters as much as raw capability; a tool that disrupts workflow will not be adopted regardless of its quality
- Physician burnout from documentation is a quantifiable problem (studies show 15-30 minutes saved per encounter note), making this a high-impact target for AI assistance
