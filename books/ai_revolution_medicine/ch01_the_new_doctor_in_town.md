# Chapter 1: The New "Doctor" in Town

**Author:** Carey Goldberg

## Summary

This chapter provides the reader's first direct encounter with GPT-4's medical capabilities. Goldberg describes the moment she first witnessed GPT-4 handle a complex medical case and her astonishment at the quality of its responses. The chapter walks through several medical scenarios to demonstrate the breadth of GPT-4's knowledge.

GPT-4 is shown interpreting symptoms, generating differential diagnoses, and reasoning through clinical scenarios in ways that mirror -- and sometimes surpass -- the performance of medical professionals. The chapter emphasizes that GPT-4 passed the USMLE (United States Medical Licensing Examination) with scores well above the passing threshold, a feat no previous AI system had achieved convincingly.

Goldberg frames GPT-4 not as a replacement for doctors but as a "new doctor in town" -- a powerful tool that will inevitably change the landscape of medicine. She highlights the speed at which this transformation is unfolding, noting that ChatGPT became the fastest-adopted technology in history.

The chapter also introduces the book's methodology: the authors present actual GPT-4 interaction transcripts throughout the book, allowing readers to judge the AI's capabilities firsthand. These are not cherry-picked successes; failures and errors are also documented.

## Key Medical AI Applications

- **Medical exam performance:** GPT-4 passing the USMLE with high scores
- **Differential diagnosis generation:** Producing ranked lists of possible diagnoses from symptom descriptions
- **Clinical reasoning:** Walking through diagnostic logic step by step
- **Medical knowledge breadth:** Demonstrating expertise across multiple specialties simultaneously
- **Patient case analysis:** Interpreting complex, multi-system clinical presentations

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- GPT-4's medical competence is broad rather than narrow, distinguishing it from all previous medical AI systems that were designed for specific tasks
- The USMLE performance establishes a measurable baseline for comparing AI to human medical knowledge
- Scientists should test AI systems themselves rather than relying on secondhand reports; direct interaction reveals both capabilities and limitations that are difficult to convey through description
- The pace of AI advancement means that limitations documented today may be resolved in subsequent model versions
