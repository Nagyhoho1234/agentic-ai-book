## Report Overview

This consensus study report, commissioned by the U.S. Department of Defense under Executive Order 14110, examines how AI-enabled biological tools trained on biological data may increase or mitigate biosecurity risks. The committee of experts in AI, infectious agents, and biosecurity assessed the capability uplift (delta-AI) that current and emerging AI tools provide to the synthetic biology Design-Build-Test-Learn cycle, with specific focus on transmissible biological agents that could pose epidemic- or pandemic-scale consequences.

## Key Conclusions

1. The most significant AI capability uplift is in the **ideation and design** phases of synthetic biology, not in the physical build-and-test phases.
2. Current AI tools **can** design simple biomolecules (e.g., toxins) that could evade nucleic acid synthesis screening.
3. Current AI tools **cannot** de novo design a replication-competent virus or accurately model pathogenicity and transmissibility at epidemic/pandemic scale.
4. The **digital-to-physical divide** remains a critical bottleneck -- AI designs still require extensive wet-lab validation.
5. High-quality biological data is a **leading indicator** of emerging AI capabilities and should be monitored.
6. AI tools have enormous **beneficial potential** for biosurveillance, vaccine design, and medical countermeasure development.

## All Recommendations

- **Rec 1-1:** Continue vigorous research on biology of infectious agents; invest in biosurveillance networks via CDC and USDA.
- **Rec 1-2:** Fund AI-enabled design tools for medical countermeasure development.
- **Rec 1-3:** Establish public-private partnership (analogous to Operation Warp Speed) for AI tools and compute in emergency response.
- **Rec 2:** Develop an "if-then" strategy for continuous evaluation of data availability and emerging AI capabilities in the risk landscape.
- **Rec 3-1:** Fund creation of AI-compatible biological datasets and training sets; develop frontier AI tools trained on biological data.
- **Rec 3-2:** Treat biological databases as strategic assets; implement robust data provenance.
- **Rec 3-3:** NAIRR pilot should explore central repositories for training AI models.
