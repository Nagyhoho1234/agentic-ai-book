# Summary

## Comprehensive Summary

AI applications in the life sciences have the potential to enable biological discovery and design at a much-accelerated pace when combined with classical experimental approaches. AI models in biology are trained on existing biological datasets built from experimental data. The convergence of AI and biology is a relatively new area of research that spans several disciplines and affects the research workflow from hypothesis generation to data analysis. Current applications include genomics, omics analysis, synthetic biology, drug discovery, and healthcare delivery. The 2024 Nobel Prize for Chemistry recognized computational protein design and protein structure prediction (AlphaFold).

However, concerns have been raised that AI-enabled biological tools could be misused to design transmissible biological agents posing epidemic- or pandemic-scale threats. This report assesses how AI-enabled biological tools can **uniquely** enable biosecurity risks (the capability uplift, referred to as delta-AI) and how AI in biology can be used to **mitigate** such risks.

## AI-Enabled Biological Design

The most significant capability uplift of AI-enabled biological tools is in the **ideation and design phases** of the Design-Build-Test-Learn (DBTL) cycle. AI tools can accelerate research design by providing data-driven guidance for experimental validation, generating novel hypotheses, and discovering patterns in large datasets. Importantly, AI-enabled design does not replace the need for experimental validation.

The committee assessed delta-AI in the context of three harmful applications:

1. **Design of biomolecules (e.g., toxins):** Current AI tools CAN design and redesign toxins using different amino acid building blocks, but threats would likely be limited to the local level rather than epidemic/pandemic scale.

2. **Modification of existing pathogens for increased virulence:** AI tools MAY be capable of modeling features that predict phenotypes related to virulence, but insufficient datasets and the complexity of modeling biological networks remain limiting factors.

3. **De novo design of a virus:** NO available AI tool currently possesses this capability. No biological datasets exist to train such models. This would represent the most significant capability uplift risk for epidemic/pandemic-scale threats.

**Critical finding:** AI-enabled biological tools do NOT necessarily reduce the bottlenecks at the digital-physical divide (i.e., physical production of AI-designed experiments or outputs).

## AI-Enabled Applications for Biosecurity

AI tools can IMPROVE biosecurity by enhancing:
- Prediction, detection, prevention, and response to biological threats
- Biosurveillance capabilities
- Medical countermeasure (MCM) development (vaccines, antibodies)
- Emergency response acceleration

## Guiding Principles for Continuous Evaluation

The committee recommends an **"if-then" strategy** to account for the dynamic pace of AI development:
- Monitor data availability as a leading indicator of emerging capabilities
- Use principles-based framework for periodic reassessment of vulnerabilities
- Incorporate all four factors from the NASEM 2018 framework: usability of technology, usability as a weapon, requirements of actors, potential for mitigation
- Establish benchmarks and thresholds for activating risk assessment and mitigation

## Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening

Unlike other AI domains where digital harm is immediate, biological threats materialize only when physical agents are produced. Nucleic acid synthesis screening is an important checkpoint, but:
- More research is needed on AI-enhanced screening methodologies
- Current homology-based screening may be evaded by AI-redesigned sequences
- Benchtop synthesis devices could bypass commercial screening entirely

## Optimizing Data Resources

- Biological data is fragmented and often not suitable for AI model training
- The PDB (Protein Data Bank) is a model of a standardized, curated, publicly available resource
- Building AI-ready biological datasets should be a national research priority
- Data provenance and quality are essential

## Key Recommendations

**Recommendation 1-1:** U.S. agencies (DoD, HHS, USDA, DOE, DHS) should continue investing in research programs to understand the biology of infectious agents and implement biosurveillance networks through CDC and USDA globally.

**Recommendation 1-2:** Entities within HHS (NIH, BARDA, ARPA-H) and DoD (DARPA, Chemical and Biological Defense) should fund AI-enabled design tools for medical countermeasure development, especially for epidemics and pandemics.

**Recommendation 1-3:** BARDA, DOE, and DoD should establish a public-private partnership, analogous to Operation Warp Speed and the COVID-19 High-Performance Computing Consortium, that can leverage and provide access to AI-enabled tools and computational resources and be activated rapidly in an emergency response.

**Recommendation 2:** DoD and the U.S. AI Safety Institute should develop an "if-then" strategy to evaluate continuously both data availability and emerging AI capabilities. Evaluation of AI-enabled biological tool capabilities may be conducted in a sandbox environment.

**Recommendation 3-1:** Federal research funding agencies should fund efforts to: (a) create and steward AI-compatible data and training sets for biological applications; (b) initiate public-private partnerships for generating training datasets; and (c) develop frontier AI-enabled biological tools trained on biological data.

**Recommendation 3-2:** Federal agencies housing biological databases should treat them as strategic assets and implement robust data provenance mechanisms for the highest data quality.

**Recommendation 3-3:** A dedicated organization is central to sustainable national data stewardship. The NAIRR pilot should explore approaches for establishing central repositories for training AI models.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- AI tools accelerate ideation and design but cannot yet replace experimental validation for complex biological systems
- The physical production bottleneck (build and test) remains the primary barrier to misuse for creating transmissible agents
- Scientists should engage with nucleic acid synthesis screening improvements
- Invest in creating standardized, curated, AI-ready biological datasets
- Monitor for the emergence of datasets linking viral sequences to virulence/transmissibility phenotypes as a signal for emerging risk
- Public-private partnerships for data generation and AI tool development are encouraged
