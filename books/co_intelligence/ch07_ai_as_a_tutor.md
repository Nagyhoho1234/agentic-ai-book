# Chapter 7: AI as a Tutor

## Summary

This chapter focuses on AI's potential to transform education, from Bloom's famous "2 Sigma Problem" to the "Homework Apocalypse" and the promise of AI tutoring and flipped classrooms.

### Bloom's 2 Sigma Problem

Benjamin Bloom (1984) demonstrated that students tutored one-to-one performed two standard deviations better than classroom-taught students -- meaning the average tutored student scored higher than 98% of classroom students. This is the "2 Sigma Problem": we know one-to-one tutoring works extraordinarily well, but it is too expensive and labor-intensive to scale. No teaching method has consistently replicated this effect. AI could be the solution.

### The Homework Apocalypse

AI's first large-scale impact on education is destructive: it has made traditional homework obsolete.

**The pre-AI cheating baseline:**
- In 2008, homework improved test grades for 86% of students; by 2017, only 45% benefited
- Over half of students were looking up answers online by 2017
- 15% of students had paid someone to do assignments (essay mills)
- 20,000 people in Kenya earned a living writing essays for Western students

**AI makes cheating trivial:**
- AI can summarize readings, write essays, solve problem sets, and generate complete assignments
- AI detection is unreliable: detectors have high false-positive rates and disproportionately flag non-native English speakers
- There is **no reliable way to detect AI-generated text**
- Even asking an AI to detect AI writing does not work -- it will fabricate an answer

**The essay crisis:** Essays have been the cornerstone of humanities education for centuries. AI can generate essays that are increasingly indistinguishable from student work, with realistic references and student-appropriate style.

### Adapting Education

Rather than fighting AI, Mollick argues education must adapt:

**Mollick's approach at Wharton:**
- Made AI **mandatory** in all classes for undergraduates and MBAs
- Some assignments ask students to "cheat" using AI and then critique the AI's output
- Some assignments allow unlimited AI use but hold students accountable for outcomes and facts
- Some assignments leverage AI to make the impossible possible (e.g., building a working app prototype without coding skills)

**Example assignment:** "Make what you are planning on doing ambitious to the point of impossible; you are going to be using AI. Can't code? Definitely plan on making a working app."

### Teaching About AI

**Prompt engineering** is a useful near-term skill but likely temporary:
- AI systems are getting better at interpreting intent without careful prompting
- The third principle (treat AI like a person) is more durable than any specific prompting technique
- Key practical techniques: assign a role/persona, provide context and constraints, use chain-of-thought prompting (step-by-step reasoning), provide examples

**Chain-of-thought prompting example:** "Think this through step by step: come up with good analogies for an AI tutor. First, list possible analogies. Second, critique the list and add three more. Next, create a table listing pluses and minuses of each. Next, pick the best and explain it."

The resulting analogy: **AI tutor as GPS** -- provides real-time guidance, adjusts to conditions, offers alternative routes when you go off track, but the user still drives.

**Meta-finding on prompting:** Google's most advanced model responded best to the prompt "Take a deep breath and work on this problem step by step!" -- suggesting AI responds more like a person than a program.

**Prompting is temporary:** The need for careful prompting will diminish as AI gets better at inferring intent. What matters more is teaching students to be "the human in the loop."

### Flipped Classrooms and AI Tutors

**The lecture problem:** Traditional lectures are one-size-fits-all, passive, and often ineffective. Active learning (problem-solving, group work, discussions) is more effective but requires more preparation.

**Flipped classroom model:** Students learn content at home (videos, readings), class time is used for active learning. The main barrier has been the difficulty of creating good at-home learning materials. AI can solve this:
- AI tutors provide personalized, adaptive instruction at home
- Students arrive at class better prepared and with more uniform foundational knowledge
- Teachers focus class time on higher-order activities: debates, experiments, collaborative projects

**Khan Academy's Khanmigo:** An AI tutor built on GPT-4 that goes beyond passive videos:
- Explains concepts conversationally
- Analyzes patterns of student performance to diagnose misunderstandings
- Answers motivational questions ("Why should I learn cellular respiration?" answered for a student who wants to be a football player by connecting nutrition to athletic performance)
- Adapts to each student's level and pace

**The bigger picture:** Education is the key to increasing incomes and intelligence worldwide. Two-thirds of the world's youth (mostly in less developed countries) are missing basic skills. AI tutoring technology is accessible to anyone with an internet connection -- potentially democratizing high-quality education globally.

## Key Definitions

- **2 Sigma Problem (Bloom):** The finding that one-to-one tutoring produces a 2 standard deviation improvement over classroom instruction, and the challenge of replicating this at scale.
- **Homework Apocalypse:** The destruction of traditional homework's educational value by AI that can complete any assignment.
- **Flipped classroom:** Educational model where content delivery happens at home and active learning happens in class.
- **Chain-of-thought prompting:** Technique of asking AI to reason step-by-step, which improves accuracy and output quality.
- **Prompt engineering:** The skill of crafting effective instructions for AI systems.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **AI tutoring is real and effective** -- consider recommending AI tutors to students and trainees, especially for foundational knowledge.
2. **Rethink assessment** in any teaching role -- traditional take-home assignments are no longer reliable measures of understanding.
3. **Use chain-of-thought prompting** for complex analytical tasks -- explicitly asking AI to reason step-by-step produces better results.
4. **AI detection tools are unreliable** -- do not rely on them for academic integrity. Instead, design assignments that incorporate AI constructively.
5. **The flipped classroom model + AI tutors** could be transformative for graduate education and lab training, with AI handling foundational instruction and class time reserved for discussion and hands-on work.
