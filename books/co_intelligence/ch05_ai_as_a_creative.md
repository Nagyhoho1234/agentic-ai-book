# Chapter 5: AI as a Creative

## Summary

This chapter challenges the assumption that creativity is uniquely human. Mollick presents evidence that AI is already more creative than most humans on standard creativity tests, and explores what this means for creative work, art, and the meaning of human expression.

### Hallucination as the Paradox of AI Creativity

The same feature that makes AI unreliable for factual work -- hallucination -- is what makes it creative. LLMs are "connection machines" that find unexpected links between concepts in their training data. The randomness inherent in token generation produces novelty. This is the fundamental paradox: **the feature that makes AI dangerous for factual work makes it powerful for creative work.**

Key examples of hallucination:
- **The Schwartz case (2023):** Lawyer Steven Schwartz used ChatGPT to research legal precedents. The AI cited six completely fabricated court cases. Schwartz submitted these to the court without verification, resulting in sanctions.
- Hallucination rates are dropping (GPT-3.5 hallucinated 98% of citations; GPT-4 hallucinated only 20%), but the problem may never fully disappear.
- Small hallucinations are harder to catch than large ones because they are "completely plausible."

### AI Creativity on Standard Tests

**Alternative Uses Test (AUT):** Measures divergent thinking by asking for creative uses of common objects. Humans typically generate 5-10 ideas in two minutes. AI generated 122 ideas, many highly creative. In a study by Jennifer Haase and Paul Hanel, GPT-4 outperformed all but 9.4% of humans tested.

**Remote Associates Test (RAT):** Asks people to find a word connecting three unrelated words (e.g., pine, crab, sauce --> apple). AI maxes out this test as a "connection machine."

**Practical creativity:** In a Wharton innovation class, GPT-4 competed against 200 MBA students to generate product ideas costing $50 or less. Results: AI won decisively. Of the 40 best ideas rated by judges, 35 came from ChatGPT. AI generated more ideas, faster, and with higher commercial potential.

### The Limits of AI Creativity

- **Sameness problem:** Without careful prompting, AI tends to generate the same ideas repeatedly. A large group of creative humans will generate a wider *diversity* of ideas than AI.
- **The most innovative people benefit least** from AI creative help, because they already generate high-quality ideas on their own.
- **Originality questions:** It is often impossible to determine whether AI "ideas" are truly novel or recombinations of training data.
- **Copyright:** It is unclear whether AI outputs are protectable by copyright, and AI may reproduce copyrighted styles.

### Adding AI to Creative Work

Mollick presents research showing dramatic productivity gains when AI is applied to creative work:
- **MIT study (Noy and Zhang):** Workers using ChatGPT completed writing tasks 37% faster with higher quality. The entire grade distribution shifted upward. AI particularly helped lower performers, reducing inequality.
- **Microsoft coding study:** AI increased programmer productivity by 55.8%. AI can even turn non-programmers into coders.
- **Financial analysis (University of Chicago):** ChatGPT outperformed specialized machine learning models at predicting stock price volatility from conference call transcripts, because it could apply general world knowledge to contextualize risks.
- **Medical Q&A (JAMA Internal Medicine):** ChatGPT-3.5 was almost 10x more likely to be rated "very empathetic" and 3.6x more likely to be rated as providing good-quality information compared to human doctors.

### The Meaning of Creative Work

The chapter's most provocative section addresses the existential question: what does it mean when AI can write better than most people, create art, and generate ideas more effectively?

**The Button:** Every email client and office application will soon include a button to generate AI drafts. This creates a crisis of meaning:
- If writing a letter of recommendation takes effort, it signals genuine investment in the student. AI makes it effortless and potentially better -- destroying the signal.
- Performance reviews, strategic memos, grant applications -- all "signal" tasks where the effort is the point, not just the output.
- The MIT study found most participants used ChatGPT as a **substitute** for their own work, not a complement. They pasted questions directly and submitted AI answers with minimal editing.

**Art and Expression:** Mollick shares his personal experience with Midjourney (AI art), describing genuine creative fulfillment despite not being a visual artist. He argues:
- Only 31% of people feel they are living up to their creative potential
- AI democratizes creative expression, giving new modes of expression to people who lacked traditional skills
- Writers are the best prompt engineers because they understand how to describe desired effects in language
- Humanities knowledge becomes more valuable, not less, because deep cultural knowledge helps create better AI prompts

### Reconstructing Meaning

Technologies have always disrupted creative industries:
- Musicians shifted from records to live performance when recording threatened their livelihood
- Photography didn't kill painting -- it freed artists from realism, enabling Impressionism and abstract art
- Spreadsheets didn't eliminate accountants -- they changed what accountants do

AI will similarly force a reconstruction of meaning in creative work. Some tasks will be revealed as "mere ceremony" -- previously useful tasks made meaningless. Others will shift from output-focused to process-focused.

## Key Definitions

- **Hallucination:** AI generating false information presented as fact. The flip side of creativity.
- **Alternative Uses Test (AUT):** Psychological test measuring divergent thinking ability.
- **Remote Associates Test (RAT):** Test of creative association -- finding connections between unrelated concepts.
- **The Button:** Mollick's term for the ubiquitous AI draft-generation feature that will appear in all software.
- **Equal-odds rule:** The finding that creative quality is correlated with quantity -- people who generate more ideas also generate better ideas.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Use AI for brainstorming** -- ask it to generate many ideas, expect most to be mediocre, but look for the gems. Explicitly ask for "weird" or "unusual" ideas to push past AI's tendency toward average outputs.
2. **AI is excellent for first drafts** of grants, abstracts, cover letters, and other formulaic writing -- but be aware this may anchor your thinking to AI's initial framing.
3. **AI summarization is powerful but risky** -- it can synthesize literature quickly but may hallucinate citations. Always verify.
4. **The biggest creative gains go to those with lower baseline skills** -- if writing is not your strength, AI can be transformative.
5. **Humanities knowledge is an asset** -- understanding context, culture, and narrative helps you get better outputs from AI.
