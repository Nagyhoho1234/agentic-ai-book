# Chapter 3: Four Rules for Co-Intelligence

## Summary

Mollick presents four practical principles for working with AI, born from extensive personal experimentation and research. These are framed as actionable rules rather than abstract guidelines.

### Principle 1: Always Invite AI to the Table

Every task, every project, every problem -- try using AI. The reason is pragmatic: the Jagged Frontier of AI capability is unknown and constantly shifting. You cannot predict what AI will be good at without testing it. Mollick describes this as developing an intuition for where AI falls on its capability frontier.

This principle is especially important because:
- AI capabilities are expanding rapidly
- What failed yesterday may work today
- Opportunities are invisible unless you actively look for them
- The cost of trying is minimal (a few minutes) while the cost of missing an application could be significant

### Principle 2: Be the Human in the Loop

AI makes mistakes -- hallucinations, biases, confabulations. Humans must remain the quality control mechanism. But being "in the loop" means more than just checking outputs:
- You need enough expertise to evaluate whether AI output is correct
- You need to understand the domain well enough to spot plausible-sounding errors
- The more AI automates, the more critical human oversight becomes
- There is a danger of "falling asleep at the wheel" -- becoming complacent as AI quality improves

### Principle 3: Treat AI Like a Person (but Tell It What Kind of Person It Is)

This is a practical prompting strategy, not a philosophical claim. LLMs respond to context and persona assignment because they are trained on human text. When you tell the AI "you are an expert in X," it generates text from the probability distribution of how an expert in X would write. Key techniques:
- Assign the AI a role: "You are a senior data analyst..."
- Give it context about the audience and purpose
- Be specific about the style and level of detail you want
- This works because the model was trained on texts written by people in various roles

### Principle 4: Assume This Is the Worst AI You Will Ever Use

Current AI models will be surpassed rapidly. The GPT-4 of today will seem primitive compared to what comes next. This principle has important implications:
- Don't dismiss AI based on current limitations -- they will improve
- Don't build long-term strategies assuming current failure modes will persist
- Invest in learning how to work with AI now, because the returns will compound
- Current AI already has impressive capabilities despite being at an early stage

### The Jagged Frontier Explained

The chapter elaborates on this key concept: AI capability is not a smooth spectrum from easy to hard. It is irregular and unpredictable:
- AI can write a convincing legal brief but cannot reliably count the letters in a word
- It can analyze complex datasets but may hallucinate basic facts
- It can generate creative marketing copy but struggle with simple logical puzzles
- The frontier shifts with each model update, making it a moving target

## Key Definitions

- **Jagged Frontier:** The irregular, unpredictable boundary of AI capabilities -- AI excels at some hard tasks while failing at some easy ones.
- **Human in the loop:** The practice of maintaining human oversight and judgment in AI-assisted workflows.
- **Hallucination:** When AI generates plausible but false information with apparent confidence.
- **Status quo bias:** The human tendency to prefer the current state of affairs, which can prevent adoption of beneficial AI applications.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Test AI on every research task** -- literature reviews, data analysis, hypothesis generation, grant writing, peer review preparation. The only way to learn the frontier is to probe it.
2. **Never accept AI output uncritically** -- especially for factual claims, citations, and quantitative results. Always verify.
3. **Use persona prompting** -- tell the AI what kind of expert it should be when generating outputs. "You are a senior geophysicist reviewing a manuscript..." will produce better results than a generic query.
4. **Don't dismiss AI based on today's failures** -- build the habit of working with AI now, because tomorrow's models will be dramatically better.
