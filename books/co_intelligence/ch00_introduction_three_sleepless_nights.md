# Introduction: Three Sleepless Nights

## Summary

Ethan Mollick, a professor at Wharton who studies innovation and entrepreneurship, describes three sleepless nights that marked his transformation from AI skeptic to AI evangelist.

**Night 1 (Late November 2022):** The release of ChatGPT. As someone who had spent years studying technology and innovation, Mollick recognized immediately that this was different from previous AI waves. ChatGPT reached 100 million users faster than any technology in history. Unlike prior AI that was narrowly focused (prediction systems, recommendation engines), LLMs were general-purpose -- they could write, analyze, code, and create across virtually any domain.

**Night 2 (March 2023):** The release of GPT-4. This represented a massive leap -- moving from a system that was impressive but flawed to one that was close to human-level on many professional and academic tests (bar exam, medical licensing, etc.). Mollick and his wife, Dr. Lilach Mollick, had been experimenting constantly and had already published some of the first research on AI in education and work.

**Night 3:** The realization that AI is a General Purpose Technology (GPT) -- a category that includes steam power, electricity, and the computer. These technologies transform entire economies, but their effects take decades to fully manifest. Steam power improved productivity 18-22%, but it took 50 years. Computers still have difficulty showing real long-term productivity impact (the Solow Paradox). AI may follow similar patterns -- or it may move much faster.

## Key Concepts

- **General Purpose Technology (GPT):** A technology that transforms entire economies and societies, not just individual sectors. AI qualifies because it applies to nearly every domain of human activity.
- **Solow Paradox:** Robert Solow's observation that "you can see the computer age everywhere but in the productivity statistics" -- new technologies often take decades to show measurable economic impact.
- **Co-Intelligence:** Mollick's framing of AI not as a replacement for human intelligence but as a partner -- a co-intelligence that works alongside humans.

## Key Arguments

1. AI is not just another tech tool -- it is a fundamentally new kind of technology that simulates human cognitive abilities.
2. The speed of AI adoption is unprecedented -- reaching 100 million users in weeks rather than years.
3. We are in an early, uncertain period where the technology is advancing faster than our ability to understand its implications.
4. Everyone needs to engage with AI now, not wait for it to stabilize, because the pace of change is too rapid.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- AI is a General Purpose Technology that will affect every field, including scientific research.
- The historical pattern of GPTs suggests transformative but slow-to-materialize productivity gains -- but AI may break this pattern due to its speed of adoption.
- Early engagement with AI tools is essential; waiting means falling behind as the technology evolves exponentially.
