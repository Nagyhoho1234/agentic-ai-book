# Chapter 6: AI as a Coworker

## Summary

This is the most data-rich chapter of the book, presenting Mollick's own research on AI's impact on professional work. It introduces critical frameworks for understanding how AI will change jobs, tasks, and organizations.

### Jobs, Tasks, and Systems

Mollick makes a crucial distinction:
- **Jobs** are bundles of **tasks**
- Tasks fit into larger **systems** (organizations, industries, regulations)
- AI affects tasks, not entire jobs -- at least initially
- The systems in which jobs operate are resistant to change, slowing AI's impact on employment

Research by Ed Felten, Manav Raj, and Rob Seamans found that AI overlaps most with the most highly compensated, highly creative, and highly educated work. College professors are among the top 20 most AI-overlapping jobs. Only 36 out of 1,016 job categories had no AI overlap -- mostly highly physical jobs (dancers, athletes, roofers, motorcycle mechanics).

### The BCG Study: The Jagged Frontier in Action

Mollick's most important empirical contribution, conducted with Harvard social scientists Fabrizio Dell'Acqua, Edward McFowland III, Karim Lakhani, Hila Lifshitz-Assaf (Warwick), and Katherine Kellogg (MIT), with Boston Consulting Group:

**Setup:** ~800 BCG consultants randomized into AI (GPT-4) and non-AI groups. 18 realistic consulting tasks spanning creative, analytical, writing, and persuasion tasks.

**Results for tasks inside the Jagged Frontier:**
- AI-assisted consultants were significantly faster and produced higher quality work
- Measured 118 different ways; effect persisted across all analyses
- AI-powered consultants were considered more creative, better written, and more analytical

**The critical finding -- tasks outside the Frontier:**
- BCG designed one task requiring a tricky statistical judgment with misleading data
- Without AI: consultants got it right 84% of the time
- With AI: consultants got it right only 60-70% of the time
- **AI made them worse** because they trusted the AI and stopped thinking critically

**The "falling asleep at the wheel" phenomenon:**
- Most consultants simply pasted questions into AI and submitted the output with minimal editing
- Powerful AI made people lazy, careless, and less skilled in their own judgment
- Dell'Acqua's mathematical model: when AI is very good, humans have no reason to work hard, which hurts learning and skill development

### Task Categories

Mollick proposes a taxonomy of tasks based on how they should be handled with AI:

1. **Just Me Tasks:** Tasks where AI is not useful or where you choose to keep AI out:
   - Tasks AI currently does badly (e.g., humor, certain types of reasoning)
   - Tasks you believe should remain human for ethical/personal reasons (e.g., raising children, making important decisions, expressing values)
   - Writing this book was largely a Just Me Task -- the AI could approximate Mollick's style but couldn't capture his personal voice

2. **Delegated Tasks:** Tasks you assign to AI and check the results:
   - Tedious, repetitive, or boring tasks that are easy for humans but time-consuming
   - Expense reports, email sorting, scheduling, routine summarization
   - You still verify outputs but don't want to spend significant time on the task
   - Example: Mollick delegated summarizing a colleague's research paper to AI, made significant edits, and saved ~30 minutes

3. **Automated Tasks:** Tasks fully handled by AI without human oversight:
   - Currently a small category due to AI error rates
   - Spam filtering is an example of successful automation
   - As AI improves, more tasks will move into this category
   - Early AI agents (like BabyAGI) are primitive but point toward future automation

### Centaurs and Cyborgs

Two models for human-AI collaboration:

**Centaurs** (named after chess centaur teams): Clear division of labor between human and AI. The human does what humans do best, the AI does what AI does best. Like the mythical centaur -- distinct human and horse parts.
- Example: Using AI to generate graphs while the human decides on statistical methodology
- Strategic task allocation based on known strengths and weaknesses

**Cyborgs:** Deeply integrated human-AI collaboration where the two are intertwined throughout the workflow. No clear dividing line between human and AI contributions.
- Example: Starting a sentence and having AI complete it, then editing, then asking AI for alternatives
- Mollick's own writing process for this book: using AI personas (Ozymandias for critical feedback, Mnemosyne for creative connections, Steve for everyperson perspective) to improve his work

### Secret Task Automation and Organizational Challenges

A fascinating sociological finding: many workers are secretly using AI and not telling their employers. Three reasons:
1. **Company bans:** Many companies (J.P.Morgan, Apple) banned ChatGPT. Workers use personal devices to circumvent bans.
2. **Value comes from secrecy:** AI writing is powerful only if people think it came from a human. Over half of AI users in an informal poll reported hiding their usage.
3. **Fear of replacement:** If you automate 90% of your job and tell your boss, will they fire 90% of your coworkers?

### Organizational Recommendations

1. **Recognize AI power users** at all levels -- they are not necessarily senior leaders or IT staff
2. **Reduce fear** by committing to not using efficiency gains solely for layoffs
3. **Incentivize AI innovation sharing** with substantial rewards (cash prizes, promotions, flexibility)
4. **Restructure systems** -- without changing organizational systems, AI benefits remain hidden

### From Tasks to Systems to Jobs

**Historical parallel:** AT&T telephone operators in the 1920s -- 15% of all American women worked as operators. Direct dialing eliminated 50-80% of operator jobs. Young workers adjusted quickly; experienced operators took long-term earnings hits.

**Key insight:** Two-thirds of economists expect AI to have little effect on overall employment in the next few years, even as it transforms tasks. Systems change more slowly than tasks. But some industries (stock photography, call centers) will face rapid disruption.

**The Great Equalizer:** Across multiple studies, AI's biggest benefit goes to the lowest performers. In the BCG study, the gap between top and bottom performers shrank from 22% to 4% when consultants used GPT-4. This has profound implications for the value of expertise, education, and training.

## Key Definitions

- **Jagged Frontier:** The irregular boundary of AI capabilities (introduced in Ch. 1, empirically demonstrated here).
- **Just Me Tasks:** Tasks kept human by choice or necessity.
- **Delegated Tasks:** Tasks assigned to AI with human verification.
- **Automated Tasks:** Tasks fully handled by AI without oversight.
- **Centaur work:** Human-AI collaboration with clear task division.
- **Cyborg work:** Deeply integrated human-AI collaboration.
- **Falling asleep at the wheel:** Becoming complacent and uncritical when AI is very capable, leading to worse outcomes.
- **Shadow IT / Secret automation:** Workers using AI tools without organizational knowledge or approval.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Identify your tasks** -- list what you do and categorize each as Just Me, Delegated, or Automated. Start delegating the boring ones.
2. **Be a Cyborg for writing** -- use AI to get unstuck, generate alternatives, and get feedback, but maintain your own voice and judgment.
3. **Watch for "falling asleep at the wheel"** -- the better AI gets, the more dangerous it becomes to trust it uncritically. This is especially important for data analysis and statistical reasoning.
4. **AI levels the playing field** -- junior researchers and those weaker in writing/analysis benefit most. This has implications for lab management and mentoring.
5. **Organizations that embrace AI openly will outperform those that ban it** -- the AI will be used anyway, but in secret.
