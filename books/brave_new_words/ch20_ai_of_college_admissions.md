# The AI of College Admissions

## Part VIII: AI, Assessments, and Admissions

## Comprehensive Summary

This chapter examines how AI will transform college admissions -- a process Khan argues is already deeply flawed. The classic admissions components are grades, standardized tests, extracurriculars, essays, and letters of recommendation. AI will change how most or all of these are valued, developed, and evaluated.

Khan discusses the problem of essays and recommendations in the age of LLMs. Students can now generate impressive-sounding essays with ChatGPT. But Khan points out that wealthy students have always had access to expensive admissions coaches ($400/hour in Silicon Valley) who essentially write essays for them. AI has simply democratized what was previously a privilege of affluence. The Varsity Blues scandal is cited as the extreme version of this inequality.

Khan questions whether essays and recommendations should remain central to admissions at all. In most countries (e.g., India's IIT system), admission is based solely on objective test scores. In the U.S., the subjective process leads to randomness and bias. He cites a 2018 Supreme Court case revealing that Harvard admissions officers consistently rated Asian American applicants lower on "personal" traits, overruling alumni interviewers who rated them comparably to white applicants.

Khan proposes a radical alternative: AI-based interviews and assessments for college admissions. Instead of static essays, AI could conduct extensive text- or voice-based interviews with students, guidance counselors, and teachers. The AI would be aware of the student's academic record and could dig deep into their experiences, motivations, and personality. This process would be:

- **Scalable:** Every applicant gets the same depth of interview.
- **Consistent:** Unlike human interviewers, AI applies the same standard every time.
- **Auditable:** The system can be tested with identically qualified applicants of different demographics to check for bias.
- **Transparent:** Results and methodology can be published for scrutiny.

Khan describes how Schoolhouse.world transcripts are already being accepted by 18 universities as a more reliable indicator of competence and character than traditional applications. AI can write recommendation letters based on years of interaction data, creating standardized but personalized letters that are difficult to fake.

## Key Definitions

- **Varsity Blues Scandal:** A college admissions bribery case where wealthy parents paid to fabricate extracurricular records and cheat on standardized tests.
- **AI-Based Admissions Interview:** A hypothetical system where AI conducts standardized, in-depth interviews with applicants, producing consistent and auditable assessments.
- **Auditable AI:** AI systems designed so that their decision-making can be tested, verified, and checked for bias by external parties.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- AI-based interviewing could transform graduate admissions, postdoc hiring, and fellowship selection, making these processes more consistent and less biased.
- The auditability argument is powerful: any AI system used for selection should be testable for bias with synthetic applicants of different demographics.
- Current admissions and hiring processes are already deeply biased (as Harvard case shows). AI offers a path to less bias, not more -- if properly designed and audited.
- Mastery-based credentials (like Schoolhouse.world transcripts) may become increasingly important alongside traditional degrees for evaluating research talent.
