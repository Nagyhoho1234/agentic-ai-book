# Alternative Education Models and Fixing Cheating in College

## Part VI: The Teaching in the Age of AI

## Comprehensive Summary

This chapter addresses two major topics: how AI enables alternative education models (homeschooling, pod schools, hybrid models) and how AI can actually help solve the cheating problem in higher education.

**Alternative Education:** Khan notes that roughly 3 million parents homeschool their children in the U.S., with the number rising. Common concerns include socialization, subject matter expertise, time, and proving mastery to colleges. AI addresses several of these: Khanmigo can serve as a subject matter tutor, provide real-time feedback on writing, act as a guidance counselor for college admissions, and report to parents on exactly what their child has learned.

Khan describes Schoolhouse.world, a platform providing free, live, small-group tutoring via Zoom. Top universities (University of Chicago, MIT, CalTech, Brown, Yale, Georgia Tech, Ohio State, USC, Columbia) now accept Schoolhouse.world transcripts for admissions, recognizing them as evidence of mastery. AI is being used to give volunteer tutors feedback on their sessions and will eventually provide real-time coaching tips.

"Pod schoolers" -- groups of families pooling resources to collectively educate their children -- can leverage AI and platforms like Khan Academy as building blocks without reinventing everything from scratch.

**Fixing Cheating in College:** Khan describes the academic integrity crisis. A 2021 *Inside Higher Ed* survey found 47% of students think it's acceptable to use websites for test and homework answers. At Middlebury College, 2/3 of students admitted breaking the honor code. Stanford reversed its anti-proctoring policy in 2023-24 because cheating had become so pervasive.

Khan argues AI can actually solve cheating by making the process transparent. In Khanmigo, professors can create assignments with grading rubrics and have students complete work through the AI platform. The AI monitors the writing process and can report to the professor: "Sal worked on this essay for five hours. He had trouble with his thesis statement but I helped nudge him. I'd give it a B+." Conversely, if a student pastes in pre-written text: "We worked together for five minutes. The paper seemed pre-written and pasted in. The writing level is significantly more advanced than his classwork."

Khan argues this approach is superior to honor codes because it provides transparency, rapid feedback, and actual learning support simultaneously.

## Key Definitions

- **Pod Schooling:** Groups of families pooling resources to collectively educate their children, often using a mix of online platforms, AI tools, and shared instruction.
- **Schoolhouse.world:** Khan-founded platform providing free, live, small-group peer tutoring via Zoom, with transcripts accepted by 18+ universities.
- **AI-Proctored Writing:** A system where AI monitors the writing process to verify authenticity, detect plagiarism patterns, and report to instructors on student engagement.
- **Honor Code Crisis:** The breakdown of academic integrity systems in higher education, exacerbated by AI tools but predating them.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- AI transparency in the writing/research process is valuable: having AI track and document the research or writing process provides evidence of authentic work.
- The Khanmigo model of AI-proctored writing could be adapted for thesis and dissertation supervision: the AI documents the student's process and provides evidence of genuine engagement.
- Alternative credentialing (like Schoolhouse.world transcripts) is expanding. Consider how mastery-based, AI-verified credentials might complement traditional degrees in your field.
- Cheating predates AI. Banning AI does not solve the underlying integrity problem; transparency and process-based assessment do.
