# Economics of AI in Education

## Part VII: The Global Classroom

## Comprehensive Summary

This chapter examines the economic realities of AI-powered education. Khan provides context: the U.S. spends roughly $10,000-$40,000 per student per year depending on the state; India spends $500-$1,200. Despite this range, the fundamental model is the same everywhere -- lockstep progression through curricula with limited personalization.

Khan discusses the $86 billion in U.S. federal emergency relief funds for K-12 education post-pandemic, much of which went to live tutoring programs that ultimately showed limited results. In hindsight, experts believe the tutoring was not connected to classroom learning and was logistically difficult for students to access. Khan contrasts this with Khan Academy's results: classrooms using the platform for as little as 30-60 minutes per week during the pandemic not only avoided the COVID slide but outperformed pre-COVID standards by 20-40%, at zero cost to students.

Khan addresses the cost of AI computation. Running Khanmigo costs between $5-$15 per user per month in compute alone. With millions of users, the total cost runs into tens of millions of dollars annually. As a nonprofit, Khan Academy relies on philanthropy, school district partnerships, and the expectation that compute costs will drop dramatically (by a factor of 100) within 5-10 years.

Khan discusses the digital divide issue: AI tutoring requires internet access and devices, which remain unavailable to many students globally. He expresses optimism that satellite internet (SpaceX's Starlink) and declining device costs will address this within the coming decade.

A key insight: AI can operate in every major language, removing the language barrier that limited earlier online education. An English-language learner can get math tutoring support in their native language while learning in English. The LLM's conversational abilities make it feel like a real interaction, fostering connection even in a text-based medium.

## Key Definitions

- **COVID Slide:** The pandemic-era decline in student academic performance due to school closures and disrupted instruction.
- **Compute Cost:** The cost of running AI models, measured per user per month. Currently $5-$15 for Khanmigo, expected to drop 100x in 5-10 years.
- **Education ROI:** The social return on investment from educational platforms, which Khan argues is orders of magnitude higher than traditional reform efforts.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- AI computation costs are declining rapidly. Tools that seem expensive today will be essentially free within a decade -- plan accordingly.
- The cost-effectiveness argument for AI-assisted training applies to research: AI tutoring for trainees costs far less than human tutoring while being available 24/7.
- Multilingual AI capabilities are immediately useful for international research collaborations and training programs.
- The failure of the $86 billion tutoring investment illustrates the importance of integration: AI tools must be connected to existing workflows (classroom or lab) to be effective.
