# The Teaching in the Age of AI

## Part VI: The Teaching in the Age of AI

## Comprehensive Summary

Part VI turns to how AI affects the teaching profession. Khan opens with a reference to Isaac Asimov's Foundation series and its character Hari Seldon, who predicted that machines would eventually teach. Khan explicitly disagrees: AI is not here to replace teachers but to be their teaching assistant.

The chapter features prominently the views of Wharton professor Ethan Mollick, who identifies three things teachers must do differently in the age of generative AI:

1. **Expect more from students.** Since AI raises the baseline quality of student work (just as word processors raised the baseline quality of formatting), teachers should raise their expectations accordingly.

2. **Integrate AI into assignments.** Rather than banning AI, Mollick requires his students to use generative AI to review and critique their work. Students must do a "pre-mortem" using AI to identify how their projects might fail, then work backward to prevent those failures.

3. **Flip all classrooms.** Since AI can do what lectures do (deliver content on demand), class time should be used for discussion, collaboration, and hands-on work -- activities that require human presence.

Khan discusses Doug Engelbart's work on augmenting human capabilities with technology (the computer mouse inventor), drawing a parallel to AI augmenting rather than replacing teachers. The central argument: no job is safer in the LLM world than teaching, because AI will support teachers while making their work more sustainable and impactful.

The chapter addresses the dichotomy between useful and harmful technology, arguing that the fear teachers feel is real but misplaced. The AI is the teaching assistant; teachers are irreplaceable because students need human connection, mentorship, and inspiration.

## Key Definitions

- **Flipped Classroom (AI-enhanced):** A teaching model where AI and video handle content delivery outside class, freeing class time for Socratic dialogue, collaborative assignments, and supported student work.
- **Pre-mortem (AI-assisted):** A technique where students use AI to anticipate how their project might fail before completion, then work to prevent those failures.
- **Augmentation vs. Replacement:** Doug Engelbart's framework arguing that technology should augment human capabilities rather than replace humans.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- Raise expectations when AI tools are available. If trainees have access to AI writing assistants, expect clearer, better-structured manuscripts.
- The "pre-mortem" technique is directly applicable to research: use AI to stress-test experimental designs, proposals, and manuscripts before submission.
- The flipped model works for research group meetings: have trainees engage with AI-delivered background material beforehand, then use meeting time for discussion and problem-solving.
- Teaching is safe from AI displacement, and so is scientific mentoring -- but both must evolve to leverage AI's capabilities.
