# Employment in an AI World

## Part IX: Work and What Comes Next

## Comprehensive Summary

Part IX turns to the future of work. Khan opens with the common fear that AI will cause mass layoffs. He cites IBM's 2023 announcement that it was suspending or slowing back-office hiring by 30% over five years for jobs that AI could do. The signal is clear: back-office and middle-office jobs (budgets, data management, records) are disappearing.

Khan discusses productivity gains: writers and copywriters using AI can be 3-5x more productive; software engineers using AI copilots write code faster; graphic designers can make 50 variations of a logo by tweaking text prompts. The question is: will we need as many of these workers?

Khan's answer is nuanced. For engineers, demand will likely increase because AI creates new software-building opportunities (just as globalization in the 2000s, which was supposed to kill engineering jobs through outsourcing, actually increased demand for domestic engineers). For routine writers (news summaries, stock market updates), 90% may need to find new work. But generative AI will also create entirely new job categories (prompt engineers, AI safety specialists, etc.).

Khan presents the case study of Chegg, the education technology company that was disrupted by ChatGPT. Chegg had nearly 3 million customers for homework help, textbook rentals, and online tutoring. When ChatGPT arrived, students abandoned Chegg. The company's stock plummeted 50% after admitting in May 2023 that it could not compete with OpenAI's free offering. This is "AI-induced natural selection" playing out in real time.

Khan quotes Ethan Mollick: "The job that's least affected by AI is roofing, and yet I've talked to roofers who say AI now does all their proposals." The implication is that AI affects every industry, though differently.

Khan describes AI-induced natural selection at the corporate level: smaller, AI-leveraged companies will outcompete larger, human-dependent ones, leading to net job losses even if the products are better and cheaper. He acknowledges this will cause real pain for displaced workers.

## Key Definitions

- **AI-Induced Natural Selection:** The process by which AI-leveraged organizations outcompete and displace less efficient competitors, including at the corporate level.
- **Centaur Model:** The idea of combining human and AI capabilities, where workers are "half human and half large language model" -- using AI to augment rather than replace their skills.
- **General-Purpose Technology:** A technology that transforms many sectors simultaneously (steam power, electricity, internet, now generative AI).

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- AI-powered productivity gains of 30-80% on white-collar analytical tasks (per Wharton studies) are directly relevant to scientific work: writing, analysis, consulting, and programming.
- The Chegg case study is a warning: established institutions that do not adapt to AI will be disrupted by those that do. This applies to universities, publishers, and research organizations.
- The "centaur model" -- combining human expertise with AI capabilities -- is the optimal approach for researchers. Neither pure AI nor pure human work will be competitive.
- New roles are emerging (AI safety, prompt engineering) that represent opportunities for scientists with domain expertise.
