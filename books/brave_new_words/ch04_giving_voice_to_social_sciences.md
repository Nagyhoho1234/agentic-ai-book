# Giving Voice to the Social Sciences

## Part II: Giving Voice to the Social Sciences

## Comprehensive Summary

Part II opens by confronting a fundamental question: can AI add value to subjects that are inherently about human interpretation, nuance, and debate? Khan argues yes, and this chapter focuses on how AI can transform the teaching of reading, writing, and the humanities.

Khan presents data showing that 54% of Americans read below a sixth-grade level, costing the US economy $2.2 trillion annually (per a 2020 Gallup analysis). He argues that AI tutoring could dramatically improve literacy by providing every struggling reader with a patient, adaptive reading partner.

The chapter describes how Khanmigo handles writing instruction. Rather than writing essays for students (as raw ChatGPT does), Khanmigo acts as a writing coach: helping students brainstorm thesis statements, providing feedback on outlines, asking questions about argument structure, and offering revision suggestions. Crucially, teachers can see the entire student-AI interaction, making the process transparent.

Khan also addresses the "Chomsky critique" -- Noam Chomsky's argument that LLMs do not truly understand language and therefore cannot genuinely teach it. Khan's response is pragmatic rather than philosophical: regardless of whether the AI "understands" in a human sense, the empirical question is whether students learn better with AI support, and early evidence suggests they do.

The chapter discusses how AI can make book reports and literary analysis more engaging by allowing students to "converse" with literary characters or debate themes with the AI, transforming passive reading into active intellectual engagement.

## Key Definitions

- **Functional illiteracy:** Reading below the level required to navigate daily life (approximately a sixth-grade level). Affects 54% of American adults.
- **AI writing coach:** An AI system designed to guide students through the writing process rather than writing for them, providing feedback on structure, argument, clarity, and style.
- **Process transparency:** Making the student-AI interaction visible to teachers, enabling assessment of the learning process rather than just the final product.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **AI as writing coach for scientific papers:** The Khanmigo writing coach model could be adapted for scientific manuscript preparation -- helping with argument structure, identifying logical gaps, and suggesting revisions while keeping the researcher in control.
2. **Process over product:** The emphasis on transparent process (not just the final output) has direct implications for how scientific training handles AI-assisted writing. Advisors could review student-AI interactions to assess genuine understanding.
3. **Literacy is a scientific workforce issue:** The 54% functional illiteracy rate means many potential scientists are lost before they reach higher education. AI-based literacy intervention could expand the scientific talent pool.
4. **The Chomsky critique is worth understanding:** Debates about whether AI "truly understands" are relevant to how much scientists should trust AI-generated analyses, hypotheses, or interpretations.
