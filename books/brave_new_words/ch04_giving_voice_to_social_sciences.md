# Giving Voice to the Social Sciences

## Part II: Giving Voice to the Social Sciences

## Comprehensive Summary

Part II opens by addressing the widespread perception that AI is most useful for STEM subjects. Khan argues that AI may actually have its most transformative impact on the humanities and social sciences -- subjects that revolve around language, interpretation, and critical thinking.

Khan describes how AI can transform reading comprehension and literary analysis. Instead of passively reading a novel and answering multiple-choice questions, students can now have a conversation with AI about the text. Khanmigo can role-play as literary characters -- a student can literally "talk to" Jay Gatsby from *The Great Gatsby* and ask him about his motivations, or debate Atticus Finch about justice.

The chapter explores how AI changes writing instruction. Khan argues that AI should not write essays for students, but it can be an excellent writing coach -- helping students brainstorm topics, develop outlines, strengthen arguments, and refine prose. He cites a 2020 Gallup analysis showing illiteracy costs the U.S. economy $2.2 trillion annually, underscoring the importance of improving literacy education.

Khan addresses Noam Chomsky's critique that LLMs are "basically high-tech plagiarism" and "a way of avoiding learning." Khan disagrees, arguing that when properly guided, AI forces students to engage more deeply with texts rather than less. The AI asks students to justify their interpretations with evidence, think about author intent, and consider multiple readings -- exactly what good humanities instruction should do.

The chapter also discusses how AI handles the U.S. Department of Education's concerns about quality and equity in language arts education. Only about a third of American students read at grade level, and this has worsened since the pandemic. AI tutoring can provide the individualized reading support that most schools cannot afford.

## Key Definitions

- **Character Simulation:** AI role-playing as literary or historical figures, allowing students to have conversations with characters from texts they are studying.
- **Writing Coach (AI):** An AI that guides students through the writing process -- brainstorming, outlining, drafting, revising -- without writing the essay for them.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- AI can engage with qualitative, interpretive work, not just quantitative tasks. Consider using it for literature reviews, argument analysis, and exploring alternative interpretations of your findings.
- The "conversation with a character" approach can be adapted for research: have AI role-play as a skeptical reviewer to stress-test your arguments before submission.
- AI writing coaching is applicable to scientific writing: use it for brainstorming, outline development, and argument structure rather than raw text generation.
- The ability to assess textual reasoning at scale has implications for peer review and research training.
