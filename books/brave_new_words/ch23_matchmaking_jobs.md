# Matchmaking Between Job Seekers and Employers

## Part IX: Work and What Comes Next

## Comprehensive Summary

Khan describes a vision for AI-powered employment matching that goes far beyond current job boards and resume screening. He begins with a personal anecdote: in 1999, as a young Oracle product manager, he predicted in a *Computerworld* cover story that we would all have AI personal agents in "cyberspace" that would broker transactions and matchmake between employers and employees. Twenty-three years later, generative AI is making those predictions look conservative.

Khan describes the current hiring process and its failures. For every job opening at Khan Academy, they receive hundreds of resumes. Screeners spend a few seconds on each, focusing on brand names (employers, universities, job titles). Great candidates from non-traditional backgrounds are systematically overlooked. The interview process is expensive ($600+ per candidate for six one-hour interviews), inconsistent, and biased by interviewer mood and preferences.

Khan's vision of the future: every job seeker has an AI agent that knows their entire history, skills, interests, and career goals. Every employer has an AI recruiter that understands the role. These AI agents have rich conversations with each other, finding matches that neither human recruiters nor human job seekers would have identified. The job seeker's agent could read literally every job posting globally and find the 1% of employers willing to take a chance on a career switcher.

During live interviews, the AI recruiting assistant could whisper follow-up questions to the interviewer, provide real-time feedback on interviewing fairly and consistently, and generate auditable summaries.

Khan acknowledges the bias concern: AI screening and interviewing could introduce bias. But he argues this is testable and auditable in a way that human bias is not. You can run synthetic candidates with different demographics through the system and publish the results. The goal is not a bias-free system (which may be impossible) but a demonstrably less biased system than the status quo.

## Key Definitions

- **AI Job Agent:** A personal AI that represents a job seeker, knows their complete history and goals, and communicates with employer AI recruiters on their behalf.
- **AI Recruiter:** An employer-side AI that screens candidates through rich conversations rather than resume scanning, applying consistent standards.
- **Interactive Resume:** A dynamic AI-powered interface that replaces static resumes, engaging with employer AI systems through conversation.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

- AI-powered matching could transform how postdocs find positions, how labs find collaborators, and how scientists find interdisciplinary partners.
- The auditability argument applies to academic hiring: AI-assisted screening could be tested for bias in ways that committee reviews cannot.
- Consider how an "AI agent" could represent your research group to potential recruits, giving every candidate a rich, consistent interaction rather than a quick resume scan.
- The current hiring process in academia (like everywhere else) is biased, inconsistent, and often misses excellent candidates. AI matching offers a path to improvement.
