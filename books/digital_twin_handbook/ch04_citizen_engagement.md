# Chapter 4 -- Digital Twins for Citizen Engagement

**Authors:** Andrew S. Wilson and Adrian Walker

## Summary

This chapter presents a detailed case study of "Virtual Bradford" -- an open digital twin of the City of Bradford, UK, co-developed by the University of Bradford and the City of Bradford Metropolitan District Council. It demonstrates how DT technology can serve as a vehicle for citizen engagement, heritage conservation, urban planning, and inclusive data-driven decision-making. The chapter is deeply practical, grounded in a real multi-year partnership between academia and local government, and emphasises FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) and open-source methods.

The work originated from the EU-funded SCORE (Smart Cities Open Data Re-use) project and has since expanded to cover 100 km of streets at LoD1-LoD3 detail levels, incorporating Ordnance Survey data, aerial/drone photogrammetry, LiDAR, and BIM models of proposed developments. The chapter also describes replication efforts in Saltaire World Heritage Site, coastal cities in Tanzania, and Irish heritage towns.

## Key Concepts and Architectures

### Virtual Bradford Platform
- **Technology stack:** ESRI ArcGIS Online (AGOL), ArcGIS Experience Builder, 3D Analyst; supplemented by photogrammetry (Agisoft Metashape), mobile mapping (Leica TRK 700 EVO), and immersive visualisation (iGloo shared immersion).
- **Data sources:** Ordnance Survey mapping, Blue Sky tree data, drone-captured photogrammetry, LiDAR point clouds, BIM models from architects.
- **Level of Detail (LoD):** Ranges from LoD1 (extruded building footprints) to LoD3 (detailed facade geometry).

### Citizen Engagement Approaches
1. **Government-backed initiatives:**
   - Heritage asset capture and digital preservation.
   - Blue-green infrastructure documentation (Horton Park life-critical project).
   - Countryside and Rights of Way trail content creation (Saltaire to Canal Road Greenway).
   - PropTech schemes for inclusive planning consultation.
2. **Humanities-led place-based research:**
   - Alignment with UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs 9 and 11).
   - Health improvement pathways through heritage interpretation.
   - Queen's Anniversary Prize-winning work in heritage technology.
3. **Data-driven decision-making:**
   - Bradford Digital Strategy (2022) embedding the DT for clean growth planning.
   - Simulation of development scenarios (energy, carbon, transport).
   - Southern Gateway regeneration planning using rapid 3D capture.

### FAIR Principles in Practice
- Open data, open models, copyright-free where possible.
- Capacity building within the local authority to maintain and extend the DT independently.
- Replication methodology designed for transfer to other local authorities.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Partnership model matters** -- the Bradford case shows that success depends on a genuine, long-term partnership between academia and local government, not a one-off project.
2. **Open-source and FAIR-compliant approaches reduce costs and increase longevity** -- proprietary lock-in is a major risk for DT projects with limited budgets.
3. **Start with existing data** -- Ordnance Survey, satellite imagery, and open government data can provide a usable baseline without expensive custom data capture.
4. **Citizen engagement requires meeting people where they are** -- workshops, walking trails with AR content, and interactive web platforms each reach different demographics.
5. **Heritage and cultural applications of DTs can unlock non-traditional funding sources** (Arts & Humanities Research Council, Heritage Lottery Fund, EU cultural programmes).
6. **The incremental development model** (start with city centre, expand outwards) is more sustainable than attempting city-wide coverage from day one.
