# Chapter 2 -- Towards a Maturity Model for Digital Twins

**Authors:** Chapter contributors (academic/industry collaboration)

## Summary

This chapter addresses a critical gap in DT adoption: the lack of a structured way to assess how mature a DT implementation is. The authors develop a comprehensive maturity model grounded in a systematic literature review and design science methodology. The model synthesises existing DT reference architectures and maturity frameworks from both DT-specific and general digital maturity literature into a novel nine-dimensional framework with eight operationalisation sub-dimensions per layer, yielding a 72-item measurement instrument.

The chapter begins with a working definition of DTs, surveys 11 influential maturity model papers, identifies the gap (most models lack an operationally applicable measurement tool), and then constructs both a conceptual maturity model and a survey-based measurement model that organisations can directly use.

## Key Concepts and Architectures

### Nine-Dimensional Reference Architecture (Maturity Layers)

1. **Business model** -- Strategy, revenue models, market positioning for DT products/services.
2. **System security** -- Data privacy, cybersecurity posture, governance of security measures.
3. **Deployment** -- Strategies and architectures for deploying DT components at scale.
4. **Data models and algorithms** -- Integration of AI/ML, data modelling approaches, algorithm maturity.
5. **Data preparation and representation** -- Data structures, formats, cleaning, and transformation pipelines.
6. **Data storage** -- Storage strategies, technologies, scalability of data infrastructure.
7. **Networks and connectivity** -- Network architecture, IoT connectivity, bandwidth and latency management.
8. **Data generation** -- Sensor strategies, data acquisition, source definition and quality.
9. **Physical entities** -- Selection and augmentation of physical objects with sensors and actuators.

### Eight Operationalisation Sub-Dimensions (per layer)

For each of the nine layers above, maturity is assessed across:
- **S** -- Strategy
- **L** -- Leadership
- **M** -- Market
- **O** -- Operations
- **P** -- People
- **C** -- Culture
- **G** -- Governance
- **T** -- Technology

### Measurement Model

- 72 survey items (9 layers x 8 dimensions), each rated on a Likert scale.
- Can be used for holistic organisation-wide assessment or targeted assessment of individual architectural layers.
- Designed for self-assessment by management teams.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **Use the 9-layer checklist before starting a DT project** -- it forces you to think beyond just the technology layer and consider business models, people, governance, and security.
2. **Maturity is not just about technology** -- the framework reveals that leadership buy-in, organisational culture, and governance structures are equally important dimensions.
3. **The 72-item questionnaire (Table 2.5)** can be adapted as a readiness assessment tool for any DT initiative in a research lab or institution.
4. **Start by identifying which layer is your bottleneck** -- most scientific DT projects stall at "data preparation and representation" or "deployment," not at the conceptual modelling stage.
5. The conceptual model from Table 2.4 provides a comprehensive checklist for writing DT project proposals or grant applications.
