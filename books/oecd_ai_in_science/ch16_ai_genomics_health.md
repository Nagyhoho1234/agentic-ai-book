# AI for Genomics and Health

**Authors:** Multiple contributors
**Pages:** 153-162

## Summary

This essay surveys AI applications in genomics and health sciences, covering gene expression analysis, variant calling, genome interpretation, protein function prediction, electronic health record analysis, and personalised medicine. AI has become essential for making sense of the vast datasets generated by modern sequencing technologies.

Key applications include: variant effect prediction (determining which genetic variants cause disease), gene regulatory network inference, drug-gene interaction prediction, patient stratification for clinical trials, and AI-assisted diagnosis from medical images and genomic data.

The essay discusses both the enormous promise and the significant challenges. On the promise side, AI can identify patterns in genomic data that are invisible to human analysis and can integrate multiple data types (genomic, transcriptomic, clinical) for more accurate predictions. On the challenge side, genomic data are complex, high-dimensional, and often lack standardised formats. Privacy and ethical concerns are particularly acute in health-related applications.

## Key Policy Recommendations

- Health data governance frameworks must balance the need for data access (to train AI models) with privacy protection and patient consent.
- Standardisation of genomic and clinical data formats is essential for effective AI applications in health.
- Public investment in genomic databases and biobanks should be maintained and expanded.
- Regulatory frameworks for AI-assisted clinical decision-making should be developed.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists and Institutions

- AI is most effective in genomics when applied to well-curated, standardised datasets; data quality matters more than data quantity.
- Privacy-preserving techniques (federated learning, differential privacy) can enable AI training on sensitive health data without centralising it.
- Clinicians and genomic scientists need training in AI methods to effectively use and evaluate AI-generated predictions.

## Notable Data and Statistics

- The UK Genome Implementation Plan (2021-2022) sets out a roadmap for integrating genomic data into clinical practice.
- AI models can predict the pathogenicity of genetic variants with accuracy comparable to expert panels in some cases.
- The volume of genomic data is doubling approximately every 7 months.
