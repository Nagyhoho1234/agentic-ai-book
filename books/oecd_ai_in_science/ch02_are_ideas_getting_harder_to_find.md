# Are Ideas Getting Harder to Find?

**Authors:** N. Bloom, C. Jones, J. Van Reenen, M. Webb (Stanford University and others)
**Pages:** 51-56

## Summary

This landmark essay presents evidence that research productivity -- measured as the ratio of economic growth or idea output to the number of researchers -- has been declining sharply across a wide range of sectors and scientific fields. The central finding is that the same rate of economic or technological progress now requires substantially more researchers and resources than it did decades ago.

The authors examine multiple case studies, including Moore's Law in semiconductors, agricultural crop yields, and medical research. In all cases, they find that maintaining a constant rate of progress requires exponentially increasing research effort. For example, the number of researchers required to double chip density (Moore's Law) has increased by a factor of 18 since the 1970s.

The paper implies that without AI or other productivity-enhancing tools, the rate of scientific and technological progress will either slow down or require ever-larger teams and budgets -- a pattern that cannot continue indefinitely.

## Key Policy Recommendations

- Governments should recognise that maintaining historical rates of progress will require either dramatically increased R&D spending or new tools (like AI) that amplify researcher productivity.
- Research funding models should account for the rising cost of generating new ideas.
- AI and automation represent a potential countervailing force against declining research productivity.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists and Institutions

- Individual researchers should expect to specialise in ever-narrower domains and collaborate in larger teams.
- Institutions should invest in tools and methods that increase research efficiency, including AI-assisted literature review, automated experimentation, and data analytics.
- The declining productivity of research has implications for career planning: high-impact breakthroughs increasingly require large, well-funded, interdisciplinary teams.

## Notable Data and Statistics

- Research productivity in the US economy has declined by a factor of 41 since the 1930s.
- The number of researchers needed to achieve Moore's Law has increased 18-fold since the 1970s.
- Across multiple sectors, the "ideas production function" shows strongly diminishing returns to research effort.
