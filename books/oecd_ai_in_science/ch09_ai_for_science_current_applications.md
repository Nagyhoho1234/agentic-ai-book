# AI for Science: Overview of Current Applications

**Source:** Overview chapter by A. Nolan, OECD
**Pages:** 25-36 (from overview)

## Summary

This section of the overview chapter surveys AI's current applications across scientific disciplines. It provides a panoramic view of how AI -- particularly machine learning and deep learning -- is being deployed in real scientific research today, highlighting both successes and limitations.

Key application areas include:

**Drug discovery:** AI is used to identify drug candidates, predict molecular properties, optimise lead compounds, and design clinical trials. Companies like Insilico Medicine have used AI to bring drug candidates from concept to clinical trials in record time.

**Protein structure prediction:** DeepMind's AlphaFold solved the protein folding problem, predicting the 3D structures of over 200 million proteins. This has been called the most important AI contribution to science to date.

**Materials science:** AI accelerates the discovery of new materials by screening vast combinatorial spaces of possible compositions and structures. Applications include battery materials, catalysts, and superconductors.

**Mathematics:** AI has contributed to new mathematical conjectures, helped prove theorems, and assisted in discovering previously unknown mathematical structures.

**Particle physics:** AI is used at facilities like CERN for event classification, anomaly detection, and data reduction in experiments that generate petabytes of data annually.

**Climate science:** AI improves climate models by learning parameterisations of sub-grid-scale processes that are too complex for explicit mathematical modelling.

**Genomics:** AI aids in gene expression analysis, variant calling, protein function prediction, and personalised medicine.

## Key Policy Recommendations

- Public research funding should actively prioritise AI applications in scientific domains where progress has been slower (e.g., social sciences, environmental monitoring).
- Interdisciplinary collaboration between AI researchers and domain scientists should be systematically supported.
- Open-source AI tools and pre-trained models adapted for scientific use should be developed as public goods.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists and Institutions

- AI is most effective in scientific domains with large datasets, clear evaluation metrics, and combinatorial search spaces.
- Scientists should look for opportunities to apply AI even in fields not traditionally associated with computation -- the tools are increasingly accessible.
- Pre-trained models and transfer learning can reduce the data and compute requirements for applying AI in new scientific domains.

## Notable Data and Statistics

- AlphaFold predicted structures for over 200 million proteins.
- Exascale supercomputers can perform 10^18 floating-point operations per second.
- Major scientific facilities (CERN LHC, Square Kilometre Array) generate petabytes of data per year, creating an urgent need for AI-assisted analysis.
- The US NSF has established 18 National AI Research Institutes across 40 states.
