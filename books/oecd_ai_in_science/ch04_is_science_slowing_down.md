# Is Science Slowing Down?

**Author:** S. Milojevic, Indiana University, United States
**Pages:** 65-73

## Summary

This essay examines whether science is genuinely slowing down by looking at the growth rate of novel scientific concepts and ideas rather than simply counting publications. Milojevic argues that while the number of publications has grown exponentially, the number of genuinely new scientific ideas has expanded only linearly. This means the ratio of new ideas to papers (and researchers) is declining.

The analysis uses a large-scale computational approach to identify new scientific concepts appearing in the literature over time. The key finding is a divergence between the exponential growth of publications and the linear growth of new concepts, suggesting that a growing share of publications represents incremental contributions rather than novel discoveries.

Milojevic also examines whether this pattern varies across fields, finding that while some fields are more productive than others, the overall trend toward diminishing novelty per paper is broadly consistent.

## Key Policy Recommendations

- Science policy should distinguish between the quantity of publications and the quality/novelty of ideas. Simply increasing the number of publications does not guarantee scientific progress.
- AI tools that help identify genuinely novel ideas and under-explored research directions could help reverse the trend.
- Funding agencies should consider metrics beyond publication counts when evaluating research productivity.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists and Institutions

- Researchers should be aware that the pressure to publish can lead to a proliferation of incremental work.
- AI-assisted tools for mapping the knowledge landscape and identifying gaps could help researchers find more productive research directions.
- Institutions should reward genuinely novel contributions rather than publication volume.

## Notable Data and Statistics

- The number of scientific publications has been growing exponentially, but the number of novel scientific concepts grows only linearly.
- The ratio of new ideas per paper has been declining across most fields.
- The pattern is consistent across natural sciences, social sciences, and engineering.
