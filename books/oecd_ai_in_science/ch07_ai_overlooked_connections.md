# Can AI Help Find Overlooked Connections Between Research Papers?

**Author:** N. Smallheiser, University of Illinois at Chicago, United States
**Pages:** 87-92

## Summary

This essay builds on the concept of "undiscovered public knowledge" -- the idea that important connections between existing findings in different fields remain unnoticed because no single researcher reads across all relevant literatures. Smallheiser describes how AI and NLP techniques can be used to systematically mine the scientific literature for such overlooked connections.

The classic example is Swanson's 1989 discovery of a link between fish oil and Raynaud's disease through purely literature-based reasoning, connecting papers in two separate fields that were never cited together. Modern AI tools can automate this kind of literature-based discovery at scale.

The essay describes approaches including word vector analysis, knowledge graph construction, and hypothesis generation from cross-corpus text mining. These methods have already produced testable hypotheses in fields ranging from materials science to biomedicine.

## Key Policy Recommendations

- Funding agencies should support research on AI-assisted literature-based discovery and the development of tools for cross-disciplinary hypothesis generation.
- Open access to scientific literature and standardised metadata are prerequisites for effective literature mining.
- Policy makers should recognise that some of the most valuable scientific insights may already exist in the literature, waiting to be connected.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists and Institutions

- Researchers should explore literature-based discovery tools to identify connections between their work and findings in other fields.
- The growing fragmentation of scientific knowledge makes cross-disciplinary tools increasingly valuable.
- Institutions should encourage cross-disciplinary reading and collaboration, supported by AI tools that make this practical.

## Notable Data and Statistics

- Swanson's literature-based discovery approach has been validated multiple times since 1989.
- Word vector analyses of the materials science literature have successfully predicted previously unknown thermoelectric materials.
- The volume of scientific literature makes manual cross-disciplinary synthesis increasingly impossible without AI assistance.
