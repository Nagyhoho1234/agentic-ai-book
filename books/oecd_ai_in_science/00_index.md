# OECD: Artificial Intelligence in Science -- Challenges, Opportunities and the Future of Research (2023)

## Book Overview

**Publisher:** OECD Publishing, Paris
**Year:** 2023
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1787/a8d820bd-en
**Editor:** Alistair Nolan (OECD Directorate for Science, Technology and Innovation)

This volume brings together 29 essays from leading researchers, policy makers and practitioners to examine how artificial intelligence is transforming science and what this means for public policy. The contributions cover AI's current uses in scientific research, the challenges and limitations of AI methods, near-future directions, and the policy implications for governments and research institutions.

## Key Themes Across the Book

1. **Science is getting harder** -- Breakthrough ideas require larger teams, more money, and longer timescales; disruptive papers are declining as a share of all publications.
2. **AI is already transforming many scientific fields** -- Drug discovery, materials science, protein folding, particle physics, genomics, climate science, and mathematics are all seeing significant AI applications.
3. **Current AI has serious limitations for science** -- Black-box models, lack of causal reasoning, data scarcity in many scientific domains, bias, reproducibility problems, and the absence of true "understanding."
4. **Policy interventions are needed** -- Governments should invest in AI infrastructure for science, ensure equitable access to compute, support open data/models, diversify AI research beyond deep learning, and reform incentive structures.
5. **The human-AI collaboration model** -- The most productive path forward combines human scientific insight with AI analytical power, not full automation of science.
