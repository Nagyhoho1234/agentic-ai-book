# AI for Mathematics

**Author:** G. Williamson
**Pages:** 121-128

## Summary

This essay explores the emerging use of AI in pure and applied mathematics. While mathematics has traditionally been considered resistant to AI (because it requires abstract reasoning, proof construction, and deep conceptual understanding), recent developments have shown that AI can make genuine contributions.

Key applications include: generating conjectures by identifying patterns in mathematical data (e.g., DeepMind's collaboration with mathematicians that led to a new conjecture in representation theory and a result in knot theory published in Nature); automated theorem proving using reinforcement learning and neural networks; and AI-assisted exploration of large mathematical spaces.

The essay emphasises that AI's role in mathematics is currently best understood as a collaborator rather than a replacement for human mathematicians. AI excels at pattern detection and exhaustive search but struggles with the creative, conceptual, and explanatory aspects of mathematics.

## Key Policy Recommendations

- Funding agencies should support interdisciplinary research between AI and mathematics.
- Mathematical datasets and benchmarks for AI should be developed as community resources.
- The potential for AI to accelerate mathematical research should be communicated to the broader mathematical community.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists and Institutions

- Mathematicians should explore AI tools for conjecture generation and pattern detection, even in abstract fields.
- AI-assisted theorem proving is advancing rapidly and may soon be practical for routine mathematical proofs.
- The most productive use of AI in mathematics is as a "conjecture engine" that generates candidates for human mathematicians to evaluate and prove.

## Notable Data and Statistics

- DeepMind's collaboration with mathematicians led to a new conjecture in representation theory and a result in knot theory (published in Nature, 2021).
- Automated theorem provers can now handle some undergraduate-level proofs but struggle with research-level mathematics.
