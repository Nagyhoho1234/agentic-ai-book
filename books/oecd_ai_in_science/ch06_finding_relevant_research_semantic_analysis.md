# Finding Relevant Research More Efficiently with Semantic Analysis

**Authors:** I. Rafols and colleagues
**Pages:** 81-86

## Summary

This essay explores how AI-powered semantic analysis tools can help researchers navigate the exploding volume of scientific literature. Traditional keyword-based search has significant limitations: it requires researchers to know the right terminology, misses relevant work that uses different vocabulary, and cannot easily identify cross-disciplinary connections.

Semantic analysis approaches use NLP and machine learning to understand the meaning of text, enabling searches that go beyond keyword matching. These tools can identify papers that address similar problems or methods even when they use completely different terminology, making cross-disciplinary discovery much more feasible.

The essay describes several practical applications, including automated literature mapping, research trend detection, identification of emerging fields, and tools that help funders and policy makers understand the landscape of research in a given area.

## Key Policy Recommendations

- Funding agencies should invest in the development and deployment of open-access semantic analysis tools for the research community.
- Standards for metadata and open access to research publications are essential for making semantic analysis effective at scale.
- Policy makers should use semantic analysis tools to better understand the research landscape when making funding decisions.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists and Institutions

- Researchers should explore semantic search tools as complements or alternatives to traditional keyword-based search.
- Cross-disciplinary researchers stand to benefit most from semantic analysis, which can uncover connections between fields that share methods or problems but not vocabulary.
- Institutions should support open access to publications and standardised metadata to enable more effective semantic analysis.

## Notable Data and Statistics

- More than 4,000 biomedical papers are published daily, making comprehensive manual literature review impossible.
- Semantic analysis tools can identify relevant papers that keyword searches miss, especially across disciplinary boundaries.
