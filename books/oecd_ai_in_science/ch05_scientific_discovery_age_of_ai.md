# Scientific Discovery in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

**Author:** H. Kitano, Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology, Japan
**Pages:** 74-80

## Summary

Hiroaki Kitano proposes the concept of a "Nobel Turing Challenge" -- the goal of developing an AI system capable of making a Nobel Prize-worthy scientific discovery autonomously. He argues that science needs a grand challenge to focus AI research toward genuinely transformative scientific discovery, rather than incremental improvements on existing benchmarks.

Kitano outlines a hierarchy of AI capabilities relevant to science: from AI as a tool for data analysis, through AI as a research assistant, to AI as a hypothesis generator and potentially as an autonomous scientist. He emphasises that current AI, while powerful at pattern recognition and optimisation, falls far short of the reasoning, abstraction, and creativity required for transformative discoveries.

The essay also discusses the role of AI in accelerating the scientific process by automating routine tasks, synthesising literature, and generating hypotheses for human scientists to evaluate. Kitano stresses that even if fully autonomous discovery remains distant, AI can dramatically accelerate human-led science.

## Key Policy Recommendations

- Governments and research organisations should establish grand challenges for AI in science, analogous to the Human Genome Project or CERN, to focus effort and resources.
- Funding should support high-risk, high-reward research on AI for autonomous scientific discovery, not just incremental applications.
- International collaboration on AI for science should be encouraged to pool resources and talent.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists and Institutions

- Scientists should view AI as a tool that can amplify their creativity and productivity, not replace their judgment.
- High-impact scientific problems that are well-suited to AI (large data, clear metrics, combinatorial search spaces) should be prioritised for AI-assisted research.
- Research institutions should invest in the infrastructure (data, compute, interdisciplinary teams) needed to pursue AI-driven scientific discovery.

## Notable Data and Statistics

- AlphaFold solved the 50-year-old protein folding problem -- a result described by Nobel laureate Venki Ramakrishnan as "stunning."
- The combinatorial search space for protein conformations is approximately 10^300.
- The essay proposes the "Nobel Turing Challenge" as a new organising goal for AI research in science.
