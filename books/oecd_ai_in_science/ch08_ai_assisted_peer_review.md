# AI-Assisted Peer Review

**Authors:** L. Li and N. Aggarwal
**Pages:** 93-100

## Summary

This essay examines how AI tools are being applied to assist the peer review process, which is increasingly strained by the rapid growth in publication volume. AI applications in peer review include automated matching of papers to reviewers, detection of statistical errors or methodological weaknesses, plagiarism detection, and assessment of scientific claims.

The essay discusses both the potential benefits (faster, more consistent review; reduced bias in reviewer selection; detection of errors that humans miss) and the risks (algorithmic bias, lack of transparency in automated decisions, potential for gaming, and the danger of displacing human judgment on questions that require deep expertise).

The authors note that AI is unlikely to replace human peer reviewers but can augment the process by handling routine checks and flagging potential issues. They emphasise that the peer review system must maintain trust and transparency, and that AI tools should be used to support -- not replace -- expert human judgment.

## Key Policy Recommendations

- Publishers and funding agencies should experiment with AI-assisted peer review tools while maintaining robust safeguards against bias and gaming.
- Transparency is essential: authors and reviewers should know when and how AI tools are used in the review process.
- Funding should support research on the effectiveness and fairness of AI-assisted peer review.
- Standards and best practices for the use of AI in peer review should be developed collaboratively across the research community.

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists and Institutions

- Authors should be aware that AI tools are increasingly used in the review process, including for plagiarism detection, statistical checking, and reviewer matching.
- Reviewers can benefit from AI tools that help identify relevant prior work, check statistical claims, or flag methodological issues.
- Institutions should invest in training reviewers on the capabilities and limitations of AI-assisted review tools.

## Notable Data and Statistics

- The volume of scientific submissions has grown far faster than the pool of available reviewers, creating a "review crisis."
- AI tools for reviewer matching (e.g., at the National Natural Sciences Foundation of China) have been lauded for speed but criticised for potential bias.
- Few publishers currently provide standardised instructions or checklists for reviewers, though AI conferences are an exception.
