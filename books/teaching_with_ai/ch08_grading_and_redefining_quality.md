# Chapter 8: Grading and (Re-)Defining Quality

## Summary

This chapter argues that AI fundamentally changes what constitutes quality student work and that grading standards must be raised accordingly. The central claim: "AI is the new C work." A Harvard sophomore tested ChatGPT on her freshman assignments and professors gave the AI output a 3.34 GPA -- solid but average. The implication is clear: if AI can produce C-level work, then human students must aim higher, and our rubrics and grading standards must reflect this new baseline.

The authors present a powerful framework: rather than banning AI, ban C-level work. They propose "AI-proof rubrics" where the standards that previously constituted passing (clear thesis, some evidence, basic organization, correct grammar) now represent the AI-equivalent baseline and should receive an F (50%) or below. The chapter includes a detailed writing rubric (Table 8.1) that redefines each quality dimension with AI as the new floor: a focused essay with some evidence and simple sentence structures is now the AI-level standard, not the human passing standard.

The chapter also explores AI's potential role in grading itself. The authors tested multiple AI tools on rubric creation and found all could produce usable rubrics. Grading by AI is more complex -- Claude initially refused, while GPT-4 attempted but warned it could not evaluate against detailed rubrics. The authors see potential but caution about accuracy, consistency, privacy, and ethical concerns. They recommend using AI to assist grading (first pass, consistency checks) rather than fully automating it.

Key to the chapter is the idea that grading should increasingly focus on process rather than product, and on higher-order Bloom's taxonomy levels. The Oregon State revised Bloom's taxonomy distinguishing AI capabilities from human ones by cognitive level provides a useful framework: AI is good at remembering and summarizing but less capable of original insight, creative synthesis, and authentic voice.

## Key Pedagogical Strategies

- **Raise the bar**: Redefine passing standards so that AI-level work does not pass
- **Create AI-adjusted rubrics**: Make the AI baseline explicit in rubrics (Table 8.1)
- **Grade process, not just product**: How students arrived at their work matters as much as the final output
- **Focus on higher-order thinking**: Prioritize original insight, voice, and creativity in grading
- **Use AI to help design rubrics**: AI can generate initial rubric drafts that you then refine

## Practical Takeaways for University Faculty

1. Generate an AI response to your own assignment prompt -- this is now the baseline your students must exceed
2. Share the AI baseline with students: "Here is what AI produced. Your job is to do better. Here is how."
3. Revise rubrics to make the old C-level criteria the new F-level, and articulate what genuinely excellent (human-added-value) work looks like
4. Use the Oregon State revised Bloom's taxonomy to identify which cognitive levels AI can reach in your discipline
5. Consider grading both the product and the process -- require students to document their journey
6. Use AI to assist with grading consistency checks, but maintain human oversight and final judgment
7. Discuss with students why AI-level work is no longer sufficient -- this is about their employability, not just grades
