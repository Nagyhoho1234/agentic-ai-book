# Epilogue

## Summary

The epilogue places the current AI moment in historical perspective, comparing it to the early days of internet search engines (Altavista, Lycos, Ask Jeeves, WebCrawler). The tools are rapidly evolving, and the current landscape may soon consolidate around a few dominant platforms, with major players like Apple still entering the field. The authors invoke Moore's Law to argue that AI will only get faster, cheaper, and more capable.

The authors pose a series of forward-looking questions that higher education must confront:

**Institutional transformation**: What campus functions (advising, admissions, financial aid, security) could change with AI? Who will decide? Will AI become a dependable tool for predicting student success? Could AI deliver and assess courses?

**Classroom evolution**: What will real-time AI analysis of classroom interactions mean for teaching? Can or should we ban AI from classrooms? Will students receive AI coaching throughout the day -- reminders about assignments, study advice, grade predictions?

**Academic integrity**: As AI becomes better at mimicking different writing voices and styles for different audiences, detection will become even less reliable. Policies and practices should embrace this reality rather than fighting it.

**Ethics and society**: Curriculum must immediately include AI topics around ethics, data privacy, civics, environmental and energy costs, labor, discrimination, and bias. These are not just computer science topics -- they belong in every discipline.

The epilogue's central argument: all future thinking will be done *with* AI. The tension between content (what to think) and process (how to think) has always existed in education, but as AI provides unprecedented access to content, the balance must shift decisively toward process. The new future of higher education is teaching students how to think with AI.

The authors close with an optimistic but urgent call to action: AI offers higher education an opportunity to reflect and reimagine how we could most benefit our students, our society, and our planet. This is a place where higher education can lead, but only if faculty engage now.

## Key Themes

- We are in the very early phase of AI adoption -- the tools will get dramatically better
- AI detection will become even less reliable as AI improves
- The shift from content to process in education must accelerate
- Ethics, bias, privacy, and environmental costs must be integrated across all curricula
- AI literacy is becoming as essential as digital literacy
- Higher education has an opportunity to lead the conversation about responsible AI use

## Practical Takeaways for University Faculty

1. Start experimenting with AI now -- waiting for it to stabilize means falling behind permanently
2. Prepare students for a world where AI collaboration is expected, not optional
3. Integrate AI ethics discussions into your courses regardless of discipline
4. Expect your AI policies to evolve continuously -- build in flexibility
5. Focus your teaching on process and critical thinking -- these are the durable skills in an AI-augmented world
6. Higher education can lead society in developing thoughtful, ethical approaches to AI -- this is both an opportunity and a responsibility
