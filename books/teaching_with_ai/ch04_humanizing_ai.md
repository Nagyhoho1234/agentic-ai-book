# Chapter 4: Humanizing AI

## Summary

This chapter explores the surprisingly human-like qualities of AI and their implications for education. The authors present research showing that AI can demonstrate empathy, emotional intelligence, and even creativity in ways that challenge assumptions about the uniqueness of human cognition. A striking finding: in a study comparing physician and chatbot responses to patient questions, chatbot responses were rated significantly higher for both quality and empathy (Morrow et al., 2023). People rated AI-written messages as more empathetic, warmer, and more professional than human-written ones.

The chapter examines AI creativity through the lens of semantic distance -- the ability to make unexpected connections between ideas. Research using the SemDis platform found that AI can produce outputs with high semantic distance, a hallmark of creative thinking. While humans still outperform AI on the most creative divergent thinking tasks (Koivisto & Grassini, 2023), AI can serve as a powerful creativity partner, suggesting unexpected combinations and perspectives.

A central framework in this chapter is Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) -- the gap between what a learner can do alone and what they can do with assistance. The authors argue that AI functions as an ideal Vygotskian scaffold: infinitely patient, always available, adjustable in complexity, and non-judgmental. This has profound implications for equity, as AI can provide the kind of individualized tutoring and feedback that was previously available only to students with access to private tutors or well-resourced families.

The chapter also addresses anthropomorphism -- the tendency to attribute human qualities to AI. While AI is not sentient or conscious, its human-like qualities can be pedagogically useful when properly understood. The authors caution against both over-humanizing AI (treating it as a person) and under-appreciating its capabilities.

## Key Pedagogical Strategies

- **Leverage AI as a Vygotskian scaffold**: Use AI to provide individualized support in the Zone of Proximal Development
- **Use AI for empathetic feedback**: AI can deliver critique in ways students find less threatening than human feedback
- **Harness AI creativity**: Use AI as a brainstorming partner to generate unexpected connections and perspectives
- **Discuss anthropomorphism**: Help students understand that AI's human-like qualities are mimicry, not sentience, while still being useful

## Practical Takeaways for University Faculty

1. AI can be a great equalizer -- it provides first-generation and under-resourced students access to patient, individualized tutoring
2. AI-generated feedback can be perceived as less threatening, potentially increasing student willingness to engage with critique
3. Use AI as a creativity catalyst -- have students brainstorm with AI, then evaluate and refine the most promising ideas
4. The emotional qualities of AI interactions matter: prompts that specify tone (kind, encouraging, challenging) significantly affect output quality
5. Do not dismiss AI's empathetic capabilities; leverage them while teaching students about the distinction between performed and felt empathy
