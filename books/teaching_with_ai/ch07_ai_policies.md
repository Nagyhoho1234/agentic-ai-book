# Chapter 7: AI Policies

## Summary

This chapter guides faculty and institutions through the complex process of developing AI policies, arguing that a thoughtful, transparent policy is essential even if it changes frequently. The authors present a spectrum of approaches ranging from complete prohibition to full integration, with most institutions and courses falling somewhere in between.

The chapter identifies five broad policy positions: (1) **No AI allowed** -- all work must be entirely human-produced; (2) **AI as editor only** -- AI can be used for proofreading and grammar but not content generation; (3) **AI with disclosure** -- AI can be used for specified purposes if fully documented; (4) **AI as collaborator** -- AI use is expected and integrated into assignments; (5) **AI-centered** -- assignments are designed around AI use with human value-added. The authors argue that most courses will need different policies for different assignments, and that a single blanket policy is usually insufficient.

A major theme is transparency. The authors insist that whatever policy faculty adopt, it must be clearly communicated to students, ideally with specific examples of what is and is not acceptable. They recommend including the AI policy in the syllabus with concrete scenarios, discussing it in class, and revisiting it as the semester progresses. They provide sample syllabus language at various levels of AI integration.

The chapter also addresses the syllabus more broadly, drawing on research about "warm" versus "cold" syllabi. Faculty who frame policies in terms of learning goals and student support (rather than punishment and surveillance) see better compliance and engagement. The authors recommend framing AI policy as "how we learn together" rather than "what you cannot do."

Institutional policies are also discussed: the authors survey how various institutions (Russell Group universities, individual US universities) have approached AI policy, noting the challenge of creating policies that are both specific enough to be useful and flexible enough to accommodate rapidly changing technology.

## Key Pedagogical Strategies

- **Be transparent**: Clear, specific communication about AI expectations reduces confusion and dishonesty
- **Differentiate by assignment**: Different tasks may warrant different AI policies within the same course
- **Frame positively**: Explain what students should do and why, rather than focusing on prohibitions
- **Revisit and adapt**: AI capabilities change rapidly; policies should be living documents
- **Start with learning goals**: Let the purpose of each assignment drive the AI policy for that assignment

## Practical Takeaways for University Faculty

1. Include explicit AI policy language in your syllabus with concrete examples -- vague policies invite confusion
2. Consider assignment-level policies rather than a single course-wide rule
3. Use a warm, supportive tone in policy language; explain the "why" behind restrictions
4. Start the semester with a discussion about AI -- have students help develop class norms
5. Provide a clear citation/disclosure format for AI use (e.g., what tool was used, what prompts were given, what was modified)
6. Be prepared to update policies mid-semester as you learn what works
7. "Sell the cookie, not the recipe" -- explain the benefits of learning, not just the rules
