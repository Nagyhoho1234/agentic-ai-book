# Chapter 12: AI Assignments and Assessments

## Summary

This chapter serves as a comprehensive catalog of AI-integrated assignment ideas, organized by category and ranging from simple modifications of existing assignments to entirely new AI-centered pedagogical approaches. The authors emphasize that all assignments are now "AI assignments" whether faculty acknowledge it or not, and the best new assignments will develop both critical thinking and AI literacy simultaneously.

The chapter organizes assignments into several major categories:

**Alternate Forms and Media**: Presentations (with AI assistance for outlines, anticipated questions, and Q&A practice), video and podcasts (AI tools for content creation), creative projects (graphic novels via AI Comic Factory, interactive fiction, game design), and visual/video demonstrations using image generators like Midjourney and DALL-E.

**Live and In-Person**: Co-teaching with AI (using AI in real-time during class), field research projects (collecting firsthand data AI cannot simulate), real-time challenges (responding to current events), mini-debates (oral defense emphasizing comprehension over text), and working with physical sources in libraries.

**Social and Peer Work**: Peer review, discussion boards (keeping prompts personally meaningful), shared documents and group writing (with tracked changes), collaboration projects, human vs. AI exercises, and social annotation tools (Hypothes.is, Perusall).

**Process Assignments**: Promoting explicit documentation of thinking, research methods, and writing process. Annotated reading, dynamic and scaffolded problems, signature work (complex real-world challenges), and customization at scale (using AI to personalize assignments for individual students).

**Discovery and Working with AI**: "What else?" assignments (iterating with AI to improve output), creative AI projects, study preparation, teaching AI (prompting AI to learn tasks), and betting against AI (comparing AI predictions with human analysis).

The chapter provides specific, implementable examples for each category, including prompt templates and assessment criteria. A key theme throughout: the goal is to make cheating harder and learning more effective simultaneously by emphasizing process, personal relevance, and human judgment.

## Key Pedagogical Strategies

- **Diversify assessment types**: Move beyond essays to presentations, debates, creative projects, and collaborative work
- **Integrate AI explicitly**: Design assignments where AI use is expected, documented, and evaluated
- **Emphasize process over product**: Grade the journey (transcripts, drafts, reflections) not just the destination
- **Make it personal and local**: Assignments tied to personal experience, local context, or real-time events resist AI completion
- **Use AI as a collaborator, not a substitute**: Students should iterate with AI, evaluate its output, and add human value

## Practical Takeaways for University Faculty

1. Start with one or two assignment modifications rather than overhauling everything -- small changes (adding an oral component, requiring version history) can be effective
2. Try assignments yourself with AI before assigning them -- know what AI can and cannot do with your specific prompts
3. Different AI tools have different strengths; consider recommending specific tools for specific assignments
4. Require transcripts of AI interactions as part of submissions -- this makes the process visible and gradable
5. Use AI to customize assignments at scale: create individualized problem sets, culturally relevant examples, or discipline-specific scenarios for different student groups
6. Consider collaborative assignments that use AI to mediate group dynamics, resolve disagreements, or ensure equal participation
7. Physical sources, in-person observations, interviews, and handwritten elements remain resistant to AI substitution
8. Frame AI assignments as preparation for the workplace: employers will expect graduates who can work effectively with AI
