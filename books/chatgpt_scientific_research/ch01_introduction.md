# Chapter 1: Introduction

## Summary

The introduction provides the context for the book by tracing the emergence of ChatGPT and its rapid adoption. Released on 30 November 2022 by OpenAI, ChatGPT reached 100 million users in just two months, making it the fastest-growing application in history. The book is positioned as a systematic evaluation of AI large language models -- specifically ChatGPT (GPT-3.5 and GPT-4) and the ChatGPT-enabled new Bing (now Copilot) -- for tasks routinely encountered in scientific research.

The authors, who are environmental scientists, conducted hands-on testing across multiple research workflow categories. They emphasize that this is a practical guide, not a technical AI textbook, written by scientists for scientists.

## Key Concepts

- **ChatGPT** is built on the Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT) architecture, a large language model (LLM) trained on vast amounts of text data.
- **GPT-3.5 vs GPT-4**: GPT-3.5 is freely available; GPT-4 requires a subscription (ChatGPT Plus, $20/month). GPT-4 generally performs better on complex tasks.
- **New Bing (now Copilot)**: Microsoft's integration of GPT-4 into the Bing search engine, which adds web search capability to the LLM, allowing it to access more recent information beyond the model's training cutoff.
- **Knowledge cutoff**: A critical limitation -- GPT-3.5's cutoff is September 2021; GPT-4's is also limited. New Bing partially addresses this through web search integration.
- **Three chat modes in new Bing**: "More Creative," "More Balanced," and "More Precise" -- the authors predominantly used "More Creative" mode for their tests.

## Key Definitions

- **Large Language Model (LLM)**: An AI model trained on large text corpora to generate human-like text responses.
- **Hallucination**: The tendency of LLMs to generate false or inaccurate information presented in a confident, authoritative tone.
- **Prompt**: The user's input text that guides the model's response.
- **Multimodal**: Ability to process both text and images (available in GPT-4 and new Bing).

## Practical Takeaways

- Scientists should view ChatGPT as an assistive tool, not a replacement for human expertise.
- The book tests 15 categories of tasks across the entire scientific workflow: from literature review and figure interpretation, to writing, peer review, experiment design, and science communication.
- Both free (GPT-3.5, new Bing) and paid (GPT-4) models are tested, making the guidance accessible to researchers with different budgets.
- All AI-generated outputs were validated by the authors and domain experts.
