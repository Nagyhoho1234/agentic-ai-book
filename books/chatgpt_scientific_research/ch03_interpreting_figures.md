# Chapter 3: Interpreting Figures and Correlating to Specific Conclusions

## Summary

This chapter tests the ChatGPT-enabled new Bing's ability to interpret complex scientific figures, including hyperspectral images and data plots. The "multimodal visual search" feature allows users to drag and drop images into the chat window for analysis.

The key finding is that the model can recognize the link between a figure, its caption, the surrounding discussion text, and the paper's conclusions. It interprets figures by correlating them to relevant text passages in the source document, producing accurate and contextually relevant interpretations. This is particularly valuable for understanding complicated figures in scientific publications that are not prepared in a "stand-alone" manner.

However, the model's interpretations are based on paraphrasing and synthesizing the text already present in the paper rather than independently analyzing raw data in the figures. The model was validated as "mostly correct" with some minor inaccuracies.

## Key Concepts

- **Multimodal visual search**: GPT-4's ability to process and interpret images alongside text, available through new Bing.
- **Stand-alone figures**: Figures that can be understood without reading the full text -- the authors note many scientific figures are NOT stand-alone, making AI interpretation valuable.
- **Figure-text correlation**: The model links figures to their captions, discussion sections, and conclusions to provide comprehensive interpretations.

## Key Findings

- The ChatGPT-enabled new Bing can quickly interpret complex scientific figures, hyperspectral images, and data plots.
- The model delves into the full text, correlates each figure to its description and the authors' interpretation, and identifies specific conclusions supported by the figure.
- Interpretations were validated as mostly correct, with some minor inaccuracies.
- Figures containing many results, complex concepts, or unexplained abbreviations are difficult for readers without the full text -- LLMs can bridge this gap.

## Practical Takeaways

- Use new Bing's multimodal capability to quickly understand complex figures without reading entire papers.
- The model works best when it has access to both the figure and the full text of the paper.
- Users should use "short and intuitive prompts" for best results.
- Do not rely solely on AI interpretation -- validate against the original text.
- This capability is especially useful for figures that use extensive symbols or abbreviations not explained in captions.
