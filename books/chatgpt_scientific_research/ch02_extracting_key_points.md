# Chapter 2: Extracting Key Points and Specific Information

## Summary

This chapter evaluates ChatGPT and new Bing's ability to extract key findings and specific information from scientific research papers. The authors tested these tools on their own publications, enabling them to validate the accuracy of the AI's responses against their firsthand knowledge of the work.

The tests showed that both ChatGPT and new Bing can effectively summarize research papers and extract key findings when given appropriate prompts. The models demonstrated proficiency in "skimming and scanning" -- quickly identifying the main points of a paper without requiring the user to read the entire text. However, the quality and depth of responses varied between sessions, and the models sometimes missed nuances or produced minor inaccuracies.

A key distinction emerged between providing the model with a paper title (for open-access papers) versus uploading the full text. New Bing, with its web search capability, could often locate and analyze open-access papers from just the title. For subscription-based papers, the full PDF had to be opened in the Microsoft Edge browser.

## Key Concepts

- **Skimming and scanning**: A reading strategy taught in academic settings where readers quickly identify main ideas (skimming) and locate specific information (scanning). ChatGPT can perform both.
- **Title-based vs. full-text analysis**: New Bing can analyze open-access papers from their titles alone by searching the web. Subscription papers require the full PDF.
- **Progressive prompting**: Starting with a general prompt and then asking follow-up questions in the same chat session to drill deeper into specific aspects.

## Practical Takeaways

- ChatGPT is useful for quickly getting the gist of a paper, particularly helpful during literature reviews when screening many papers.
- Users should start with simple, general prompts and progressively refine their queries in follow-up messages within the same chat session.
- For best results with new Bing, open the full PDF in Microsoft Edge's sidebar to give the model full-text access.
- Always validate AI-extracted information against the original paper -- minor inaccuracies are common.
- The models are better at broad summarization than extracting highly specific data points.
