# Chapter 11: Writing Research Proposals

## Summary

This chapter tests ChatGPT's capabilities for brainstorming research ideas, familiarizing users with unfamiliar topics, identifying knowledge gaps, and writing mock research proposals. The authors found the model to be a valuable brainstorming partner but noted that it cannot generate truly novel, "out-of-the-box" research ideas.

## Subsections

### 11.1 Brainstorming

**Familiarization** (Tables 31-33): The authors tested GPT-4's ability to provide quick information on unfamiliar topics:
- Controlled-release fertilizers and their mechanisms (polymer-coated, sulfur-coated, slow-release)
- Water-soluble polymers, their properties, and environmental impacts
- Links between nanoparticles and cancer (carbon nanotubes, TiO2, silver nanoparticles)

**Results**: The model provided answers with sufficient detail that were more customized and concise than typical web searches. The interactive Q&A format was more productive than keyword-searching the Internet.

**Alternative hypotheses** (Tables 34-35): The authors asked more challenging questions:
- Emerging contaminants not yet discussed in literature: The model suggested metamaterial-derived contaminants, designer microplastics, biodegradable plastic byproducts, advanced battery waste, nanocellulose-based contaminants, space exploration contaminants, and synthetic biology-derived contaminants.
- Less-discussed origins of SARS-CoV-2 and transmission routes: The model provided speculative but scientifically grounded responses.

**Limitation**: The information provided was mostly known to specialists -- the model aggregates existing knowledge rather than generating truly innovative ideas. Being knowledgeable and analytical does not equal human-level innovative thinking.

### 11.2 Writing a Mock Proposal

The authors used a multi-step workflow:
1. **Familiarize** with a topic (respiratory allergens) using ChatGPT (Table 36)
2. **Identify knowledge gaps** by asking the model to suggest under-investigated areas (Table 37) -- 11 areas identified including microclimatic variation, urbanization and allergen spread, interactions with pollutants, climate change impacts
3. **Elaborate** on specific knowledge gaps with methodologies and potential findings (Table 38)
4. **Write a mock proposal** on "Interactions between Pollutants" (Table 39) -- the model produced a complete proposal with: Introduction, Literature Review (with fictional but plausible references), Research Objectives, Methodological Feasibility, and Anticipated Outcomes.

**Important caveat**: The references in the mock proposal are fictional (explicitly noted by the model). The proposal is meant as an illustrative framework, not a submission-ready document.

### 11.3 Compliance Requirements

**NSF** (December 2023 notice):
- Encourages proposers to indicate extent of AI use in developing proposals
- Prohibits reviewers from uploading content to non-approved AI tools
- Concerned about lack of clear sources and accuracy in AI-generated content

**NIH**:
- Prohibits AI use in peer review of grant applications and contracts
- Does not prohibit AI use in writing grant applications
- Views grant applications as "original ideas" from the institution
- AI tools that introduce plagiarized text or fabricated information may trigger non-compliance actions

**Recommendation**: Always consult current guidelines of funding agencies and institutions before using AI in proposal writing.

## Practical Takeaways

- ChatGPT is excellent for initial familiarization with unfamiliar research domains.
- Use the model for brainstorming knowledge gaps and potential research directions.
- The multi-step workflow (familiarize, identify gaps, elaborate, draft proposal) is effective.
- Do not expect truly novel research ideas -- the model aggregates existing knowledge.
- Always verify any references provided in mock proposals -- they may be fictional.
- Check funding agency policies on AI disclosure before using these tools for real proposals.
- Use AI for brainstorming and illustrative purposes only, not for generating the final proposal text.
