# Chapter 6: Responding to Reviewer's Comments

## Summary

This chapter tests the AI's ability to help authors respond to peer reviewer comments -- one of the most challenging and stressful tasks in scientific publishing. The authors selected their own publications that underwent rigorous peer review and used the most critical, content-specific reviewer comments for testing. The results were among the most impressive in the entire study.

## Subsections

### 6.1 Rebuttal to Criticism
The authors tested new Bing's ability to prepare rebuttals against critical reviewer comments on two papers: a review paper on bat-borne diseases (He et al. 2021a) and a research paper on microplastics in breastmilk storage bags (Liu et al. 2023).

**Key findings:**
- The model organized arguments in a "progressive" manner, building evidence systematically to formulate strong, neutral-sounding rebuttals.
- In one case (Table 16), the model provided a detailed rebuttal about SARS-CoV-2 origins with genomic, phylogenetic, and serological evidence, though it contained some factual errors (e.g., outdated information about the closest known relative of SARS-CoV-2).
- In another case (Table 17), the model engaged in meticulous scientific debate, presenting multiple lines of evidence and respectfully disagreeing with the reviewer's criticism.
- One notable failure: the model evaded the actual point of criticism in one example, taking a wrong strategy to refute by discussing a tangentially related topic.

### 6.2 Answering Questions
The model was tested on answering specific technical questions from reviewers about a revised manuscript (Liu et al. 2023).

**Key findings:**
- The model correctly identified relevant information in the revised manuscript and supplementary data.
- It numbered the reviewer's questions separately and provided point-by-point responses with clear formatting.
- The model used a polite, appreciative tone and showed agreement with the reviewer upfront before addressing concerns.
- Minor inaccuracies were found between the model's quoted text and the original paper (e.g., "Fig. 4a" vs "Fig. 4b"), but these were actually improvements over errors in the original paper.

### 6.3 Responding to Difficult Requests
The model handled a scenario where the reviewer requested repeating experiments with advanced facilities the authors did not have access to (Table 20).

**Key findings:**
- The model admitted limitations upfront, aligning with the reviewer's comments.
- It extracted relevant facts from the paper to organize a coherent defense.
- It independently generated appropriate excuses (lack of equipment, technical challenges) without any hints from the prompt.
- The model cited non-existent references -- a recurring issue with bibliographic information.

### 6.4 A Reminder for Journal Editors and Reviewers
- Editors should be aware of AI-generated responses flooding the peer review process.
- AI responses are characteristically broad, polished, and constructive.
- Currently there is no feasible way to publicly scrutinize authors' responses after manuscript acceptance.

## Practical Takeaways

- ChatGPT can serve as a valuable drafting tool for reviewer responses, but all content must be verified.
- Use the model's output as a starting point -- refine and personalize the responses.
- The model's polite, professional tone is often better than what stressed authors might produce.
- Always fact-check references cited in the model's rebuttals -- bibliographic errors are common.
- Subject to journal policies, these tools can save significant time in preparing peer review responses.
