**Publisher:** Springer Nature, 2024

## Chapter Index

| # | Chapter | One-Line Summary |
|---|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Introduction | Overview of ChatGPT, its underlying technology, and the scope of its applications in scientific research. |
| 2 | Extracting Key Points and Specific Information | Using ChatGPT/new Bing to summarize research papers, extract key findings, and retrieve specific data from publications. |
| 3 | Interpreting Figures and Correlating to Specific Conclusions | Testing the multimodal capability of ChatGPT-enabled new Bing to analyze and interpret scientific figures. |
| 4 | Evaluation of Research Papers | Using AI to critically evaluate research papers by identifying strengths, weaknesses, and highlights. |
| 5 | Spotting Errors | Leveraging ChatGPT to detect scientific errors, mathematical mistakes, and misquotations in published literature. |
| 6 | Responding to Reviewer's Comments | Employing AI to draft rebuttals, answer reviewer questions, and handle difficult peer review requests. |
| 7 | Advanced Language Editing | Using ChatGPT for grammar correction, text rewriting, translation, and overcoming language barriers in scientific writing. |
| 8 | Crafting Article Titles | Generating creative, informative, and impactful titles for research manuscripts using AI. |
| 9 | Design of Experimental Studies | Asking ChatGPT to design experiments, refine methods, and provide reference studies for reality-checking. |
| 10 | Design of Public Surveys | Creating survey questionnaires from scratch, refining scope, and designing survey-based risk assessment studies with AI. |
| 11 | Writing Research Proposals | Using ChatGPT for brainstorming research ideas, exploring knowledge gaps, and drafting mock research proposals. |
| 12 | Science Communication and Public Engagement | Adapting research papers into popular articles, social media posts, and AI-generated visuals for public outreach. |
| 13 | Pitfalls | Key limitations including hallucination, randomness, failure to analyze supplementary materials, and bibliographic errors. |
| 14 | What Scientists and Developers Could Do | Recommendations for researchers (effective prompts, testing models, data security) and developers (needed functionalities). |
| 15 | Conclusion | Summary of findings: LLMs provide meaningful research assistance but require rigorous human validation. |
