# Chapter 1: Introduction to Generative AI in Education

## Summary

This opening chapter establishes the foundational context for the entire book by introducing Generative AI (GenAI) and its significance for higher education. The authors trace the evolution from early AI concepts to the current explosion of GenAI tools, with ChatGPT as the catalyst that brought AI into mainstream educational discourse. Released on November 30, 2022, ChatGPT reached 100 million users within two months, making it the fastest-growing consumer application in history.

The chapter distinguishes between different types of AI: Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI), which performs specific tasks; Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), which would match human-level intelligence across domains; and Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI), which would surpass human capabilities. Current GenAI tools are ANI systems that excel at specific generative tasks.

The authors argue that GenAI represents a paradigm shift comparable to the introduction of calculators, the internet, and smartphones in education. Rather than banning these tools, institutions must adapt their teaching, assessment, and curriculum to account for a world where students have access to powerful AI assistants.

### Key Concepts Introduced

- **Generative AI defined**: AI systems that can create new content (text, images, code, audio, video) based on patterns learned from training data
- **Discriminative vs. Generative models**: Discriminative models classify existing data; generative models create new data
- **Large Language Models (LLMs)**: The transformer-based models underlying tools like ChatGPT, which predict the next token in a sequence
- **Diffusion models**: Used for image generation (e.g., DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney), working by learning to remove noise from images
- **The ChatGPT Effect**: The sudden, widespread awareness of AI capabilities that forced educational institutions to respond rapidly

### The GenAI Landscape (as of 2023-2024)

The chapter provides an overview of GenAI tools across categories:
- **Text generation**: ChatGPT, Google Bard, Claude, Bing Chat
- **Image generation**: DALL-E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion
- **Code generation**: GitHub Copilot, Code Llama
- **Audio/music**: Amper Music, Jukebox, Musico
- **Video**: Synthesia, RunwayML
- **Multimodal**: GPT-4 (text + images)

### The Impact on Higher Education

The authors identify several immediate impacts:
1. Students using ChatGPT for assignments, raising academic integrity concerns
2. Faculty uncertainty about how to respond -- ban, ignore, or integrate
3. Institutions scrambling to develop policies
4. Assessment methods being questioned when AI can produce passable essays
5. The need for new literacies around AI use

### The Book's Approach

The authors advocate for a balanced, proactive approach:
- Neither uncritical embrace nor blanket prohibition
- Focus on developing frameworks that can adapt as technology evolves
- Emphasis on practical strategies faculty can implement
- Recognition that different disciplines will need different approaches

## Practical Takeaways for University Faculty

1. **GenAI is here to stay** -- avoidance is not a viable long-term strategy
2. **Start experimenting** -- try ChatGPT and other tools yourself to understand their capabilities and limitations
3. **Understand the technology basics** -- you do not need to be a computer scientist, but understanding that LLMs predict tokens (not "think") helps calibrate expectations
4. **Recognise limitations** -- hallucinations, bias, outdated training data, inability to cite reliably
5. **Engage with institutional policy discussions** early rather than waiting for mandates
6. **Consider your discipline's specific needs** -- the impact of GenAI varies significantly across fields
