# Foundational Research Gaps and Future Directions for Digital Twins

**National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2024)**

Publication: National Academies Press, Washington, DC
DOI: https://doi.org/10.17226/26894

## Report Overview

This consensus study report from the National Academies identifies foundational mathematical, statistical, and computational research gaps that must be addressed to realize the full potential of digital twins across engineering, biomedical, and atmospheric/climate/sustainability sciences. The committee proposes a formal definition of a digital twin and maps out the research landscape across four key elements: the virtual representation, the physical counterpart, and the two directions of feedback flow between them.

## Definition of a Digital Twin (from the report)

> A digital twin is a set of virtual information constructs that mimics the structure, context, and behavior of a natural, engineered, or social system (or system-of-systems); is dynamically updated with data from its physical twin; has a predictive capability; and informs decisions that realize value. The bidirectional interaction between the virtual and the physical is central to the digital twin.

## Key Themes Across All Chapters

1. **Verification, Validation, and Uncertainty Quantification (VVUQ)** is the single most important cross-cutting need -- it must be embedded in every element of the digital twin lifecycle.
2. **Bidirectional feedback** between virtual and physical is what distinguishes a digital twin from a simulation.
3. **Fit-for-purpose modeling** -- no one-size-fits-all; fidelity and resolution must match the decision task.
4. **Hybrid modeling** combining mechanistic and data-driven (ML) approaches is a productive but immature path forward.
5. **Interdisciplinary workforce** development is essential -- current academic structures do not adequately train digital twin researchers.
6. **Data challenges** span acquisition, quality, integration, interoperability, privacy, and governance.
7. **Scalability** of both computational infrastructure and organizational practices remains a barrier.
8. **Ethics, privacy, and security** are especially acute for personalized digital twins (biomedical, human).

## All Formal Recommendations

1. Federal agencies should launch new crosscutting programs to advance mathematical, statistical, and computational foundations for digital twins.
2. Federal agencies should ensure that VVUQ is an integral part of new digital twin programs.
3. Federal agencies should create mechanisms to provide digital twin researchers with computational resources.
4. Federal agencies should assess needs to maintain and sustain data, software, sensors, and virtual models for digital twins.
5. Agencies should provide cross-disciplinary workshops and venues; include international collaborators.
6. Federal agencies should identify targeted areas for industry collaboration.
7. Federal agencies should seed forums for collaborative data and model exchange across disciplines and domains.
8. Federal agencies should organize workshops to incentivize creation of interdisciplinary degrees.
