# Chapter 23: Outlook

**Authors:** Markus Reichstein, Gustau Camps-Valls, Devis Tuia, and Xiao Xiang Zhu

## Summary

This concluding chapter synthesizes the book's themes and charts the future directions for deep learning in Earth sciences. The authors identify four interconnected pillars for future progress: (1) integration of DL with observations through experimental design, (2) hybrid modeling combining DL with system/process-based models, (3) causal modeling to move beyond correlation, and (4) causal testing through real-world experiments. The chapter emphasizes that the field must move from "black-box" classification toward knowledge generation, interpretability, and causality -- and that collaboration between domain scientists and computer scientists is essential.

## Key Concepts and Methods

### Current State of the Field
- More than half of all "Earth Science + deep learning" publications on Google Scholar appeared since 2019
- DL has evolved from black-box classification toward scientific knowledge generation
- Two major strands identified:
  1. **Remote sensing** (Part I): Extracting spatial, temporal, and geometric features from multi-source observations (Chapters 2-11)
  2. **System modeling** (Parts II-III): Emulating, parameterizing, and correcting physical models (Chapters 12-22)

### Four Pillars for the Future (Figure 23.1)

**Pillar A: Experimental Design**
- Linking DL with observations and system modeling
- Optimal experimental design: Where should sensors be placed for maximum information?
- Deep generative models can play a role in designing informative experiments
- Bayesian nonparametric models for spatio-temporal stochastic process estimation

**Pillar B: Hybrid Modeling**
- Combining DL with system/process-based models in differentiable programming frameworks (PyTorch, JuliaFlux)
- DL can accelerate calibration, replace expensive parameterizations, and correct model biases
- Key challenge: identifiability -- when DNN weights, physical parameters, and latent states are all being estimated simultaneously
- Regularization techniques needed to constrain the solution space

**Pillar C: Causal Modeling**
- DNNs find correlations, not causes -- a critical limitation for scientific understanding
- Two approaches:
  1. Use causal theory to explain DNN functioning (e.g., feature relevance via causal graphs)
  2. Use DNNs for causal hypothesis generation or causal inference
- Particularly important for climate change attribution
- System models provide "ground truth" causal structures for validation

**Pillar D: Causal Testing**
- Real-world experiments to test hypotheses generated by DL/causal models
- Feasible only in less complex systems or for selected variables
- Cannot build full analog of the Earth system -- pragmatic experimental approaches needed

### Key Cross-cutting Challenges

**Explainability (XAI):**
- Decomposing model responses into interpretable building blocks (climatic, demographic, perceptive drivers)
- Rising trend: Layer-wise Relevance Propagation, SHAP, attention mechanisms
- Essential for gaining scientific trust and making genuine discoveries

**Causal Inference:**
- Learning causal feature representations instead of merely discriminative ones
- Link to system modeling provides ground-truth causal relations for validation
- Ultimate goal: machine reasoning about anthropogenic climate change

**Collaboration:**
- Domain knowledge is "elusive and difficult to encode"
- Interaction between computer scientists and Earth scientists is "still complicated"
- Education in synergistic concepts is "a hard task to achieve"
- The way forward: "collaborate, collaborate, collaborate!"

## Practical Takeaways for Scientists

1. **The field is moving from classification to knowledge discovery** -- focus future work on interpretable, physics-aware, and causal DL methods.
2. **Hybrid models in differentiable frameworks** (PyTorch, JuliaFlux) are the most promising direction for combining DL with physics.
3. **Explainability is not optional** -- XAI techniques must be integral to any DL application intended for scientific insight.
4. **Causal inference is the frontier** -- correlation-based DL will always be limited; integrating causal reasoning is essential for climate science.
5. **Interdisciplinary collaboration is mandatory** -- neither AI specialists nor domain scientists can solve these problems alone.
6. **The companion GitHub** (https://github.com/DL4ES) provides code and resources for all book chapters.
