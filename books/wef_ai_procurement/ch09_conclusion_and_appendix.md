# Conclusion and Appendix

## Summary

### Conclusion

The conclusion reiterates that AI growth has been exponential and is now achieving goals that seemed distant just a few years ago. Organizations worldwide are increasingly adopting AI/ML to support business growth, improve efficiency, and gain a competitive edge. AI solutions are deeply embedded into enterprise applications, making recommendations, predictions, and influencing critical decision-making.

The procurement team needs to maintain a high degree of control to ensure that the AI tool delivers ethical, responsible, and reliable solutions, instilling confidence. The toolkit offers a **holistic procurement process** that goes beyond technical evaluation of the solution provider's capabilities and can be tailored to the needs of the organization and industry.

The toolkit enables a clear understanding of:
- The **business strategies** driving the need to procure an AI solution
- The **commercial implications** of the decision
- The **quantity, quality, and security** of data involved
- The **governance** necessary for accountability and risk management
- The **impact** on the organization's ethics and sustainability policies

It explores the role of various internal stakeholders -- from procurement to legal to IT to product -- in addressing the five key considerations.

Overall, the toolkit provides **comprehensive, non-prescriptive, and industry-agnostic guidelines** that will establish a procurement process to facilitate the selection of a suitable, ethical AI solution.

### Appendix: Preliminary Understanding and Assessment (Table 9)

The appendix provides a condensed set of preliminary questions for organizations exploring AI offerings for the first time. These are meant for initial screening of all potential suppliers before the more detailed assessment. Commercial strategy questions are typically excluded at this stage.

#### 1. Business Strategy (preliminary questions)
- What kind of **assurance or warranty** does the supplier offer regarding business outcomes?
- Is the proposed AI/ML solution an **optimal approach** to meet requirements? Can the supplier provide case studies?
- Does the solution **measure** business results and user satisfaction? How are insights used for product modifications?
- Does the supplier provide **KPIs for monitoring performance drifts** that may prompt retraining?

#### 2. Data Strategy (preliminary questions)
- What **data quality assurance** processes and frameworks does the supplier follow?
- For external data ingestion, how does the supplier ensure complete, relevant, unbiased, and timely data? Who is responsible for third-party data quality?

#### 3. Ethics and Sustainability (preliminary questions)
- What methods are used to train the AI model? Do the **training methods** uphold principles of ethics (fairness, interpretability, privacy, security)?
- Can the supplier identify possible **sources of bias**? What checks prevent biases from creeping in?
- Does the solution address new and emerging ethical risks such as **misinformation, over-reliance, and loss of skills**?

#### 4. Governance, Risk and Compliance (preliminary questions)
- Does the supplier comply with **data-related regulations** (GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA, COPPA, etc.)?
- Does the supplier follow any **AI governance standards** and best practices set by international standards organizations (ISO, IEEE)?

## Practical Takeaways for University Leaders

- **Use the preliminary assessment questionnaire (Table 9) as your starting point** -- It provides an excellent first-pass screening tool that can be included in initial vendor RFI/RFP documents
- **The five-pillar framework maps naturally to university governance structures:**
  - Business strategy --> Provost's office / Academic affairs (alignment with educational mission)
  - Commercial strategy --> CFO / Budget office (financial sustainability)
  - Data strategy --> CIO / Institutional Research (data infrastructure and quality)
  - Ethics and sustainability --> Faculty senate / Ethics committees / DEI offices
  - Governance, risk and compliance --> General counsel / Compliance office / Internal audit
- **Build an institutional AI procurement checklist** derived from the full Tables 3-8 in this report, customized for higher education contexts
- **The "non-prescriptive" nature of the framework is a strength for universities** -- it allows adaptation to the enormous diversity of AI use cases in higher education (research tools, student services, administrative automation, learning analytics, library systems, etc.)
