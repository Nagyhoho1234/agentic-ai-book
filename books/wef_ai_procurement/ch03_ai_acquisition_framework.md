# The AI Acquisition Framework

## Summary

This chapter introduces the overarching framework for responsible AI acquisition. The foundation is a holistic model with **ethics and sustainability at the core**, surrounded by five interconnected pillars: business strategy, commercial strategy, data strategy, ethics and sustainability, and governance/risk/compliance. The framework provides directional guidelines rather than prescriptive rules, as the degree to which each factor applies can vary significantly across AI solutions and enterprises.

The key purpose statement: **"The purpose of the developed framework is to ensure that the acquisition of AI systems by commercial enterprises is grounded in ethical principles with strong governance to ensure those principles are applied, thereby mitigating bias and driving resilience."**

## Key Procurement Guidelines

- The framework is sector-agnostic but acknowledges that sector-specific concerns may exist that it does not address
- Questions and considerations should be raised both **internally** (within the enterprise) and **externally** (with the supplier/solution provider) through the sourcing life cycle and supplier relationship management (SRM)
- Procurement can be the orchestrator, but success depends on strong partnership among all concerned functions
- The report includes a set of preliminary questions (see Appendix) distilled from the comprehensive assessment questionnaire as a first step for organizations considering AI for the first time

The visual model shows concentric rings:

- **Inner ring:** Business strategy, Commercial strategy, Data strategy
- **Outer ring:** Governance, risk and compliance; Sustainability

This arrangement emphasizes that ethics is not a separate consideration but the foundation upon which all other pillars rest.

## Practical Takeaways for University Leaders

- When evaluating AI tools for the university, ethics should not be treated as a checkbox at the end -- it should be the starting point of every evaluation
- The five-pillar model provides a ready-made structure for university AI governance committees to organize their evaluation criteria
- Universities should address considerations both internally (do we have the data infrastructure, governance, and skills?) and externally (does the vendor meet our standards?)
- Start with the preliminary assessment questionnaire in the Appendix before diving into detailed evaluation -- especially useful for institutions procuring AI for the first time
