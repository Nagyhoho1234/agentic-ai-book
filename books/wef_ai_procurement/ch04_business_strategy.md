# Business Strategy (Pillar 1)

## Summary

This pillar focuses on evaluating whether the AI solution creates a competitive advantage through enhanced business decision-making, leading to positive changes in business outcomes. The central message is that organizations must define their desired business goals first and then link them to AI capabilities -- not the reverse. A common failure mode is when organizations are impressed by AI capabilities in the market and try to force-fit the solution to their requirements.

Key strategic considerations include:
- **Solution adaptability** -- The AI solution must handle gradual and abrupt concept drifts and incorporate algorithm changes
- **Scalability** -- Capabilities to scale on demand with minimal complexity
- **Thought leadership and vision** -- The solution provider's expertise in the AI application domain and underlying core AI techniques
- **AI centre of excellence/data teams** must be involved from the start in determining scope, timeline, and organizational impact

## Key Procurement Guidelines (Table 3)

### 1.1 How will the AI solution deliver on expected business outcomes?
- Can the supplier understand your business goals and explain how their capabilities help achieve them?
- Are capabilities outlined clearly with demonstrated use cases?
- How much custom development is required?
- What level of guarantee does the provider give for process and business outcomes?
- Can the solution scale up or adjust to changed needs?

### 1.2 Is there transparency about what the AI solution can and cannot achieve?
- Does the supplier explain techniques applied, including algorithms and software libraries?
- Can the supplier articulate workings in an easy-to-understand manner?
- Does the supplier dedicate resources to train/educate your team?
- Does the solution offer explainable results and transparency in decision-making?
- Does the supplier recognize and describe limitations?

### 1.3 Can a non-AI solution deliver the same outcomes?
- Can you justify why AI/ML is the optimal approach?
- Can any other cost-effective, non-AI technology achieve the expected outcomes?

### 1.4 Is there an inherent feedback loop to adjust to changes in the external environment?
- Does the solution have an automatic feedback/retraining loop, or is there a human in the loop?
- How does the solution measure business outcomes and user satisfaction?

### 1.5 How effectively can the solution be updated to accommodate change in requirements?
- How easy is it to adapt or customize during initial and steady state?
- Does the solution update its behaviour based on newly ingested data?
- How will the supplier help in course correction if the model delivers unexpected results?
- Does the supplier provide KPIs for monitoring performance drifts?
- Does the supplier provide documentation on how to reconfigure the system?

### 1.6 Is the supplier a thought leader in this domain?
- How much does the supplier invest in R&D?
- Does the supplier publish reports offering industry best practices?
- Does the supplier organize networking events for clients?
- How does the solution differentiate from competitors?
- Is the supplier investing in upskilling its talent pool?

## Practical Takeaways for University Leaders

- **Start with the problem, not the technology** -- Define what educational, research, or administrative outcome you need before evaluating AI tools
- Always ask: **Can a non-AI solution deliver the same outcome?** Many university processes may be better served by simpler automation or improved workflows
- Demand **transparency and explainability** from vendors -- critical in higher education where decisions (admissions, grading support, research funding allocation) affect people's lives
- Evaluate whether the vendor has a **feedback and retraining mechanism** -- university data patterns change (e.g., enrollment shifts, new programs) and the AI must adapt
- Assess the vendor's **thought leadership** -- are they investing in the education sector specifically, or is education an afterthought?
- Involve your institutional research/data science team from the very beginning of the evaluation
