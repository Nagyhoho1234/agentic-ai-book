# Data Strategy (Pillar 3)

## Summary

This pillar focuses on evaluating the effectiveness of the AI solution in terms of analysing valuable data in support of enterprise objectives. Data is the fundamental input for any AI solution, making a robust data strategy the foundation for successful AI deployment.

Key themes:
- **Data preparation** is the critical first step. Organizations must prepare the input dataset, and costs vary significantly -- higher for organizations with "data swamps" rather than data lakes
- **Data relevance** is just as important as data availability. Even if data exists, it must be the right data, sufficient for the use cases, and accessible to the right teams
- **Synthetic data** may be needed if internal data is insufficient for training and acquisition costs are high
- **Future data strategy** must be planned in parallel, including scalable data platforms, centralized data governance, and adherence to legal/regulatory requirements
- Organizations should consider **monetization opportunities** from the data they collect -- negotiating better contract terms or generating additional revenue streams

## Key Procurement Guidelines (Table 5)

### 3.1 Do you have a clear and defined as-is and to-be data strategy?
- What data platform does your organization currently have? How will the new AI impact it?
- What is the enterprise data structure strategy for cloud migration? Single or multi-cloud?
- Is there an existing data culture across the organization?
- Is senior leadership involved in establishing data strategies?
- Are different parts of the organization incentivized to share and reuse data?
- Does data-based feedback need to be looped back into business decisions?
- Is there a designated team for data collection, validation, storage, governance, security, and accountability?
- Who is responsible for ethical data use in the enterprise?

### 3.2 What are the different sources of data to be considered?
- What kinds of data will the AI solution ingest?
- Can the AI solution meet objectives based on internally available data?
- Is the available internal data accurate, complete, consistent, and up to date?
- Will external sources or synthetic data be needed? At what cost?
- Can the external data provider ensure complete, relevant, unbiased, and timely data?
- Should there be contractual provisions for exchange of different categories of internal data (especially PII-related data)?
- Is additional data collection necessary in the future for optimal performance?

### 3.3 Does the supplier have satisfactory data management practices?
- Does the supplier have adequate data quality assurance processes?
- Who will have ownership and be accountable for data and derivative models?
- Does the supplier have standard data privacy/security frameworks for its industry?
- How will the supplier follow data privacy practices for sensitive data outside of GDPR?
- Where is the data for the AI model stored? What security measures prevent a data breach?
- Who has access to stored data? What is the liability in case of data breach?

## Practical Takeaways for University Leaders

- **Audit your data readiness before procurement** -- Many university systems contain fragmented, siloed data across SIS, LMS, HR, finance, and research systems. Understand the gap between your current state and what the AI solution requires
- **Establish data governance before AI deployment** -- Appoint clear ownership for data quality, privacy, and ethical use. Universities handle highly sensitive data (student records under FERPA, research data, health records)
- **Assess whether internal data is sufficient** -- University datasets may be too small, biased, or incomplete for certain AI applications. Consider whether synthetic data or external datasets are needed
- **Clarify data ownership contractually** -- Who owns the trained model? Who owns derivative insights? Can the vendor use your institution's data to train models for other clients?
- **Plan for ongoing data needs** -- AI models need fresh data to remain accurate. Ensure you have a sustainable pipeline, not just a one-time data dump
- **Consider FERPA, HIPAA, and state privacy law compliance** in addition to GDPR when evaluating vendor data practices
