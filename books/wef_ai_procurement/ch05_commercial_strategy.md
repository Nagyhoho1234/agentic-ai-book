# Commercial Strategy (Pillar 2)

## Summary

This pillar addresses how the AI solution aligns with the broader commercial strategy. The core insight is that commercial strategy should not be viewed solely through the lens of monetary returns -- it should **focus on the value delivered** for the money invested. AI introduces unique commercial considerations compared to traditional technology procurement because of longer deployment timelines, ongoing retraining costs, and inherent uncertainties.

Key commercial considerations:
- **Timeframe for TCO (Total Cost of Ownership):** Typically 3-7 years for non-AI solutions, but AI deployments should include a much longer timeframe due to the ongoing cost of retraining and managing concept drifts
- **Managing uncertainties:** New technologies carry unforeseen risks; strong governance, supplier risk assessment, performance management, and risk mitigation plans with linked financial compensations are essential
- **Outcome-based pricing models** should be considered to drive joint accountability and mitigate investment risk
- **Much of the cost of AI projects is internal** -- getting data ready to enable an AI solution is time-consuming and expensive

## Key Procurement Guidelines (Table 4)

### 2.1 What is the expected value of the solution to be delivered?
- In what ways is the supplier committed to achieving business case objectives?
- Are there dependencies/assumptions on achieving objectives?
- Are there ways to optimize value delivered during steady state?
- Will the solution require co-development/co-innovation? How will you benefit from supplier gains?

### 2.2 Do you understand all the costs involved in purchase and maintenance?
- Can the supplier provide a cost breakdown based on project milestones/measurable deliverables?
- Has the supplier provided resource-wise effort estimation for each milestone?
- Are recurring cost components clearly defined with clarity on frequency?
- What would be the cost of changes required in existing systems/infrastructure?
- Has the supplier provided hourly/daily rates of consulting/technical resources?
- What are the relevant skills/years of experience of the supplier's resources?
- Does the supplier offer different pricing options for gradual expansion?
- What discounts/rebates are offered?

### 2.3 How can organizations mitigate the investment risks of the solution?
- Can the supplier delink discovery and execution phases to restrict upfront investment during business case testing?
- Can implementation be phased (e.g., low-cost proof of concept before full-scale)?
- Can the supplier commit to outcome-based pricing? What KPIs can be linked to outcomes?
- Is the supplier financially solvent?
- Is the supplier going through or planning an acquisition/merger?
- Who will own the AI model in the event of insolvency or ownership transfer? (code escrow, data escrow, model escrow)

## Practical Takeaways for University Leaders

- **Extend TCO calculations beyond the typical 3-year budget cycle** -- AI solutions require ongoing retraining, data pipeline maintenance, and concept drift management that can last well beyond initial deployment
- **Negotiate phased implementation** -- Start with a proof of concept or pilot program (e.g., one department or one use case) before committing to institution-wide deployment
- **Push for outcome-based pricing** where possible -- tie vendor compensation to measurable outcomes (e.g., improved retention prediction accuracy, reduction in administrative processing time)
- **Assess vendor financial stability** -- universities often plan for long-term technology investments; a vendor acquisition or insolvency can disrupt critical systems
- **Plan for data preparation costs** -- the internal costs of cleaning, structuring, and governing data for AI are often larger than the software licensing costs
- **Include escrow clauses** in contracts (code, data, model escrow) to protect the institution if the vendor becomes insolvent or is acquired
