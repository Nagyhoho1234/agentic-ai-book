# AI Snake Oil -- Book Summary Index

**Authors:** Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor (Princeton University)
**Published:** 2024, Princeton University Press

## Overview

*AI Snake Oil* is a critical examination of what AI can and cannot do, written by two Princeton computer scientists. The book distinguishes between AI applications that genuinely work (e.g., language translation, image recognition) and those that are "snake oil" -- products sold with exaggerated or false claims. The authors provide a taxonomy of AI into three categories: predictive AI, generative AI, and content moderation AI, arguing that each has fundamentally different capabilities and limitations.

## Key Themes for Scientists

1. **The reproducibility crisis in AI-based science is severe.** Hundreds of papers across medicine, psychiatry, genomics, and more contain data leakage errors. Results from AI-based science should be treated with extreme caution.
2. **Benchmark performance does not equal real-world utility.** Passing a bar exam or achieving 90% accuracy on a dataset tells you almost nothing about whether an AI tool will work in practice.
3. **Predictive AI for social outcomes is fundamentally limited.** Life outcomes are largely unpredictable, and attempts to predict them create self-fulfilling prophecies and encode existing biases.
4. **Generative AI is a powerful tool but not a replacement for expertise.** It excels at augmenting knowledge work but hallucinates, reflects training biases, and lacks genuine understanding.
5. **Demand critical evaluation of any AI tool before adoption.** Ask for peer-reviewed evidence, independent validation, and transparency about training data and methods.
